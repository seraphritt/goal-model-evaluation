### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **Phase Decomposition & Sequencing**:
The mission naturally divides into two macro-phases: **Collection** and **Delivery**. Because delivery cannot occur until resources are successfully retrieved from storage, these two primary objectives (`G2` and `G3`) must be executed sequentially (`G2;G3`) under a top-level achieve goal (`G1`).
2. **Multi-Robot Parallelism**:
The description highlights that when multiple items from different storages are required, multiple robots can operate in parallel to minimize mission duration (`[1, n]` or `[2, n]` robot distributions).
3. **Exception Handling & Fault Tolerance (Fallbacks)**:
The mission specifies conditional exception rules for battery depletion and task failures:
* **10% battery threshold in collection**: Triggers a fallback from normal collection (`G2.1`) to recharging and mission reassignment (`G2.2`).
* **30% battery threshold in delivery**: Triggers a fallback from normal delivery (`G3.1`) to returning resources to a checkpoint and reassigning (`G3.2.1`).
* **Checkpoint return failure**: Triggers an alert and report to the sector manager (`G3.2.2`).
Per our definitions, these conditional paths are modeled using **FALLBACK** runtime annotations and **AND** decompositions.


4. **Goal Types**:
All major objectives are classified as **Achieve Goals** because they target specific intentional states in the world (e.g., resources collected, batteries secured, alerts sent).
