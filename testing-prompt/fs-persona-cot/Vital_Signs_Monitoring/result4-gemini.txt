## Step-by-Step Reasoning and Mission Analysis

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, decomposing the natural language description of a hospital monitoring mission requires mapping high-level operational objectives into a structured, hierarchical goal model.

### 1. Mission Scope & Timing Constraints

* **Periodic Execution:** The mission runs every two hours, requiring all occupied rooms to be visited.
* **Prioritization:** High-risk patients must be checked within the first 15 minutes of the mission.
* **Resource Scaling & Feasibility:** The initial robot fleet is assigned to rooms. If time constraints cannot be met, additional robots can be assigned. If no combination of robots can complete the mission, the site manager must be alerted.

### 2. Room Visit Lifecycle & Exception Handling

Every room visit follows a strict sequence of checks before entry:

* **Pre-Entry Checks:** The robot verifies patient availability and battery level.
* If the patient is unavailable, a **Fallback** triggers a 5-minute delay before returning.
* If battery is insufficient, a **Fallback** triggers recharging and task reassignment.


* **Patient Identification:** Rooms may have multiple beds, requiring identification via asking **OR** scanning the barcode.
* **Interaction & Care:** The robot approaches, announces the procedure, and provides instructions. Vital sign collection is conditioned on patient medical history (infectious diseases, post-surgical, diabetes).
* **Failure & Escalation:** If vital sign collection fails, a fallback triggers assessment questions. If the patient does not respond, a nurse/doctor is alerted.
* **Hygiene & Safety:** Actuators are disinfected after patient contact, and robots are sterilized after exiting infectious disease rooms.

## Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The mission execution requires fleet coordination (G2) first or concurrently with managing room visits (G3), but structurally G2 and G3 follow a sequential dependency where robot resources must be allocated and monitored before and throughout room check execution.



* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Initial robot assignment (G2.1) is performed first, followed by handling unexecutable mission alerts (G2.2) if resource constraints prevent completion.


* **G2.1 -> AT1, AT2 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Both initial robot assignment and potential additional robot scaling are required complementary tasks to fulfill fleet coordination.


* **G2.2 -> AT3 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Alerting the responsible manager is the direct actionable task derived from the unexecutable mission goal.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2, G3.3, G3.4 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Room visits must follow a strict operational sequence: pre-entry verification (G3.1), room entry and identification (G3.2), patient care and vital collection (G3.3), and visit finalization (G3.4).


* **G3.1 -> G3.1.1, G3.1.2, G3.1.3 [AND] [FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G3.1.1, G3.1.2), G3.1.3)]**
* **Justification**: Pre-entry checks verify availability and battery, utilizing nested fallback relations to handle unavailability (returning in 5 mins) and low battery (recharging and task reassignment).


* **G3.1.1 -> AT4 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Checking patient availability is directly achieved by executing AT4.


* **G3.1.2 -> AT5 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Handling availability failure is achieved by executing the delay and return task (AT5).


* **G3.1.3 -> AT6 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Battery verification and recharging/reassignment are achieved through AT6.


* **G3.2 -> AT7, G3.2.1 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must first enter the room (AT7) and then identify the correct patient (G3.2.1).


* **G3.2.1 -> AT8, AT9 [OR]**
* **Justification**: Patient identification can be achieved through alternative methods: asking verbally (AT8) OR scanning the barcode (AT9).


* **G3.3 -> G3.3.1, G3.3.2, G3.3.3 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Patient care proceeds sequentially from instruction (G3.3.1) to vital sign collection/failure handling (G3.3.2) and post-interaction hygiene/sterilization (G3.3.3).


* **G3.3.1 -> AT10 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Approaching, announcing, and instructing is fulfilled by executing AT10.


* **G3.3.2 -> G3.3.2.1, G3.3.2.2, G3.3.2.3 [AND] [FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G3.3.2.1, G3.3.2.2), G3.3.2.3)]**
* **Justification**: Vital signs are collected based on condition, falling back to assessment questions on failure, and escalating to nurse/doctor alert on non-response.


* **G3.3.2.1 -> AT11, AT12, AT13 [OR]**
* **Justification**: Condition-specific vital signs depend on the patient's category (infectious OR post-surgical OR diabetes).


* **G3.3.2.2 -> AT14 [AND]**

* **G3.3.2.3 -> AT15 [AND]**
