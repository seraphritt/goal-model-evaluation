### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **Mission Objective & Scope**: The core objective is to execute patient vital signs checks every two hours across all occupied hospital rooms using a multi-robot system. This requires structuring high-level mission coordination, room visitation, clinical assessments, and exception handling.
2. **Pre-Entry Verification**: Before entering any room, robots must verify patient availability and battery sufficiency. These checks can occur in parallel or sequence, leading to query goals (`G2.1.1`, `G2.1.2`).
3. **In-Room Patient Identification**: Rooms may have multiple beds, requiring patient identification either by asking or scanning a barcode—a classic **OR-decomposition** (`G2.2.1`).
4. **Clinical Condition-Specific Checks**: Vital signs checks vary based on patient profiles (infectious diseases, post-surgical conditions, diabetes history). These condition-based checks run concurrently or dependently based on patient attributes (`G2.2.3.1`).
5. **Fallback and Exception Handling**:
* Patient unavailability triggers a 5-minute retry (modeled via operational flow/fallback).
* Insufficient battery triggers recharging and task reassignment (`G3.1`).
* Vital sign collection failure triggers assessment questions, and non-response triggers an alert to medical staff—modeled as **FALLBACK** relationships (`G2.2.3.2`).
* Time constraint failures allow additional robot assignment, and if impossible, escalates to the responsible manager (`G3.2`).


6. **Hygiene & Post-Room Actions**: Sterilization after infectious rooms and actuator disinfection after patient contact are mandatory post-room execution requirements (`G2.3`).
7. **Depth-First Numbering**: All goals and tasks are numbered strictly in depth-first traversal order (`G1` through `G3.2`, and `AT1` through `AT15`).

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The mission requires coordinating both patient room monitoring execution (`G2`) and resource/exception management (`G3`), where room monitoring and resource contingencies are handled sequentially and continuously.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Pre-entry checks (`G2.1`) must occur before entering the room to perform in-room assessment (`G2.2`), which must complete before post-room actions (`G2.3`) are executed.


* **G2.1 -> G2.1.1, G2.1.2 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Checking patient availability and robot battery sufficiency can be performed concurrently or in sequence prior to room entry.


* **G2.2 -> G2.2.1, G2.2.2, G2.2.3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Patient identification (`G2.2.1`) must precede approach and instruction (`G2.2.2`), which must precede vital signs collection and failure handling (`G2.2.3`).


* **G2.2.1 -> AT1, AT2 [OR]**
* **Justification**: The robot identifies the patient either by asking or by scanning the patient's barcode, representing alternative operational methods.


* **G2.2.2 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must physically approach the patient first before announcing the procedure and providing instructions.


* **G2.2.3 -> G2.2.3.1, G2.2.3.2 [AND][FALLBACK(G2.2.3.1, G2.2.3.2)]**
* **Justification**: Standard vital signs collection (`G2.2.3.1`) is attempted first; if collection fails, the fallback assessment and escalation procedure (`G2.2.3.2`) is attempted as an alternative.


* **G2.2.3.1 -> AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Depending on patient medical profiles (infectious, post-surgical, diabetes), respective vital signs checks can be evaluated concurrently or in combination.


* **G2.2.3.2 -> AT8, AT9 [AND][FALLBACK(AT8, AT9)]**
* **Justification**: Assessment questions (`AT8`) are asked first upon vital sign failure; if the patient is non-responsive, an alert (`AT9`) is sent as a fallback exception handling mechanism.


* **G2.3 -> AT10, AT11, AT12 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Once the patient is checked, the room must be marked as done (`AT10`), followed sequentially by required sterilization/disinfection sanitization tasks (`AT11`, `AT12`).


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Battery/reassignment management (`G3.1`) and time-constraint/escalation management (`G3.2`) operate concurrently as multi-robot support systems.


* **G3.1 -> AT13 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Insufficient battery directly triggers the recharge and task reassignment task.


* **G3.2 -> AT14, AT15 [AND][FALLBACK(AT14, AT15)]**
* **Justification**: Assigning additional robots (`AT14`) is attempted first to meet time requirements; if no robot combination can complete the mission, manager escalation (`AT15`) is triggered as a fallback.
