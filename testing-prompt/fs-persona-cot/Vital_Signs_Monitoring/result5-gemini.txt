As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital vital signs monitoring scenario. Below is the systematic decomposition of the mission into a hierarchical goal model, complete with runtime annotations, goal types, task attributes, justifications, and logical relationships.

---

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Global Objective Identification**:
The primary mission objective (`G1`) is to periodically (every two hours) check all patients' vital signs across occupied hospital rooms while adhering to strict time constraints, patient medical histories, hygiene protocols, and resource management policies.
2. **Decomposition into Core Operational Streams**:
* **Room-Level Execution (`G2`)**: Involves verifying entry prerequisites (availability and battery), preparing the patient, checking specific vital signs based on medical history, handling collection failures/non-responses, and executing post-check hygiene/maintenance.
* **Mission-Level Management (`G3`)**: Involves high-risk patient prioritization within the first 15 minutes, robot reassignments, additional robot allocation, and notifying the site manager if completion is unfeasible.


3. **Runtime Annotations & Fallbacks**:
* **Fallback (`FALLBACK`)**: Used where conditions dictate alternative paths—such as returning in 5 minutes if a patient is unavailable, recharging and reassigning tasks if battery is low, assessing patient status via questions upon vital sign collection failure, and handling robot resource shortages.
* **Sequential (`;`)**: Used where steps must occur in a strict order (e.g., verifying prerequisites before entering, identifying and approaching the patient before announcing procedures).
* **Parallel (`#`)**: Used where tasks or sub-goals can be executed concurrently (e.g., periodic room checks running alongside mission-level resource and high-risk monitoring).

## 5. Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Periodic room checking (`G2`) and mission-level timing/resource management (`G3`) must run concurrently throughout the mission duration.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3, G2.4, G2.5 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: A room check must strictly follow the sequence of verifying prerequisites (`G2.1`), entering and preparing the patient (`G2.2`), collecting condition-specific vitals (`G2.3`), handling any collection failures (`G2.4`), and executing post-check cleaning/marking (`G2.5`).


* **G2.1 -> G2.1.1, G2.1.2 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must first query availability and battery levels (`G2.1.1`) before triggering exception handling or fallback recovery (`G2.1.2`) if prerequisites fail.


* **G2.1.1 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Checking patient availability at the door and verifying battery level can be performed simultaneously by the robot's onboard systems.


* **G2.1.2 -> AT3, AT4 [FALLBACK(AT3, AT4)]**
* **Justification**: If the patient is unavailable, the fallback is to return in 5 minutes (`AT3`); if battery is insufficient, the alternative fallback is to recharge and reassign tasks (`AT4`).


* **G2.2 -> AT5, AT6, AT7, AT8 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Inside the room, the robot must sequentially identify the patient (`AT5`), approach them (`AT6`), announce the procedure (`AT7`), and provide instructions (`AT8`).


* **G2.3 -> G2.3.1, G2.3.2, G2.3.3 [OR]**
* **Justification**: Depending on the patient's medical history, the robot executes infectious, post-surgical, or diabetic vital checks as alternative clinical branches.


* **G2.4 -> AT12, AT13 [FALLBACK(AT12, AT13)]**
* **Justification**: If vital collection fails, the robot attempts assessment questions (`AT12`); in case of non-response, it falls back to alerting medical staff (`AT13`).


* **G2.5 -> AT14, AT15, AT16 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: After checking the patient, the robot marks them as done (`AT14`) and then performs required sterilization (`AT15`) or actuator disinfection (`AT16`).


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: High-risk patient prioritization (`G3.1`) and robot capacity/manager alerting (`G3.2`) operate concurrently as overarching mission management threads.
