### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **Mission Objective & Scope (`G1`)**: The overarching mission requires checking all patient vital signs every two hours and ensuring hospital-wide coverage by a fleet of robots. This is modeled as an **Achieve Goal** because the objective is to reach a target system state where all occupied rooms and patients are successfully serviced within the required time window.
2. **Fleet & Resource Management (`G2`)**: The mission introduces resource constraints (battery levels, task reassignment, robot scaling, and manager notifications). Because fleet-level resource handling operates concurrently with room execution, `G2` and `G3` are structured in parallel (`#`).
3. **Pre-Entry & Fallback Handling (`G3.2.1`)**: Robots must verify patient availability and battery before entering. If a patient is unavailable, the mission specifies an alternative behavior (come back in 5 minutes). In goal modeling terminology, this explicit exception/alternative condition is captured using the **FALLBACK** runtime operator (`FALLBACK(G3.2.1.1, G3.2.1.2)`).
4. **Patient Identification (`G3.2.2`)**: The description states the robot must identify the patient "either by asking or by scanning the patient's barcode." This represents a true alternative choice, modeled as an **OR decomposition**.
5. **Conditional Vital Signs & Hygiene (`G3.2.3` & `G3.2.4`)**: Vital sign checks depend on patient conditions (infectious, post-surgical, diabetes). Furthermore, mandatory post-task sterilizations and actuator disinfection protocols must execute after patient contact or leaving infectious rooms, ensuring biosafety compliance.
