## Step-by-Step Reasoning and Mission Analysis

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), analyzing natural language mission descriptions requires translating stakeholder requirements into a rigorous hierarchical goal model. Here is the step-by-step breakdown of the analysis and decomposition process for this hospital delivery and retrieval mission:

1. **High-Level Mission Objectives Identification**:
* The text specifies two primary system functions: **delivering food from the kitchen to inpatient rooms** and **retrieving dirty dishes from inpatient rooms**.
* These form our root system objective, **G1**, which is decomposed into two sequential sub-missions: food delivery (`G2`) and dish retrieval (`G9`).


2. **Analyzing Food Delivery (`G2`)**:
* **Uncertainty & Information Gathering**: The description highlights that whether a patient/companion/nurse can retrieve food from the tray depends on patient records and companion presence, which is subject to uncertainty. This requires a **Query Goal (`G3`)** to obtain necessary runtime information before delivery execution.
* **Delivery Execution & Monitoring (`G4`)**: Once capability is known, the system must deliver meals and track/verify retrieval. Delivery can occur via two alternative mechanisms:
* *Table Delivery*: Robot places food directly onto the table using a special manipulation skill (`G6`).
* *Tray Fetching*: Food is fetched from the robot's tray requiring cooperation with the inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot (`G7`).
* Because these represent alternative modalities governed by conditions (patient ability/availability), they form an **OR decomposition** handled via a **FALLBACK** runtime annotation.


* **Tracking & Verification**: The robot must track meal retrieval time/location and alert if a wrong meal is retrieved (`G8`), running concurrently with delivery.


3. **Analyzing Dish Retrieval (`G9`)**:
* Retrieving dirty dishes requires physical access to the room and coordination.
* First, the room door must be opened via cooperation with a human or another robot (`G10`).
* Second, dirty dishes are retrieved either unassisted or via robot-robot/robot-human cooperation (`G11`).
* These steps are executed sequentially (`G10;G11`).


4. **Task and Resource Allocation**:
* **Locations**: All locations mentioned (`kitchen`, `inpatient room`) are explicitly mapped to tasks.
* **Robot Counts**: Single-robot operations (e.g., table manipulation, record querying, tracking) are assigned `1`. Cooperative tasks involving human-robot or multi-robot teams (e.g., tray fetching, door opening, dish pickup) are assigned the range `[1, 2]` to reflect single or dual agent cooperation.
