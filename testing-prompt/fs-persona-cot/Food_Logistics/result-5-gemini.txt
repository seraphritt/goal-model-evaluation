As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital food delivery and dish collection system. Below is the step-by-step reasoning, followed by the comprehensive goal model, goal tables, task tables, summary table, and logical relationships.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **System Objective & Top-Level Decomposition**:
* The primary mission is twofold: delivering food from the kitchen to inpatient rooms and collecting dirty dishes. These represent two overarching operational responsibilities.
* Because both workflows are required to fulfill the mission's scope, **G1 (Manage Hospital Food Delivery and Dish Collection Operations)** is decomposed into **G2 (Execute Food Delivery to Inpatient Rooms)** and **G3 (Retrieve Dirty Dishes from Inpatient Rooms)** via an **AND** decomposition. Their execution can occur concurrently or independently in response to requests, annotated with parallel execution (`G2#G3`).


2. **Food Delivery Workflow (G2)**:
* **Prerequisite Query (G2.1)**: Before delivering food, the system must handle uncertainty regarding whether the inpatient, companion, or nurse can retrieve food from the tray. This information is obtained from inpatient records and room presence. Thus, **G2.1** is classified as a **Query Goal**.
* **Delivery Execution (G2.2)**: Once status is known, meal delivery is executed. The description specifies two alternative delivery methods: tray fetching (requiring cooperation) or direct room table delivery (requiring a special manipulation robot skill).
* **Fallback & Alternative Handling**: Since tray fetching is attempted based on patient capability and availability (with fallback to robot table placement if tray retrieval is unfeasible or fails), this is modeled using the **FALLBACK** runtime annotation: `FALLBACK(G2.2.1, G2.2.2)`. Per design rules, fallback sub-goals are connected via an **AND** decomposition.
* **Tray Fetching (G2.2.1)**: Involves indicating the correct meal, alerting if a wrong meal is taken, and tracking retrieval time and location. This breaks down sequentially into instruction/verification (**G2.2.1.1**, Perform Goal) and logging (**G2.2.1.2**, Achieve Goal).


3. **Dish Collection Workflow (G3)**:
* **Room Access & Door Opening (G3.1)**: Involves receiving a pickup call and opening the door via human or robot cooperation. This is an **OR** decomposition between alternative door-opening cooperation modes.
* **Dish Retrieval (G3.2)**: Can occur unassisted, with two robots, or with robot-human cooperation. This is an **OR** decomposition representing alternative execution modes, modeled as a **Perform Goal**.


4. **Task Allocation & Locations**:
* All locations (`kitchen`, `inpatient room`) are strictly derived from the mission description.
* Robot counts (`1` or range `[1, 2]`) reflect whether a task requires a single robot or multi-robot/human cooperation.

* **Target Condition:** Meals successfully delivered from the kitchen to the inpatient room.
* **Relation:** AND
* **Justification:** Delivering food requires a sequential flow: first querying patient capability/room status, followed by executing the meal delivery operation.

* **Name:** G2.1 - Query Inpatient Retrieval Capability and Room Status

#### G2.2: Execute Meal Delivery Operation

* **Name:** G2.2 - Execute Meal Delivery Operation

* **Text:** Query hospital database/records and sensors to determine inpatient retrieval ability and companion/nurse presence.

* **Number of Robots:** `1`

* **AT2: Indicate and Verify Meal Identification**

* **Relation:** AND
* **Location:** inpatient room
* **Number of Robots:** `[1, 2]`
* **Justification:** May involve a single delivery robot or cooperation with another robot/human assisting the patient.

* **Location:** inpatient room

* **Relation:** AND
* **Location:** inpatient room
* **Number of Robots:** `1`

* **AT6: Open Room Door via Human or Robot Cooperation**
* **Text:** Coordinate door opening mechanism with a human occupant or another robot.

* **Location:** inpatient room
* **Number of Robots:** `[1, 2]`
* **Justification:** Requires either human physical action/signaling or multi-robot cooperation (`[1, 2]`) to open the room door.


* **AT7: Retrieve Dirty Dishes Unassisted or Cooperatively**
* **Text:** Collect and load dirty dishes from the room table into the robot tray unassisted, with two robots, or with robot-human cooperation.

* **Location:** inpatient room

* **Justification:** Can be executed by 1 robot unassisted, or collaboratively by 2 robots or human-robot team (`[1, 2]`).

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND] [(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: The mission encompasses both food delivery and dish collection as complementary and core functional requirements that can be executed in parallel or independently upon request.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The system must first query the inpatient record and room status (G2.1) to determine retrieval capability before proceeding to execute the meal delivery operation (G2.2).


* **G2.1 -> AT1 [AND]**
* **Justification**: The query goal for inpatient retrieval capability is directly operationalized by performing data/record queries.


* **G2.2 -> G2.2.1, G2.2.2 [AND] [FALLBACK(G2.2.1, G2.2.2)]**
* **Justification**: Tray fetching (G2.2.1) is attempted first based on patient capability; if conditions fail or tray fetching is not possible, robot table delivery (G2.2.2) is executed as an alternative fallback behavior. Per rule, fallback subgoals are connected by an AND-decomposition.


* **G2.2.1 -> G2.2.1.1, G2.2.1.2 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must first indicate the meal and verify/alert incorrect pickups (G2.2.1.1) before successfully completing the tracking of the retrieval event (G2.2.1.2).


* **G2.2.1.1 -> AT2 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Direct meal retrieval and verification is executed through task AT2 (indicating and verifying meal identification).


* **G2.2.1.2 -> AT3 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Tracking meal retrieval events is operationalized by logging timestamps and locations (AT3).


* **G2.2.2 -> AT4 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Delivering the meal to the room table via robot manipulation is achieved through task AT4 (placing the meal on the table).


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must successfully gain access to the room (G3.1) before it can execute the dish collection operation inside the room (G3.2).


* **G3.1 -> AT5, AT6 [OR]**
* **Justification**: Managing room access involves alternative pathways: receiving pickup calls/checking door signals (AT5) and opening the door via human or robot cooperation (AT6).


* **G3.2 -> AT7 [OR]**
* **Justification**: Executing the dish collection operation is achieved through task AT7 across alternative cooperative or unassisted modes.
