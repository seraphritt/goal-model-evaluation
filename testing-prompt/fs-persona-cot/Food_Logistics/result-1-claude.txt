# Goal Model for the Hospital Meal-Delivery & Dish-Retrieval Multi-Robot System

## Step-by-Step Reasoning (Methodology)

**1. Identifying the top-level responsibilities.**
The description states two explicit, mandatory system responsibilities: *"The system should deliver food from the kitchen to an inpatient room"* and *"Dirty dishes should be retrieved from the room."* These are complementary (both are required of the system, not alternatives), so I modeled a root **Achieve Goal (G1)** with an **AND** decomposition into **Deliver Food Order (G2)** and **Retrieve Dirty Dishes (G16)**. No explicit order or dependency is stated between these two processes (a delivery robot and a pickup robot could in principle work at the same time, in different rooms), so I used the **parallel (#)** runtime annotation rather than forcing a sequence I cannot justify textually.

**2. Decomposing food delivery.**
The text gives a causal chain: a delivery happens *"in response to kitchen delivery requests"* (so a request must be received first), food must get *"from the kitchen to an inpatient room"* (so it must be transported), and only then can it actually be delivered to the patient by one of two explicit means: *"delivered into a room table... or... fetched from the robot's tray"* (explicit **OR**, no priority stated between the two → **parallel (#)**). Because the request→transport→delivery order is causally/textually implied, I used **sequential (;)** for G2's children.

**3. Handling the uncertainty around tray-fetch.**
This is the richest part of the description. Four explicitly listed actors can retrieve food from the tray (inpatient, companion, nurse, another robot) — an enumeration, not a stated priority order, so this is an **OR**, not a FALLBACK (the instructions are explicit: without a stated order, FALLBACK must not be used, even though a "try inpatient, then companion, then nurse" real-world policy is plausible — it is never stated in the text). I collapsed the three *human* actors into one branch (**G10 – Retrieve Meal with Human Assistance**) because, from the robot's perspective, the operational task performed is identical in each case (indicate the meal, then let a human take it) — splitting them further would duplicate the same underlying intention, which the instructions explicitly ask to avoid. The robot's own uncertainty-handling requirement — *"information... can be obtained based on the inpatient record... and the presence of a companion... This information is subject to uncertainty"* — is modeled as a dedicated **Query Goal (G8)**, explicitly split into two tasks because the text names two distinct information sources (the patient's record, and companion presence in the room).

**4. Multi-meal bookkeeping.**
Three distinct, separately-stated robot obligations appear here and I kept them as three distinct nodes to avoid conflating different intentions:
- *"indicate which meal should be retrieved"* → **Perform Goal G11** (an action, not a state).
- *"track when and where each meal was retrieved"* → **Query Goal G14** (obtaining/recording data — "when" and "where" are two explicit properties, so I split it into two AND-related tasks).
- *"alert if a wrong meal is retrieved"* → **Perform Goal G15** (a reactive action).

These, together with capability-determination (G8) and the actual retrieval (G9), are all explicitly required behaviors of the tray-fetch process, so they are joined with **AND** under G7. I treated their order as sequential (determine → retrieve → track → alert) because each step logically depends on data/outcomes of the previous one, even though the text doesn't use "first/then" wording explicitly for this chain — this is the one place I inferred sequence from clear causal necessity rather than explicit phrasing, and I flag that inference here for transparency.

**5. Dish retrieval.**
The text explicitly parallels the delivery structure: *"As with delivery, the dish retrieve can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots or with the cooperation of robots and a human"* — three enumerated, unordered alternatives → **OR / parallel (#)**. Door-opening (*"can involve cooperation with another robot or a human"*) is textually anchored to the dish-pickup paragraph (it sits between the collection-method sentence and the sentence about someone calling the robot and signaling about the door), so I nested it only under dish retrieval rather than inventing an unstated door-opening requirement for the table-delivery branch. The call-and-signal sentence (*"someone... can call a robot... that person can signal if they can open the door"*) is modeled as a **Query Goal (G17)**, again split into two tasks (the call itself, and the door-opening-availability signal), by analogy with G8 — both are "obtain decision-relevant information under uncertainty" goals.

**6. No FALLBACK anywhere.**
I searched the text specifically for "if X fails/cannot, then Y" constructions. None exist — every multi-option situation in the text (table vs. tray; inpatient/companion/nurse/robot; unassisted/2-robot/robot+human; robot vs. human door-opening) is phrased as a plain enumeration ("or", "can involve"), never as a stated primary behavior with a triggered alternative. Per the instructions, I therefore used **OR** decompositions with **parallel (#)** runtime everywhere an order is not stated, and reserved **sequential (;)** strictly for places where the text's own ordering of events (request → transport → delivery; request → access → collection) makes the sequence unambiguous.

**7. Locations and robot counts.**
Only two locations are ever named in the text: **Kitchen** and **Inpatient Room** (the "room table" and "tray" are objects/interfaces inside the inpatient room, not separate locations), so every task's Location is one of these two (or both, for the transport task, modeled as a collection). Robot counts default to **1** (the single delivering/collecting robot) unless the text explicitly names a second cooperating robot ("another robot," "cooperation of two robots"), in which case I set it to **2**. Human helpers (inpatient, companion, nurse, visitor) are never counted as robots, consistent with the "zero-robot goals/tasks must not appear" rule being about robot-performed work, not about excluding human-assisted tasks (a robot is still the one executing/monitoring/coordinating in every task I kept).

**8. Leaf goals with a single task.**
Several leaf goals (e.g., G4, G6, G11, G12, G13, G15, G19, G20, G22–G24) map to exactly one operational task rather than two. The mission text does not provide enough granularity to further decompose these without inventing unstated sub-steps, so I treat the goal→task edge as a (trivial) AND — the single task is the complete and sole operationalization of that goal's intention. Wherever the text *did* name two distinct properties or sources (G8, G14, G17), I split the task layer accordingly to produce genuine AND branching.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G16 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both food delivery and dish retrieval are independently mandated system responsibilities; no ordering between them is given, so they can proceed in parallel.

- **G2 -> G3, G4, G5 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: A request must be received before the robot transports the meal, which must happen before the final delivery to the patient — the text's own phrasing ("in response to...requests", "from the kitchen to...room") imposes this order.

- **G5 -> G6, G7 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Delivered into a room table... **or**... fetched from the robot's tray" are explicit, sufficient alternatives with no stated priority.

- **G7 -> G8, G9, G14, G15 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Capability-determination, retrieval, tracking, and alerting are all separately required behaviors for a well-formed tray-fetch process; each depends causally on the one before it.

- **G9 -> G10, G13 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Requires cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, **or** another robot" — enumerated alternatives, no stated order, hence OR with parallel runtime (not FALLBACK, since no "try X then Y on failure" language appears).

- **G10 -> G11, G12 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The robot must indicate the correct meal before the human can correctly take it — indication must precede handover.

- **G8 -> AT4, AT5 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Capability info explicitly comes from two sources — the inpatient record and companion presence — both are required to determine capability.

- **G14 -> AT9, AT10 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: "Track when **and** where" names two distinct, jointly-required tracked properties.

- **G16 -> G17, G18, G21 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The pickup process is triggered by a request, which is followed by gaining room access, which is followed by collecting the dishes — the call-then-arrival-then-pickup structure in the text implies this order.

- **G17 -> AT12, AT13 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: The call and the door-opening-availability signal are both explicitly mentioned as information obtained at request time.

- **G18 -> G19, G20 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Cooperation with another robot **or** a human" — explicit, unordered alternatives for opening the door.

- **G21 -> G22, G23, G24 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots **or** with the cooperation of robots and a human" — three explicit, unordered alternatives.

### Key modeling caveats (for transparency)
1. **No FALLBACK relations appear anywhere.** Every multi-option construct in the text is a plain enumeration ("or", "can involve"), never an explicit "attempt X, and if it fails do Y" — so per the strict instruction, OR + parallel was used throughout instead of inferring a priority order (e.g., inpatient-first, then companion, then nurse) that is plausible but not textually stated.
2. **G7's internal sequencing** (capability → retrieval → tracking → alerting) is the one place I inferred order from causal necessity rather than explicit "first/then" wording — flagged here so it can be revisited if a different reading of the mission is intended.
3. **"Robots" plural in "cooperation of robots and a human"** for dish collection was interpreted as one robot + one human (by analogy with the clearly singular "another robot or a human" used for the door), not two-or-more robots plus a human — this is an interpretive judgment call, not an explicit statement.
