### Step-by-Step Mission Analysis & Reasoning

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a natural language mission description into a goal model requires identifying the core system objectives, system boundaries, executing agents (robots), locations, and temporal/logical dependencies.

#### 1. System Boundary & Multi-Robot Identification

* **Identified Robot Agents**:
1. *Mobile Transport Robots*: Responsible for navigating between patient floors and the laboratory, transporting samples inside secure locked drawers, and interacting with authorization systems.
2. *Robotic Arms*: Responsible for laboratory-side operations, including picking up samples from drawers, scanning barcodes, sorting, and loading them into analysis machine entry-modules.


* **Exclusion of Non-Robot Entities**: Human actions performed exclusively by nurses (e.g., collecting samples, manual data entry into the tracking system) or human laboratory personnel (when not assisted by robots) are excluded in accordance with the zero-robot rule. Only tasks executed by robots or cyber-physical robot subsystems are modeled in the goal tree.

#### 2. Goal Decomposition Strategy

The mission is decomposed hierarchically using **AND** decompositions to group complementary operational pillars:

* **G1 (Root)** breaks down into **G2** (Transport), **G3** (Laboratory Processing), and **G4** (Secure Logging & Tracking).
* **Temporal/Runtime Annotations**:
* Transport occurs first, followed by laboratory processing, while secure tracking operates concurrently (`(G2;G3)#G4`).
* Within transport (`G2`), tasks occur sequentially: navigation (`AT1`) $\rightarrow$ sample reception (`AT2`) $\rightarrow$ transit (`AT3`).
* Within lab processing (`G3`), drawer opening (`G3.1`) precedes robotic arm manipulation (`G3.2`).

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4 [AND][(G2;G3)#G4]**
* **Justification**: Fulfilling the mission root goal requires completing sample transportation (`G2`) followed sequentially by laboratory processing (`G3`), while secure logging and tracking (`G4`) run concurrently throughout the mission lifecycle.


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2, AT3 [AND][AT1;AT2;AT3]**
* **Justification**: Transporting samples requires the mobile robot to first navigate to the patient floor (`AT1`), receive and secure the sample (`AT2`), and then transit to the laboratory (`AT3`) in strict sequential order.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND][G3.1;G3.2]**
* **Justification**: Laboratory processing requires opening the secure locked drawer (`G3.1`) upon arrival before the robotic arm can access, pick up, and process the samples (`G3.2`).


* **G3.1 -> AT4, AT5 [AND][AT4;AT5]**
* **Justification**: Opening the secure drawer requires verifying the robot's authorization and location (`AT4`) prior to actuating the physical unlock mechanism (`AT5`).


* **G3.2 -> AT6, AT7 [AND][AT6;AT7]**
* **Justification**: The robotic arm must pick up the sample and scan its barcode (`AT6`) before it can sort and load the sample into the analysis machine entry-module (`AT7`).


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2 [AND][G4.1#G4.2]**
* **Justification**: Secure logging of pick-up metadata (`G4.1`) and time tracking against protocols (`G4.2`) are complementary monitoring functions that execute in parallel.


* **G4.1 -> AT8, AT9 [AND][AT8#AT9]**
* **Justification**: Recording pick-up location (`AT8`) and recording pick-up timestamp (`AT9`) are parallel data acquisition tasks during sample collection.


* **G4.2 -> AT10, AT11 [AND][AT10;AT11]**
* **Justification**: Measuring delivery duration (`AT10`) must be completed upon lab arrival before validating that duration against established laboratory protocols (`AT11`).
