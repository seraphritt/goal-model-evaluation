### Step-by-Step Thinking and Reasoning

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, decomposing the natural language mission description into a formal goal model requires a systematic analysis of the operational objectives, agent capabilities, environmental constraints, uncertainties, and collaborative requirements.

#### 1. Mission Scope and High-Level Objectives

The mission description defines an autonomous inpatient support service within a hospital environment comprising two primary macro-objectives:

1. **Food Delivery**: Delivering meals from the kitchen to inpatient rooms upon request, handling multi-meal logistics, verifying meal-patient matching, and adapting to patient/companion availability.
2. **Dish Retrieval**: Collecting dirty dishes from inpatient rooms in response to room calls, managing room entry door opening, and executing dish pickup through unassisted or collaborative actions.

#### 2. Goal Types Identification and Justification

* **Achieve Goals**: Used when the system intends to reach a specific target state in the world through planning and action (e.g., delivering food, retrieving dishes, executing meal delivery).
* **Query Goal**: Used specifically when the system's purpose is to retrieve information without necessarily altering the physical world (e.g., checking inpatient records and room companion presence to determine tray retrieval capability).
* **Perform Goal**: Used when executing specific actions where success is defined by the execution of the action itself (e.g., transporting meals from the kitchen, opening room doors, picking up dirty dishes).

#### 3. Runtime Annotations & Execution Semantics

* **Sequential Operator (`;`)**: Applied where milestones must occur in order (e.g., querying retrieval capability before executing delivery, transporting meals before delivering them, opening doors before picking up dishes).
* **Fallback Operator (`FALLBACK(...)`)**: Applied where the mission explicitly defines a primary normal behavior and an alternative behavior triggered by conditions, exceptions, or patient inability (e.g., trying direct table manipulation delivery first, and falling back to tray fetching with cooperation if table delivery is not feasible or chosen based on patient capability).
* **Leaf Nodes (`-`)**: Assigned to leaf goal nodes and tasks where no further sub-goal decomposition occurs.

#### 4. Task Allocation and Robot Count Derivation

* Tasks represent actionable, concrete operations performed by robots at specific locations cited in the text (`kitchen` and `inpatient room`).
* Robot counts are derived from the textual requirements:
* Unassisted robot actions or specialized manipulation tasks: $[1, 1]$ robot.
* Collaborative tasks involving multiple robots or robot-human cooperation (e.g., tray fetching, door opening, multi-robot dish retrieval): $[2, 2]$ robots or $[1, 2]$ cooperative agents.
