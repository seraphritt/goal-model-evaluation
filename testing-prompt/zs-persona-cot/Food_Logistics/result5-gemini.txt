As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital robotic service. Below is the systematic decomposition, reasoning process, goal model specification, task mapping, and logical relationships.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **Mission Scope & Objectives Analysis**:
* The mission encompasses two primary high-level responsibilities: **Food Delivery** from the kitchen to inpatient rooms and **Dirty Dish Retrieval** from inpatient rooms.
* These two responsibilities represent sequential or iterative operational phases of inpatient room service, captured under a root goal (**G1**).


2. **Decomposition of Meal Delivery (`G2`)**:
* Before delivering a meal, the system must acquire necessary operational data: kitchen orders and inpatient/companion status (`G3`). These can be queried in parallel (`#`).
* The actual delivery (`G6`) has two alternative paths specified by the description: direct table placement using a special manipulation skill (`G7`) versus tray fetching with cooperation (`G8`). Because table placement is attempted first (requiring manipulation capabilities), and tray fetching serves as the alternative when table placement is not feasible or fails, this forms a **FALLBACK** relationship.
* Tray fetching (`G8`) requires indicating the correct meal (`G9`) followed by assisted retrieval (`G10`), establishing a sequential dependency (`;`).


3. **Decomposition of Dish Retrieval (`G11`)**:
* Retrieving dirty dishes requires opening the room door (`G12`) followed by collecting the dishes (`G15`), forming a sequential workflow (`;`).
* **Door Opening (`G12`)**: Prioritizes unassisted occupant signaling (`G13`), falling back (`FALLBACK`) to cooperative opening with a human or another robot (`G14`).
* **Dish Collection (`G15`)**: Features three operational modalities structured as nested fallbacks (`FALLBACK(G16, FALLBACK(G17, G18))`): unassisted pickup (`G16`), dual-robot cooperation (`G17`), and robot-human cooperation (`G18`).


4. **Task Derivation & Resource Allocation**:
* Leaf goals are grounded into actionable tasks (`AT1` through `AT10`) tied strictly to locations mentioned in the description (`kitchen`, `inpatient room`) and robot counts ranging from single robots (`1`) to multi-robot/human teams (`[1, 2]` or `2`).

* **Justification**: The top-level hospital mission requires sequential execution where meal delivery (G2) must be completed before dirty dishes can be retrieved from inpatient rooms (G3).


* **G2 -> G3, G6 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Meal delivery requires first obtaining delivery requests and patient retrieval capabilities (G3), followed by transporting and dispensing the meal (G6).


* **G3 -> G4, G5 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Kitchen orders and inpatient record/companion presence data can be queried in parallel or independently.


* **G4 -> AT1 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Querying kitchen delivery requests is operationally fulfilled by querying the kitchen order list task.


* **G5 -> AT2 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Querying inpatient capabilities is operationally fulfilled by querying hospital records and room sensor data.


* **G6 -> G7, G8 [AND][FALLBACK(G7, G8)]**
* **Justification**: The robot attempts direct table placement first (G7); if unfeasible or failing, it falls back to tray-based delivery with cooperation (G8).

* **Justification**: Direct table delivery is executed through specialized robotic arm placement.


* **G8 -> G9, G10 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Tray-based delivery requires the robot to first indicate the correct meal (G9) before cooperative retrieval takes place (G10).


* **G9 -> AT4 [AND]**

* **G10 -> AT5 [AND]**
