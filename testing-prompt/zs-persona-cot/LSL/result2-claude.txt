**2. Apply the zero-robot exclusion rule.** Several actions are performed solely by humans or by non-robot software, so they're left out of the tree entirely:
- Nurse requesting a delivery — human-only.
- Barcode identification "before collection" — attributed to the nurse/administrative step, not a robot.
- Nurse inserting data into the track system — human-only.
- "picked by... laboratory personnel" — this is one branch of an OR; since it involves zero robots, only the "robotic arm" branch survives in the model.
- "opened by authorized personnel..." — same treatment; only the "specific robots in specific locations" branch survives.

This is why several OR constructs in the prose collapse into single-child AND nodes in the model — the human alternative isn't deleted arbitrarily, it's excluded per the stated rule, and I've flagged this explicitly in the relevant justifications below rather than silently dropping it.

**3. Group remaining robot-relevant intentions.** What's left is: (a) get the locked drawer from floor to lab and unlock it, (b) have the robotic arm pick/scan/sort/load the sample, (c) log where/when the pick happened, (d) track elapsed time from collection to delivery against protocol. These become G2, G4, G5, G6 respectively, grouped under a root mission goal G1, with G3 as the intentional parent over the in-lab work (G4+G5).

**4. Determine goal types.** G1–G4 and G2 are classic achieve goals (there's a clear target world-state: drawer at the lab, sample loaded into the machine). G5 is a perform goal — "should be logged securely" is an imperative action with no further planning involved; success = the action executed. G6 is a query goal — tracking elapsed time is about obtaining a piece of information (a duration), not changing the world.

**5. Determine runtime annotations.** No conditional/failure language appears anywhere in the text ("if X fails, do Y"), so FALLBACK is never used. Where the text lists actions as an explicit sequence ("picks up, scans, sorts, and loads"), sequential (`;`) applies. Where two concerns can reasonably proceed independently of each other (transport vs. time-tracking; sample handling vs. logging the pick event), I used parallel (`#`).
