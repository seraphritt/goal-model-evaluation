## Mission Analysis and Reasoning (Step-by-Step Thinking)

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), analyzing natural language mission descriptions requires strict adherence to system boundaries, agent capabilities, and spatial constraints.

### 1. System Boundary & Agent Filtering (Zero-Robot Rule)

The mission description mentions both human agents (nurses, laboratory personnel) and robotic agents (mobile delivery robots, robotic arms). According to the core requirement:

> *"Goals and tasks that are not performed by any robot (zero robot) must not be on the goal model."*

Therefore, human-only actions—such as the nurse collecting samples, the nurse manually entering data into the tracking system, or human lab personnel picking samples—are explicitly **filtered out** of the goal model. The model focuses exclusively on robot-executed goals and tasks:

* **Mobile Delivery Robots**: Responsible for receiving, securing, transporting, and unlocking sample drawers.
* **Robotic Arm**: Responsible for picking samples, scanning barcodes, sorting, and loading samples into analysis machines in the laboratory.

### 2. Spatial Mapping (Location Constraints)

Locations are strictly derived from the mission description without fabrication:

* **Patient Floors**: Where samples originate and are loaded into mobile robot drawers.
* **Laboratory**: Where mobile robots arrive, drawers are unlocked, and the robotic arm processes samples.

### 3. Hierarchical Goal Decomposition & Runtime Semantics

* **Root Goal (`G1`)**: Manages the end-to-end automated sample transport and laboratory processing pipeline.
* **Level 1 Decomposition**: Split into transport (`G2`) and laboratory processing (`G3`). Because transport must chronologically precede lab processing, a sequential runtime annotation `G2;G3` is applied under an AND decomposition.
* **Level 2 Decomposition (`G2` - Transport)**: Broken down into receiving/securing (`G2.1`), transit navigation (`G2.2`), and drawer unlocking (`G2.3`) performed sequentially `(G2.1;G2.2);G2.3` by mobile delivery robots. Each maps directly to concrete tasks (`AT1`, `AT2`, `AT3`).
* **Level 2 Decomposition (`G3` - Lab Processing)**: Broken down into picking/scanning (`G3.1`), sorting/loading (`G3.2`), and logging/tracking metadata (`G3.3`). Picking and scanning precede sorting and loading sequentially, while logging and tracking occur alongside processing. Each maps to robot tasks (`AT4`, `AT5`, `AT6`).
