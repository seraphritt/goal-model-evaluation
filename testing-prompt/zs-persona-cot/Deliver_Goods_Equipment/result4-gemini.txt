### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Mission Objective & Scope**:
The core mission requires a multi-robot system to collect required resources from storage locations and deliver them to requesting agents at specified locations. Examples include sterile medical equipment logistics (sterile facilities to use destinations) and clean linens (laundry to storage rooms).
2. **Phase Decomposition & Execution Flow**:
* The mission naturally decomposes into two primary sequential phases: **Collection Phase** and **Delivery Phase**.
* **Collection Phase**: Robots must navigate to storage places (ordered by waiting time plus path length), request resources via messages, and wait until retrieval. When multiple items are required from different storages, multiple robots can execute collect-deliver tasks in parallel.
* **Delivery Phase**: Robots make as many runs as necessary to transport all resources to the specified location.


3. **Exception Handling & Fallbacks**:
* **Battery Monitoring in Collection**: If battery drops to $10\%$, the robot must fall back to returning to the recharging station and reassigning the mission to another robot.
* **Battery Monitoring in Delivery**: If battery drops to $30\%$, the robot must fall back to returning the resource to a checkpoint and reassigning the remaining task to another robot.
* **Checkpoint Failure**: If returning the resource to a checkpoint fails, an alert must be triggered and a report sent to the sector manager.


4. **Goal Types & Decompositions**:
* **Achieve Goals** are used where a specific target condition must be reached (e.g., resources collected, delivered, or exceptions handled).
* **Perform Goals** are used where specific actions must be executed without a strict persistent target state (e.g., navigation/travel, alert triggering).
* **AND Decompositions** are used for complementary subgoals/tasks that must all be fulfilled.
* **OR Decompositions** are used for alternative paths.
* **Fallbacks** are applied where alternative behaviors are triggered by conditions, thresholds, or failures.
