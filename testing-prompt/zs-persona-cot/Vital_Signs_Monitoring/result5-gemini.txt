## Mission Analysis & Step-by-Step Reasoning

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a natural language mission description into a formal goal model requires rigorous analysis of operational dependencies, temporal constraints, exceptions, conditional branchings, and agent allocation rules. Below is the step-by-step reasoning and reflection process applied to the provided hospital monitoring mission description.

### 1. Analysis of Mission Objectives and Scope

* **Periodic Trigger & Fleet Scope:** The mission runs every two hours across all occupied hospital rooms, executed by a fleet of assigned robots. High-risk patients have a strict 15-minute priority constraint.
* **Pre-Entry Validation:** Before entering any room, robots must verify two independent preconditions: patient availability and sufficient battery. Failure triggers specific fallback routines (rescheduling or recharging/reassignment).
* **Patient Identification:** Inside the room, the robot must identify the correct patient using an alternative mechanism: either by asking or by scanning a barcode (**OR decomposition**).
* **Clinical Assessment & Conditional Vitals:** Vital sign checks depend on patient medical history (Infectious diseases, Post-surgical conditions, Diabetes). If collection fails, an alternative assessment (set of questions) is triggered (**FALLBACK**), followed by nurse/doctor alerts on non-response.
* **Hygiene & Safety Protocols:** Robots must disinfect actuators after patient contact and sterilize when exiting infectious disease rooms.
* **Scalability & Failure Handling:** Additional robots can be dynamically assigned (`[1, n]`) if timing constraints cannot be met by single robots. If the entire fleet fails to cover the mission, the site manager must be alerted.

## Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G1.1, G1.2 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: The main mission requires sequentially executing room visitation cycles across the hospital while simultaneously managing fleet scalability and failure contingencies if time limits are breached.


* **G1.1 -> G1.1.1, G1.1.2, G1.1.3, G1.1.4 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: A room visitation cycle strictly follows a sequential order: verifying pre-conditions before entry, identifying and instructing the patient inside, performing clinical vital checks, and finally completing post-room hygiene and marking completion.


* **G1.1.1 -> G1.1.1.1, G1.1.1.2 [AND][FALLBACK(G1.1.1.1, G1.1.1.2)]**
* **Justification**: Patient availability and battery levels are checked jointly as prerequisites; failure in availability triggers a 5-minute return timeout, while low battery triggers recharging and task reassignment.


* **G1.1.2 -> G1.1.2.1, G1.1.2.2 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Patient identification must precede procedure announcement and instructions inside the room.


* **G1.1.2.1 -> AT4, AT5 [OR]**
* **Justification**: The robot identifies the patient either by asking or by scanning their barcode (alternative choices).


* **G1.1.3 -> G1.1.3.1, G1.1.3.2 [AND][FALLBACK(G1.1.3.1, G1.1.3.2)]**
* **Justification**: Primary vital signs collection is attempted first; if collection fails, fallback assessment questions and medical staff escalation are triggered.

* **Justification**: Assigning additional robots is attempted first to meet required mission times; if no combination of robots can complete the mission, site manager alerts are triggered as a terminal fallback.
