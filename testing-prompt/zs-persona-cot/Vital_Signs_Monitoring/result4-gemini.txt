## Reasoning and Step-by-Step Thinking

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a natural language mission description into a formal goal model requires a systematic analysis of operational constraints, temporal dependencies, resource allocations, and exception handling protocols. Here is the step-by-step reflection process applied to this hospital multi-robot monitoring mission:

1. **Global Mission Objectives & Constraints Analysis**:
* The primary objective is to execute patient vital signs checks across all occupied hospital rooms every two hours.
* A critical spatial-temporal constraint is that **all high-risk patients must be checked within the first 15 minutes** of the mission. This requires global mission scheduling and prioritization (Goal G2).
* Multi-robot scalability is explicitly mentioned: additional robots can be assigned if the mission cannot be completed in time, and the site manager must be alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission.


2. **Pre-Entry and Operational Verification**:
* Before entering any room, robots must execute pre-entry checks: patient availability and battery sufficiency. These are query goals (`G3.1.1` and `G3.1.2`).
* If a patient is unavailable, the robot handles this via fallback/retry (come back in 5 minutes). If battery is insufficient, the robot must recharge and tasks must be reassigned (`G2.2.1`).


3. **Room Entry, Identification, and Patient Interaction**:
* Rooms may have multiple beds, necessitating patient identification either by asking or scanning a barcode—representing an **OR-decomposition** (`G3.2.1`).
* Once inside, the robot approaches, announces the procedure, and gives instructions (`G3.2.2`).
* Vital signs checks are condition-dependent (infectious, post-surgical, diabetes, or general), grouped under condition-specific measurement tasks (`G3.3.1`).


4. **Exception Handling and Sanitization**:
* Failure to collect vital signs triggers assessment questions (`G3.4.1`). Persistent non-response triggers an alert to a nurse or doctor (`G3.4.2`).
* Post-check procedures include marking the task done, sterilizing robots after infectious disease rooms, and disinfecting actuators after physical contact (`G3.4.3`).

* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: High-risk scheduling (G2.1) is prioritized at mission start, followed by ongoing exception handling and resource allocation (G2.2).
