As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, I have analyzed the mission description and decomposed it into a comprehensive hierarchical goal model and task list. Below is the step-by-step reasoning, followed by the detailed goal tables, task tables, summary table, and logical relationships.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Mission Objective & Scope**:
* The primary objective is to check all patient vital signs every two hours across all occupied hospital rooms, ensuring high-risk patients are checked within the first 15 minutes (**G1**).
* This requires a dual focus: managing mission resources, timelines, and robot allocations (**G2**), and executing the room-by-room vital signs checking workflow (**G3**).


2. **Resource & Timeline Management (`G2`)**:
* The mission requires initial robot assignment (**AT1**). If the mission cannot be completed in the required time, additional robots can be assigned (**AT2**), and if no combination of robots can complete the mission, the site manager must be alerted (**AT3**). This is modeled as an OR-decomposition between adding robots and alerting the manager when robot capacity is constrained.


3. **Room-by-Room Execution Workflow (`G3`)**:
* The execution sequence within rooms follows a structured progression: Pre-Entry Validation (`G3.1`) -> In-Room Interaction & Collection (`G3.2`) -> Post-Room Sterilization (`G3.3`) -> Proceeding to Next Room (`G3.4`), executed sequentially (`G3.1;G3.2;G3.3;G3.4`).


4. **Pre-Entry Validation (`G3.1`)**:
* Before entering, the robot must verify patient availability and battery sufficiency.
* Patient availability checking uses a fallback: if the patient is unavailable, the robot returns in 5 minutes (**G3.1.1** fallback **G3.1.2**).
* Battery check uses a fallback: if battery is insufficient, the robot recharges and tasks are reassigned (**G3.1.3**).
* Patient identification inside the room can be performed either by asking or by scanning the barcode, representing an **OR-decomposition** (**G3.1.4.1 OR G3.1.4.2**).


5. **In-Room Interaction & Vital Signs Collection (`G3.2`)**:
* Once inside, the robot approaches the patient, announces the procedure, and provides instructions (**G3.2.1**).
* Vital signs are collected based on medical conditions (infectious diseases, post-surgical, diabetes), which occur in parallel/combination depending on patient profiles (**G3.2.2**).
* If collection fails, assessment questions are triggered; in case of non-response, an alarm is sent to the nurse/doctor (**G3.2.3**, modeled with FALLBACK).
* Robots touching patients must disinfect their actuators (**G3.2.4**), and the check must be marked as done (**G3.2.5**).


6. **Post-Room & Sterilization (`G3.3` & `G3.4`)**:
* When exiting an infectious disease room, the robot must be sterilized (**G3.3**), followed by navigation to the next room (**G3.4**).
