### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Mission Scope & Stakeholders**:
* The mission involves heterogeneous agents: **human actors** (nurses, laboratory personnel) and **robotic actors** (mobile transport robots, robotic arms), interacting with hospital infrastructure (tracking systems, secure drawers, analysis machines).


2. **Decomposition into Core Goals**:
* **G1 (Root Goal)**: The overarching mission is to transport and process lab samples from patient floors to the laboratory.
* **G2 (Request & Registration)**: Initiated by nurses who collect samples, identify them with barcodes, and input data into the track system.
* **G3 (Secure Transport)**: Involves moving samples across hospital floors inside secure locked drawers with strict access control (authorized personnel or specific robots).
* **G4 (Laboratory Processing)**: Involves retrieving samples upon arrival at the lab, scanning barcodes, sorting, and loading them into analysis machines. This goal incorporates flexibility via a fallback between a robotic arm and human personnel.
* **G5 (Auditing & Monitoring)**: Ensures compliance and traceability by securely logging pickup locations/times and tracking elapsed time against laboratory protocols.


3. **Task Allocation & Runtime Semantics**:
* Tasks are mapped to specific hospital locations and assigned to appropriate resource counts (robots, humans, or software systems).
* Sequential (`[;]`) and parallel (`[#]`) runtimes, along with `FALLBACK` mechanisms, capture the operational workflow dependencies of a multi-robot hospital logistics system.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The top-level mission requires sequential progression: first a nurse registers and requests the sample (G2), then the sample is transported securely (G3), followed by laboratory processing upon arrival (G4), while audit logging and protocol tracking (G5) run concurrently throughout the mission.


* **G2 -> AT1 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Requesting and registering a sample relies directly on the nurse performing data entry into the tracking system (AT1).


* **G3 -> AT2, AT3 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Secure transport requires both the physical mobile transport of the locked drawers (AT3) and the active enforcement of secure access control / locking mechanisms (AT2) running concurrently during transit.


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2, G4.3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Laboratory processing must occur in a strict sequence: first the sample is retrieved (G4.1), then its barcode is scanned and it is sorted (G4.2), and finally it is loaded into the analysis machine (G4.3).


* **G4.1 -> AT4 [FALLBACK(Robotic Arm, Laboratory Personnel)][AND]**
* **Justification**: Sample retrieval in the laboratory can be performed either by a robotic arm or by human laboratory personnel as a fallback/alternative mechanism.


* **G4.2 -> AT5 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Scanning barcodes and sorting precede the final machine loading task executed by the robotic arm (AT5).


* **G5 -> AT6 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Logging location/timestamp and tracking protocol timing run continuously and concurrently in parallel alongside the physical mission execution.
