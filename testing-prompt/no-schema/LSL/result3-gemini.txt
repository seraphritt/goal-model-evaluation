As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), I have analyzed the hospital lab sample transport mission description. Below is the structured analysis, step-by-step reasoning, goal model decomposition, task mapping, summary, and logical relationship hierarchy.

### Step-by-Step Mission Analysis & Reasoning

1. **System & Stakeholder Identification:**
The mission operates in a hospital environment involving human agents (nurses, laboratory personnel), robotic agents (autonomous mobile robots with secure locked drawers, robotic arms), and cyber-physical infrastructure (tracking systems, barcode scanners, analysis machines, secure servers).
2. **High-Level Objective:**
The primary mission objective is to safely, securely, and efficiently transport medical lab samples from patient floors to the laboratory and prepare them for analysis.
3. **Workflow Decomposition:**
* **Initiation & Registration:** A nurse initiates the transport by submitting a request and registering sample data identified via a barcode into a tracking system.
* **Secure Transit:** Samples are placed in secure locked drawers. Access is restricted to authorized personnel or specific robots at designated locations. An autonomous mobile robot handles the physical transit from patient floors to the lab.
* **Laboratory Handover & Processing:** Upon arrival at the laboratory, sample extraction and loading can be performed either by a robotic arm (handling pick-up, barcode scanning, sorting, and loading into analysis machines) OR by laboratory personnel as a fallback/alternative option.
* **Auditing & Compliance:** The system must securely log pick-up locations and timestamps, and continuously track the overall delivery time against established laboratory protocols.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Fulfilling the overall mission (G1) strictly requires sequential and parallel execution of nurse registration (G2), secure transport (G3), laboratory processing (G4), and auditing/timing (G5).


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: A nurse must first submit the delivery request and subsequently scan/register sample barcode data into the track system before transport can proceed.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND][#]**
* **Justification**: During transit, the drawers must maintain secure locking enforcement (G3.1) in parallel with autonomous navigation across the hospital (G3.2).


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2 [OR][FALLBACK(G4.1 OR G4.2)]**
* **Justification**: The mission description specifies that sample pickup and processing can be handled by either a robotic arm OR laboratory personnel, providing operational fault tolerance.


* **G5 -> G5.1, G5.2 [AND][#]**
* **Justification**: System auditing requires simultaneous secure pick-up logging (location and time) and continuous delivery duration tracking against protocols.


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Goal G2 is achieved by the nurse executing task AT1 (submitting the request) and task AT2 (entering barcode data).


* **G3 -> AT3, AT4 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Secure transportation is executed by the mobile robot performing drawer locking (AT3) and navigating to the lab (AT4).


* **G4.1 -> AT5 [AND]**
* **Justification**: The robotic arm processing goal is fulfilled directly by executing robotic pick-up, barcode scanning, sorting, and loading (AT5).


* **G4.2 -> AT6 [AND]**
* **Justification**: The personnel fallback goal is achieved through manual handling and processing by laboratory staff (AT6).


* **G5 -> AT7, AT8 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Logging and tracking goals are realized through secure log recording (AT7) and real-time protocol monitoring (AT8).
