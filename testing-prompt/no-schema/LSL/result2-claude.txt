# Mission Analysis: Lab Sample Transport System (Multi-Robot System)

Before building the tables, let me work through the mission description carefully, the way a mission designer would — identifying actors, phases, cross-cutting concerns, and ambiguities that need an explicit design decision.

---

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning

### Step 1 — Identify the actors/agents in the narrative
Reading the text closely, I can list every agent that *does* something:

| Agent | Type | Role in the mission |
|---|---|---|
| Nurse | Human | Identifies sample, enters data, requests delivery |
| Track System | Software agent | Stores sample data, logs, monitors time |
| Transport Robot (implied) | Robot (mobile) | Physically carries the sample in a **locked drawer** from floor to lab — the text never names it, but "samples are transported in secure locked drawers" and the need to move between floors and the lab implies a mobile agent carrying the drawer. This is a necessary inference for a *multi-robot* reading of the text. |
| Robotic Arm | Robot (stationary, in lab) | Picks, scans, sorts, loads samples into analysis machines |
| Laboratory Personnel | Human | Alternative to the robotic arm |
| Analysis Machine | Passive equipment | Receives the sample (entry-module) — not an actor, just a target/interface |

**Reflection:** This confirms the "multi-robot" nature of the mission — there are at least **two distinct robot types** with different mobility, locations, and roles (mobile transport robot vs. stationary robotic arm). Treating them as the same actor would be a modeling mistake, so I keep them as separate goal/task owners.

### Step 2 — Segment the narrative into functional phases vs. cross-cutting concerns
Not every sentence describes a *sequential step*. Some describe **constraints that hold throughout** the mission. I classified each sentence:

- **Sequential/functional** (things that happen in order): barcode → data entry → request → load into drawer → travel → pick-up at lab → (scan → sort → load) OR (manual handling).
- **Cross-cutting (non-functional, continuous)**: "only authorized... should be able to open the locked drawers" (security, applies at *every* drawer-opening event, not just once); "information... should be logged securely" (logging applies at every pick-up event); "time... should be tracked" (a continuous measurement spanning the whole mission).

**Reflection:** If I had modeled security and monitoring as just another sequential step buried inside "transport," I would lose traceability — a reviewer wouldn't be able to see that access control governs *two* separate events (loading at the floor, and opening at the lab). So I promoted **Access Control** and **Monitoring** to top-level goals that run in **parallel** with the functional chain. This is standard practice in goal-oriented requirements engineering: separate functional goals from quality/security goals so they can be traced and verified independently.

### Step 3 — Decide Goal Types (Achieve / Perform / Query)
- Goals with a clear, checkable end-state → **Achieve** (e.g., "sample is in the lab," "drawer is locked").
- Goals that are really "execute this known procedure" (explicitly scripted by the text, e.g. "picks up... scans... sorts... loads") → **Perform**.
- A goal whose entire purpose is to obtain/verify a piece of information → **Query** (e.g., checking elapsed time against protocol).

### Step 4 — Resolve the OR vs. FALLBACK decision (most important design judgment call)
The text says: *"the sample can be picked by a robotic arm **or** laboratory personnel."* Literally this is an OR. However:
- The rest of the paragraph only elaborates the **robotic arm's** procedure in detail (scan, sort, load into analysis machine) — suggesting the robotic arm is the **primary/preferred** path, since it's the automated, more specified pipeline.
- In MRS mission design, when two execution strategies exist for the same goal and one is clearly the "automation-first" path, it is good practice to encode it as **FALLBACK(primary, secondary)** rather than a free OR, so the system knows to *attempt* automation first and *only* resort to the human path if the robot is unavailable/faulty/busy. This also gives the mission model resilience semantics it wouldn't have as a plain OR.
- The instructions explicitly state "FALLBACK must have an AND relation," which matches this framework's convention (both branches are legitimate parts of the plan; only the *trigger condition* differs, not the formal AND/OR split).

**Decision:** Model lab reception as `FALLBACK(Robotic Arm Handling, Manual Handling)` with an AND relation. I flag this clearly as a design interpretation, not a literal requirement.

### Step 5 — Reorder narrative into logical/temporal order
The text mentions "nurse can request a delivery" *before* mentioning the barcode and data entry — but logically, a sample must be **identified (barcode) → registered (data entry) → requested** in that causal order (you cannot register or request transport of a sample that hasn't been collected/identified yet). I reordered these into their logical execution sequence and justified it explicitly rather than following sentence order blindly.

### Step 6 — Explicit Assumptions (stated for traceability)
1. A **mobile transport robot** (not explicitly named in the text) is the agent that carries the locked drawer between floor and lab — inferred from "transported in secure locked drawers" + the need for floor↔lab mobility.
2. The authorization check ("only authorized personnel or specific robots in specific locations") applies at **two distinct points**: when the drawer is loaded/closed at the patient floor, and when it is opened at the laboratory — both are modeled.
3. "Information about where and when a sample was picked" is interpreted to cover **both** pick-up events (floor collection and lab retrieval), since "picked" is the same verb used generically in the text.
4. Robotic-arm-vs-personnel is modeled as **FALLBACK**, not plain OR (see Step 4).
5. The "time between sample collection and laboratory delivery" clock is treated as spanning the Preparation→Transport→Reception chain, which is why the Monitoring goal runs in parallel with that whole chain rather than with a single phase.

## 2. Goal Tree Overview (informal diagram)

```
G1  Transport Lab Sample from Patient Floor to Laboratory [Achieve]
 ├─ G2  Sample Preparation & Delivery Request [Achieve]           ─┐
 │   ├─ AT1 Attach Barcode to Sample                               │
 │   ├─ AT2 Record Sample Data in Track System                     │ sequential
 │   └─ AT3 Submit Delivery Request                                │
 ├─ G3  Secure Physical Transport to Laboratory [Achieve]           │ chain
 │   ├─ AT4 Load Sample into Locked Drawer                          │ (G2;G3;G4)
 │   └─ AT5 Navigate Patient Floor → Laboratory                     │
 ├─ G4  Laboratory Sample Reception & Processing [Achieve]         ─┘
 │   ├─ G4.1 Robotic Arm Sample Handling [Perform]   ─┐ FALLBACK
 │   │   ├─ AT6 Pick Up Sample                         │
 │   │   ├─ AT7 Scan Barcode                           │ sequential
 │   │   ├─ AT8 Sort Sample                            │
 │   │   └─ AT9 Load into Analysis Machine Entry-Module│
 │   └─ G4.2 Manual Handling by Lab Personnel [Perform]─┘
 │       └─ AT10 Manually Retrieve & Process Sample
 ├─ G5  Chain-of-Custody Monitoring [Achieve]   (‖ parallel to G2-G4)
 │   ├─ G5.1 Secure Pick-up Logging [Achieve]     ─┐ parallel
 │   │   └─ AT11 Record Pick-up Location/Time      │
 │   └─ G5.2 Protocol Time Compliance [Query]      ─┘
 │       └─ AT12 Measure & Compare Elapsed Time
 └─ G6  Drawer Access Control Enforcement [Achieve]  (‖ parallel to G2-G4)
     ├─ AT13 Verify Authorization – Pickup Point   ─┐ sequential
     └─ AT14 Verify Authorization – Laboratory Point┘
```

> **Legend:** `;` = sequential, `#` = parallel, `FALLBACK(a,b)` = try *a*, fall back to *b*.
> **Note on table columns:** For each Goal, *Runtime* and *Relation* describe how **that goal's own children** are combined/executed. For Tasks, *Relation* describes how that task relates to its **sibling tasks** under the same parent goal.

## 6. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5, G6 [AND][(G2 ; G3 ; G4) # G5 # G6]**
  - **Justification**: All five sub-goals are necessary for a successful delivery (AND). The functional pipeline (prepare → transport → process) is inherently sequential because each phase depends physically on the previous one finishing. Monitoring (G5) and access control (G6) are not discrete steps — they are continuous constraints that must hold *throughout* the functional pipeline, so they run in parallel (`#`) with it rather than before/after it.

- **G2 -> AT1, AT2, AT3 [AND][(sequential)]**
  - **Justification**: A sample must be physically identifiable (barcode) before its data can be meaningfully registered, and it must be registered before a delivery request referencing it makes sense. I reordered this from the text's sentence order into this causally necessary order (see Step 5 of my reasoning).

- **G3 -> AT4, AT5 [AND][(sequential)]**
  - **Justification**: The sample cannot be transported before it is secured in the drawer; "secure locked drawers" is the explicit transport mechanism named in the text.

- **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2 [AND][FALLBACK(G4.1, G4.2)]**
  - **Justification**: The text presents these as alternatives ("picked by a robotic arm **or** laboratory personnel"). I chose FALLBACK over a plain OR because the robotic-arm path is clearly the system's primary/automated pipeline (it's the only one elaborated with detailed steps), and modeling it as a fallback gives the mission designer explicit resilience semantics (robot unavailable/busy/failed → human takes over) rather than an unordered free choice. As instructed, FALLBACK is encoded with an AND relation.

- **G4.1 -> AT6, AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND][(sequential)]**
  - **Justification**: The text lists these actions in a fixed order — "picks up... scans... sorts... loads" — so the sequential operator directly mirrors the narrative.

- **G4.2 -> AT10 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single-child decomposition; trivial AND, no ordering needed.

- **G5 -> G5.1, G5.2 [AND][(parallel)]**
  - **Justification**: Logging a pick-up event and tracking elapsed transit time are independent monitoring functions that can and should run concurrently without one blocking the other.

- **G5.1 -> AT11 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single-task decomposition of the secure-logging requirement.

- **G5.2 -> AT12 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single-task decomposition of the time-tracking requirement.

- **G6 -> AT13, AT14 [AND][(sequential)]**
  - **Justification**: The same authorization rule applies at two physically and temporally distinct checkpoints — loading at the patient floor, then opening at the laboratory — which occur one after the other in the mission timeline, hence sequential rather than parallel.

## 7. Further Reflections / Design Notes

- **Multi-robot distinction matters**: The model deliberately keeps the *mobile transport robot* (AT4, AT5, AT13, AT14) separate from the *stationary robotic arm* (AT6–AT9), since they differ in mobility, location, and failure modes — collapsing them would hide a key MRS coordination point (handoff of the sample at the lab).
- **Security and monitoring as parallel, cross-cutting goals (G5, G6)** rather than inline steps is a deliberate traceability choice: it lets a reviewer audit "is access control enforced everywhere it needs to be?" and "is logging complete?" independently of the functional flow.
- **Open question for the mission owner**: should G4's FALLBACK trigger condition be "robotic arm unavailable/faulty" or should it instead be a true free-choice OR (e.g., load-balancing between arm and personnel during high volume)? I resolved this in favor of FALLBACK based on the text's emphasis on the arm's detailed procedure, but this is the single most interpretive decision in this model and worth confirming with stakeholders.
- **Not modeled explicitly** (outside the given text, flagged rather than invented): what happens if the robotic arm fails *mid*-sequence (e.g., after scanning but before sorting) — a full MRS specification would likely want recovery/retry goals here, but nothing in the description calls for it, so I did not add it to avoid overreach.
