# Mission Analysis: Lab Sample Transport & Processing (Hospital MRS)

Before building the model, let me think through this systematically as a mission designer would — identifying actors, extracting events, resolving ambiguity, and only then committing to goal structures.

---

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 1.1 Actor & Resource Identification
Reading the description carefully, I can identify the following actors/resources:

| Actor | Type | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Nurse | Human | Initiates request, labels sample, registers data |
| **Transport Robot** (inferred) | Robot (mobile) | Carries the sample in a locked drawer from floor → lab |
| **Robotic Arm** | Robot (manipulator, stationary) | Picks, scans, sorts, loads sample at the lab |
| Laboratory Personnel | Human | Alternative actor to the robotic arm |
| Track System | Software/Infrastructure | Stores sample metadata, logs, timing |
| Analysis Machine | Equipment | Receives sample via entry-module |

**Reflection/Assumption:** The text never explicitly names a "transport robot," but since (a) the mission is explicitly multi-robot, (b) samples are moved physically between floors, and (c) the access-control sentence mentions "specific robots in specific locations" opening drawers, I infer a dedicated mobile robot carries the locked drawer. This is a necessary inference to keep the model robot-centric rather than treating transport as a magic teleportation step. I flag this explicitly as a design assumption.

This also confirms the **multi-robot** nature of the mission: at least two heterogeneous robots cooperate — a **mobile logistics robot** and a **stationary manipulator** — a classic heterogeneous MRS pattern (transport + manipulation).

### 1.2 Event Extraction (chronological)
Breaking the paragraph into atomic events, independent of the order they appear in the text:
1. Sample gets a barcode ("before collection")
2. Nurse inserts sample data into track system
3. Nurse requests delivery
4. Sample placed into a secure, locked drawer
5. Drawer is transported floor → lab
6. Someone opens the drawer — but **only if authorized** (personnel or specific robot in specific location)
7. Pick happens via **robotic arm OR personnel** (explicit alternative)
8. If robotic arm: scan → sort → load into analysis machine entry-module
9. Log *where/when* picked, **securely**
10. Track elapsed time between collection and delivery, checked against protocol

**Reflection:** Note that the text states the nurse's "request" capability in the *first* sentence of detail, but logically a nurse would label and register a sample as part of preparing it. I chose to treat barcode-first as the defining precondition (explicit "before collection") and placed request last in the sub-sequence, since requesting delivery is the trigger that hands the sample off to the transport phase. This is an interpretive ordering choice — the text doesn't mandate it, but it is the most operationally coherent order.

### 1.3 Phase Decomposition
Grouping events into four coherent mission phases gives a clean AND-decomposition of the root goal:
- **Phase A** — Prepare & Request (events 1–3)
- **Phase B** — Secure Transport (events 4–5)
- **Phase C** — Lab Reception & Processing (events 6–8)
- **Phase D** — Monitoring & Compliance (events 9–10) — this is **cross-cutting**, not a discrete phase; logging happens *at* the pick moment (end of Phase C) and time-tracking spans *all* of Phases A–C. This is why I model Phase D as running in **parallel** with the main chain rather than as a fourth sequential step.

### 1.4 Resolving the OR in Phase C (robotic arm vs. personnel)
The text states picking "can be" done by either actor — a genuine alternative. Two valid modeling choices exist:
- **Plain OR**: either actor may be chosen, with no implied preference.
- **FALLBACK**: robotic arm is attempted first (automation-first, since this is an MRS and the text elaborates the robot's procedure in detail but not personnel's); if unavailable/fails, personnel take over.

I chose **FALLBACK** because (a) it reflects real hospital automation practice (robots are deployed for throughput, humans for resilience/exception-handling), (b) the text gives a *detailed* multi-step procedure for the robotic arm only, suggesting it's the primary/designed path, and (c) FALLBACK is more informative for runtime orchestration than a bare OR. I flag this as a **design decision**, not a strict textual requirement — a reviewer could reasonably argue for plain OR instead.

### 1.5 Placing the Authorization Constraint
"Only authorized personnel or specific robots in specific locations should be able to open the locked drawers" immediately follows the sentence about picking at the lab. I placed this as a **gating Query goal** right before the pick fallback (G3.1), rather than under transport (G2), because the act being gated is "opening," which textually belongs to the lab-arrival/pick moment, not the loading moment on the patient floor.

### 1.6 Goal-Type Assignments (Perform / Query / Achieve)
My rule of thumb:
- **Perform** → a discrete action with no meaningful "state to verify" beyond execution (e.g., submit a request, attach a label).
- **Achieve** → a target state that must hold, possibly via planning/multiple steps (e.g., "robot is at the lab," "log entry exists securely").
- **Query** → retrieving/evaluating information to gate or inform a decision (e.g., authorization check, elapsed-time check).

I applied this consistently rather than mapping 1:1 to verbs, which forced some decisions — e.g., "log securely" became **Achieve** (not Perform) because "securely" is a quality/state condition worth verifying, not just an action to fire-and-forget.

### 1.7 Runtime Operators
I used:
- `;` wherever the text implies an explicit precondition ("before collection", "picks...scans...sorts...loads" as one chained action).
- `#` wherever two concerns are logically independent in time (compliance monitoring vs. the functional pipeline; logging vs. time-tracking).
- `FALLBACK(...)` exactly once, for the robot/human alternative at pick time, per the instruction that fallback branches carry an **AND** relation.

## 5. Logical Relationships

**Goal-to-Goal:**

- **G0 -> G1, G2, G3, G4 [AND][(G1 ; G2 ; G3) # G4]**
  - **Justification**: The mission is a single chronological chain (prepare → transport → lab reception), while compliance monitoring (logging + time-tracking) spans that entire chain rather than following it — so it must run in parallel, not after. All four branches are mandatory, hence AND.

- **G1 -> G1.1, G1.2, G1.3 [AND][sequential]**
  - **Justification**: Barcode identification is explicitly required "before collection"; registering data and requesting delivery logically follow as preparation steps feeding the transport phase. None is optional.

- **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2 [AND][sequential]**
  - **Justification**: "Transported in secure locked drawers" requires securing the sample before it can be safely moved — locking is a precondition for navigation.

- **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2, G3.3 [AND][G3.1 ; FALLBACK(G3.2, G3.3)]**
  - **Justification**: Authorization must be verified before any drawer-opening ("only authorized personnel or specific robots... should be able to open"). The text then offers two alternative actors for picking ("robotic arm or laboratory personnel"); I modeled this as FALLBACK (robotic arm primary/automated, personnel as backup) to capture realistic MRS robustness — if the arm is unavailable, the mission still completes. Per the required convention, fallback branches are related by AND, not OR.

- **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2 [AND][parallel]**
  - **Justification**: Logging the pick event and tracking total elapsed time are independent, concurrently-running compliance checks — neither depends on the other's completion, and both are mandatory.

**Goal-to-Task:**

- **G1.1 -> T1.1 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single atomic action fully realizes the goal.

- **G1.2 -> T1.2 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Direct 1:1 mapping to the nurse's data-entry action.

- **G1.3 -> T1.3 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Direct 1:1 mapping to the nurse's request action.

- **G2.1 -> T2.1 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Loading and locking are bundled into one physical handoff action between nurse and transport robot.

- **G2.2 -> T2.2 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Navigation is a single continuous robot behavior realizing the "reach the lab" state.

- **G3.1 -> T3.1 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: One verification action gates the fallback pick that follows.

- **G3.2 -> T3.2, T3.3, T3.4, T3.5 [AND][sequential]**
  - **Justification**: Mirrors the text's chained description ("picks up... scans... sorts... loads...") — each action is a precondition for the next (can't scan before picking, can't load before sorting).

- **G3.3 -> T3.6 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: The fallback goal is realized by a single manual action; the text gives no further detail on personnel's internal steps, so no further decomposition is justified.

- **G4.1 -> T4.1 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: One logging action directly realizes the secure-log target condition.

- **G4.2 -> T4.2 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: One computation/verification action directly realizes the elapsed-time query.

## 6. Design Notes & Flagged Assumptions

1. **Two heterogeneous robots** emerged from the analysis: a *mobile Transport Robot* (logistics, locked-drawer carrier) and a *Robotic Arm* (manipulation, stationary, lab-side). This is the clearest multi-robot signal in the text and should anchor any downstream architecture/behavior-tree design.
2. **FALLBACK vs. plain OR** at G3: I chose FALLBACK because the text elaborates the robotic arm's procedure in detail (suggesting it's the designed/primary path) while giving personnel only a one-line mention. A reviewer preferring a neutral reading (no implied priority) could substitute plain OR with equal relation — this is a legitimate alternative interpretation worth validating with stakeholders.
3. **Authorization placement**: I scoped G3.1 to the lab-side "opening" event only, since that's where the text places the constraint. If the hospital's security policy also restricts who may *load* the drawer at the patient floor, G2 would need a symmetric authorization sub-goal — the text doesn't state this, so I did not add it speculatively.
4. **Personnel's internal steps are underspecified** — the text never says whether personnel also scan/sort/load or hand the sample to a separate manual lab workflow. I deliberately left G3.3/T3.6 shallow rather than inventing steps not supported by the text.
5. **"Securely" as a quality attribute** drove my Achieve (not Perform) classification for G4.1 — logging "securely" implies a verifiable property (tamper-resistance/access control), not just a fire-and-forget action.
