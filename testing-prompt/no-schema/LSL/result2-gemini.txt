## Mission Designer Reasoning & Step-by-Step Thinking

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a natural language mission description into a formal goal and task model requires a systematic analysis of operational workflows, spatial constraints, actor capabilities (robots vs. humans), security boundaries, and timing/monitoring requirements.

### Step 1: Stakeholder & Entity Identification

* **Actors:** Nurses, laboratory personnel, autonomous transport robots, robotic arm, analysis machines.
* **Objects/Resources:** Lab samples, barcodes, tracking system, secure locked drawers, entry-module of analysis machines, secure logs, protocol time trackers.
* **Locations:** Patient floors, hospital corridors/transit paths, laboratory facility.

### Step 2: Top-Level Mission Objective (Root Goal)

The overarching mission is to transport lab samples from patient floors to the laboratory and integrate them into analysis machines while ensuring security, traceability, and temporal compliance. This breaks down into four primary pillars:

1. **Initiation & Registration (G2):** Nurse interaction and sample identification.
2. **Secure Mobility & Transport (G3):** Moving samples across hospital zones safely.
3. **Laboratory Processing & Integration (G4):** Handling samples via robotic arm or personnel into analyzers.
4. **Auditing & Protocol Monitoring (G5):** Secure logging and time tracking.

### Step 3: Granular Goal & Task Derivation

* **G2 (Sample Request & Registration):** Requires identifying samples via barcodes and entering data into the tracking system. This is a sequential prerequisite (`[;]`) before transport can begin.
* **G3 (Secure Transport):** Involves physical navigation from patient floors to the lab using locked drawers with strict access control (restricted to authorized personnel or specific robots).
* **G4 (Lab Handling & Processing):** Involves picking, barcode scanning, sorting, and loading samples into analysis machines. This can be performed by a robotic arm or lab personnel (modeled as a `FALLBACK` with an `AND` internal relation or alternative execution paths).
* **G5 (Logging & Protocol Tracking):** Ensures pick location/time is logged securely and delivery duration conforms to lab protocols.

## Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The mission must follow a strict lifecycle: first the sample is requested and registered (G2), then transported across the hospital (G3), processed at the laboratory (G4), while logging and timing are monitored continuously throughout the process (G5).


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: A nurse must first identify the sample with a barcode (AT1) before entering the sample data and requesting delivery in the track system (AT2).


* **G3 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Samples must be secured inside locked drawers (AT3) before the transport robot can navigate and carry them from the patient floors to the laboratory (AT4).


* **G4 -> AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Upon arrival at the laboratory, authorized entities must retrieve samples (AT5), followed by robotic barcode scanning and sorting (AT6), culminating in loading them into the analysis machines (AT7).


* **G5 -> AT8, AT9 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Secure logging of pick location/time (AT8) and protocol time tracking (AT9) occur concurrently as background monitoring processes during mission execution.
