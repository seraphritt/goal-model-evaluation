### Step-by-Step Thinking & Reasoning

#### 1. Mission Analysis & Functional Decomposition

The mission describes a reactive, multi-robot logistics system operating in a structured environment (such as a hospital). The core objective is divided into two primary phases: **Collection** and **Delivery**.

* **Collection Phase:** Robots must navigate to storage facilities. The routing and sequencing are optimized using waiting time estimation plus path length. Upon arrival, the robot communicates precise requirements and waits for retrieval.
* **Delivery Phase:** The robot executes multiple transport runs until all resources are successfully delivered to the requesting agent's location.

#### 2. Fault Tolerance & Exception Management

Multi-robot systems operate in dynamic environments where hardware degradation (e.g., battery depletion) and operational failures are inevitable. The description specifies strict, context-aware contingency protocols:

* **Collection Phase Battery Low ($\le 10\%$):** Requires immediate return to a recharging station and full mission handover to another robot.
* **Delivery Phase Battery Low ($\le 30\%$):** Since carrying resources requires safety margins, the robot must deposit the resource at a designated checkpoint before reassignment so the successor knows its precise location.
* **Checkpoint Return Failure:** If a robot fails to secure the resource at a checkpoint, safety protocols dictate triggering an automated alert and dispatching an incident report to the sector manager.

#### 3. Scalability & Parallelism

To optimize throughput when multiple items are requested from disparate storages, the system supports parallelized collect-deliver tasks across a fleet of multiple robots.

#### 4. Domain Instantiation

Real-world applications like sterile medical equipment transport and clean linens distribution dictate that locations are situated within hospital facilities (storage rooms, use destinations, laundry facilities, checkpoints, recharging stations, and sector management offices).
