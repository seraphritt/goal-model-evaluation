### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Reflection

1. **Context & Domain Identification**:
The mission takes place in a hospital or logistics-driven environment (indicated by examples like *sterile medical equipment* and *clean linens*). Multiple heterogeneous or homogeneous robots operate concurrently to fetch items from storage facilities and deliver them to requesting agents.
2. **Phase Decomposition**:
The workflow naturally breaks down into two primary operational phases:
* **Collection Phase (`G2`)**: Robots must navigate to storage areas using an optimized cost function (waiting time + path cost), request the precise specifications of resources, and wait for retrieval.
* **Delivery Phase (`G3`)**: Once retrieved, robots execute single or multiple runs to transport items to the specified destination.


3. **Fault Tolerance & Exception Handling (Safety Critical)**:
Multi-robot systems operating in dynamic environments require rigorous fallback mechanisms to handle resource and hardware constraints (specifically battery depletion):
* **Collection Battery Drop ($\le 10\%$ - `G4.1`)**: Requires immediate return to the recharging station and task reassignment.
* **Delivery Battery Drop ($\le 30\%$ - `G4.2`)**: Since the robot is carrying a payload, it must drop the resource at a checkpoint and reassign the remaining task, allowing another robot to take over.
* **Checkpoint Failure (`G4.3`)**: If returning to a checkpoint fails, an escalation protocol triggers an alert and reports to the sector manager.


4. **Multi-Robot Parallelism**:
When multiple items are requested across different storages, robots can operate concurrently (`(parallel)`) to minimize overall mission completion time.
