As a mission designer specialized in **Multi-Robot Systems (MRS)**, analyzing complex operational requirements and translating them into a structured goal model is critical for ensuring fault-tolerant, efficient, and coordinated robotic execution.

Below is the step-by-step reasoning, followed by the comprehensive goal model, task decomposition, summary table, and logical relationship mapping for the hospital and facility resource logistics mission.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Mission Decomposition & Phases**:
* The mission is fundamentally a logistics operation: transporting goods (such as sterile medical equipment or clean linens) from source facilities (storage/laundry) to requested destinations (use destinations/storage rooms).
* The mission naturally splits into two main sequential phases: **Collection** (`G2`) and **Delivery** (`G3`), orchestrated under an overarching mission goal (`G1`).


2. **Collection Phase Mechanics**:
* Robots must travel to storage facilities. The priority/order is determined by optimizing the sum of estimated waiting time and path distance.
* Upon reaching storage, communication occurs (`AT2`): a message with precise specifications is sent, and the robot waits for retrieval.


3. **Delivery Phase Mechanics**:
* Once items are retrieved, delivery begins (`G3`). The robot performs multiple runs as necessary until all resources are transported to the specified location.


4. **Fault Tolerance & Battery Management (`G4`)**:
* Multi-robot systems operate in dynamic environments where hardware degradation (battery depletion) is a primary risk factor. Two distinct thresholds are defined:
* **Collection phase (10% battery)**: The robot aborts collection, heads to the recharging station, and reassigns the mission to a peer robot.
* **Delivery phase (30% battery)**: Because carrying items requires preservation, the robot must return the resource to a predefined *checkpoint* and reassign the remaining task to another robot with positional awareness.


* **Safety Exception**: If returning the resource to the checkpoint fails, an escalation protocol is triggered (alert + report to sector manager).


5. **Multi-Robot Scalability & Parallelism (`G5`)**:
* When multiple items are required from distinct storages, the system leverages multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) to dispatch multiple robots in **parallel (`[#]`)** to minimize mission completion time.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5 [AND][(sequential) with parallel task allocation support]**
* **Justification**: The top-level mission requires performing collection and delivery phases sequentially while continuously monitoring battery/fault conditions and leveraging parallel multi-robot coordination when multiple storage items are requested.


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The collection phase requires first navigating to the storage location (AT1) and subsequently requesting and waiting for the resources to be retrieved (AT2).


* **G3 -> AT3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The delivery phase requires executing transport runs (AT3) to move resources from storage to the specified use destination.


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2, G4.3 FALLBACK(AT4, AT5, AT6)**
* **Justification**: Fault and battery management operates as a fallback mechanism where normal execution falls back to battery reassignments (AT4 for collection, AT5 for delivery) or failure reporting (AT6) when thresholds or errors are encountered.


* **G5 -> AT1, AT3 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Multi-robot coordination enables multiple robots to execute navigation (AT1) and transport (AT3) tasks concurrently across different storage facilities to reduce total completion time.
