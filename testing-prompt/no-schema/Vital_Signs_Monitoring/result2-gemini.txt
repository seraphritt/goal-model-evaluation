### Step-by-Step Mission Analysis & Reflection

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a complex healthcare mission description requires a rigorous systems-engineering approach. We must account for temporal constraints, safety protocols, resource constraints (battery and fleet capacity), conditional clinical branching, and robust failure management.

1. **Temporal & Spatial Decomposition:**
* The mission is periodic (**every two hours**), demanding a temporal loop structure.
* Spatial coverage spans all occupied hospital rooms, with strict prioritization: **all high-risk patients must be checked within the first 15 minutes**. This implies task scheduling and multi-robot load balancing.


2. **Pre-Condition & Resource Verification:**
* Before entering any room, the robot must verify **battery adequacy** and **patient availability**.
* If battery is insufficient, the robot must abort/recharge, and tasks must be reassigned—highlighting the necessity of a dynamic multi-robot coordination layer.
* If the patient is unavailable, a temporal fallback (retry in 5 minutes) is triggered.


3. **Patient Identification & Clinical Protocols:**
* Rooms may have multiple beds, requiring disambiguation via **barcode scanning or direct inquiry** (a Query goal).
* Clinical checks are state-dependent based on patient history/condition:
* *Infectious diseases*: Heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, blood oxygenation.
* *Post-surgical*: Heart rate, blood oxygenation.
* *Diabetes*: Blood glucose.




4. **Safety, Hygiene, & Error Handling:**
* Touching patients requires actuator disinfection; treating infectious patients mandates full robot sterilization upon exit.
* Collection failures trigger assessment questions, and non-responses escalate to medical staff (nurse/doctor alerts).
* If the multi-robot fleet cannot complete the mission within constraints, additional robots are assigned, or a manager alert is raised if capacity is exhausted.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Fleet resource management and mission scheduling must be initialized sequentially before navigating and executing room checks.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must first arrive at the room (navigation) before verifying battery and patient availability.


* **G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Achieving room pre-checks requires executing navigation and resource/availability evaluation tasks.


* **G4 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The robot must first identify the correct patient via barcode or query before approaching and announcing the procedure.


* **G5 -> AT5, AT6 [FALLBACK(AT5, AT6)]**
* **Justification**: If primary vital sign collection (AT5) fails, the robot falls back to exception handling (AT6: assessment questions and medical staff escalation).


* **G6 -> AT7, AT8 [AND] [(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Hygiene protocols (disinfection/sterilization) and fleet task updates/coordination can be managed concurrently upon room completion.
