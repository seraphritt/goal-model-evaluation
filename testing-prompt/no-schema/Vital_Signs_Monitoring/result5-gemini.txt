### Mission Designer Analysis & Step-by-Step Reasoning

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing this natural language mission description requires a rigorous systems-engineering approach. The mission operates in a dynamic, safety-critical hospital environment with strict temporal constraints, heterogeneous robot capabilities, and robust fault-tolerance mechanisms.

#### 1. Temporal & Spatial Decomposition

* **Periodic Scheduling:** The core mission repeats every 2 hours, requiring continuous fleet coordination and fleet-level time tracking.
* **High-Priority Constraints:** Patients at high risk must be checked within the first 15 minutes, necessitating dynamic task re-ordering and prioritized task allocation across the multi-robot team.
* **Spatial Distribution:** Robots navigate across multiple hospital rooms with one or more beds, handling room-to-room transitions and condition-specific checks (infectious, post-surgical, diabetes).

#### 2. Pre-Condition & Safety Validation

* Before room entry, robots must evaluate environmental and operational preconditions: patient availability (with a 5-minute retry loop) and battery sufficiency.
* Battery depletion triggers a fallback mechanism: returning to recharge and dynamically reassigning remaining tasks to other active robots in the fleet.
* Strict biological safety protocols require robot sterilization after exiting infectious disease rooms, and actuator disinfection after any physical contact with patients.

#### 3. Fault Tolerance & Exception Handling

* Vital sign collection failures trigger an interactive assessment questionnaire. Non-responses escalate immediately to human medical staff (nurses/doctors).
* If the robot fleet cannot complete the mission within required temporal constraints, additional robots are assigned dynamically. If no combination of robots can fulfill the mission, a critical alert is dispatched to the site manager.
