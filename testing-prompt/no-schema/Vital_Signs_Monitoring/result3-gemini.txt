As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), analyzing natural language mission descriptions requires transforming unstructured operational rules into a rigorous, hierarchical goal model. Below is the step-by-step reasoning, followed by the structured goal tables, task tables, summary table, and logical relationships governing the hospital robotic monitoring mission.

---

### Step-by-Step Thinking & Mission Analysis

1. **Periodic and Temporal Constraints**:
* The mission initiates **every two hours** (`G1`).
* High-risk patients must be prioritized within the **first 15 minutes** of the mission execution window (`G2`).
* If a patient is unavailable, the robot reschedules a return visit in **5 minutes** (temporal fallback/delay).


2. **Resource & Safety Management (Multi-Robot Dynamics)**:
* Robots must monitor their **battery level** before entering rooms (`G3.1`). If battery is insufficient, the robot triggers a recharge and **task reassignment** across the multi-robot fleet.
* Because infectious disease rooms require robot sterilization upon exit, and physical contact requires actuator disinfection, strict hygiene and sanitization protocols are embedded as safety goals (`G3.2`).
* If the multi-robot fleet cannot complete the mission within required time constraints, additional robots are assigned dynamically, and the site manager is alerted if no valid combination exists (`G5`).


3. **Room-Level Clinical Assessment Protocol (`G4`)**:
* **Pre-Entry**: Check patient availability and battery (`G4.1`).
* **Patient Identification**: Use an `OR` relation between barcode scanning and direct questioning (`G4.2`).
* **Condition-Specific Clinical Checks**: Vital sign collection depends dynamically on patient medical history (infectious diseases, post-surgical conditions, diabetes history) (`G4.3`).
* **Exception Handling & Escalation**: Failed vital sign collection triggers assessment questions; non-response triggers an alert to the responsible nurse/doctor (`G4.4`).

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Periodic execution requires simultaneous management of fleet resources (G3), room navigation and prioritization (G2), execution of clinical assessments inside rooms (G4), and global mission exception handling (G5).


* **G2 -> T1, T12 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Room visitation requires navigating to the room (T1) and concluding the room cycle by marking it done and transitioning (T12).


* **G3 -> T2, T9, T10, T11 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Fleet resource and safety management oversees battery checks (T2), recharging/reassignment (T9), robot sterilization (T10), and actuator disinfection (T11) concurrently across robots.


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2, G4.3, G4.4 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Inside each room, the robot must sequentially validate pre-entry conditions (G4.1), identify the patient (G4.2), collect condition-specific vitals (G4.3), and handle post-collection processing and fallbacks (G4.4).


* **G4.1 -> T2, T3 [AND][(parallel)]**
* **Justification**: Pre-entry validation requires checking both battery sufficiency (T2) and patient availability (T3) before proceeding.


* **G4.2 -> T4 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Patient identification relies directly on task T4 (barcode scanning or verbal inquiry).


* **G4.3 -> T5, T6 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Clinical collection requires approaching and announcing the procedure (T5) before acquiring the specific vital signs (T6).


* **G4.4 -> T7, T8, T10, T11, T12 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Post-collection processing involves fallback assessment questions on failure (T7), alerting nurses on non-response (T8), executing sterilization/disinfection (T10, T11), and marking the room complete (T12).


* **G5 -> T9 [FALLBACK(T9)]**
* **Justification**: If task execution fails due to robot limitations, additional robots are assigned or tasks are reassigned (T9), falling back to site manager alerts if no fleet combination can succeed.
