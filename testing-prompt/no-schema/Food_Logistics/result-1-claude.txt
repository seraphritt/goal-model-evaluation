# Goal-Oriented Mission Decomposition — Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Retrieval Robot(s)

## 0. Method & Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the model I worked through the description in the following steps. I'm showing this explicitly because the structural choices (AND/OR, FALLBACK, Perform/Query/Achieve) all fall out of this reasoning rather than being arbitrary.

**Step 1 — Identify actors and resources.**
Delivering/retrieving robot(s), a possible *second cooperating robot*, the inpatient, a companion visitor, a nurse, the kitchen (request source), and the room door (a shared physical resource). The explicit mention of "another robot," "two robots," and "robot+human" cooperation confirms this is a genuine **multi-robot** (not single-robot) mission — cooperation is a first-class concern, not an edge case.

**Step 2 — Identify mission triggers.**
Two independent triggers are described: (a) a *kitchen delivery request* (per order), and (b) a *room-originated call* for dish pick-up. These define two **top-level mission threads** that can run concurrently and repeatedly (order-by-order), which justifies a parallel, AND-related root decomposition.

**Step 3 — Identify the linear backbone of each thread.**
Both "deliver food" and "retrieve dishes" are physically linear processes (receive trigger → move → access room → act → close the loop). This justifies **sequential ([;]) AND-decomposition** at the top level of each thread — none of these steps can be skipped or reordered.

**Step 4 — Identify points of genuine variability.**
Three places in the text explicitly present *alternative ways of achieving the same sub-goal*, each conditioned on availability/capability:
  - Delivery mode: table placement (needs manipulation skill) **or** tray fetch (needs cooperation).
  - Tray-fetch cooperator: inpatient **or** companion **or** nurse **or** another robot — explicitly flagged as *uncertain and sometimes unavailable*.
  - Door opening: cooperation with a robot **or** a human; plus, in the call-triggered case, the caller may simply open it themselves.
  - Dish retrieval: unassisted **or** two-robot cooperation **or** robot+human cooperation.

**Step 5 — Decide FALLBACK vs. plain OR.**
Because the text explicitly ties these alternatives to *uncertainty and unavailability* ("some inpatients could be unable…", "a companion… could not be available…", "subject to uncertainty"), these are not design-time, one-shot choices — they are **runtime escalation chains**: try the most autonomous/least-costly option, and if the precondition fails, escalate. This is exactly the semantics of the `FALLBACK(...)` operator. Per the notation rule given, every FALLBACK is therefore modeled with **Relation = AND** (the parent requires that *one* branch of the ordered alternative set succeeds — the decomposition itself is a structural AND over "attempt options in order," not an unordered design-time OR).
I ordered every fallback chain **in the same order the text lists the alternatives**, since this mirrors a natural "least human/robot cost first" escalation heuristic (autonomy → cooperation → escalation to staff/second robot), a common MRS design heuristic to minimize disruption to hospital staff.

**Step 6 — Classify goal types.**
- **Query**: goals whose purpose is to obtain information needed for a decision (request contents, retrieval capability, door-opening capability, retrieved-meal identity). The explicit statement "this information is subject to uncertainty" is a strong signal that these must be modeled as *Query* goals feeding into downstream *Achieve* goals, rather than being hard-coded preconditions.
- **Achieve**: goals defined by a condition to bring about, especially where multiple means exist or success isn't guaranteed by construction (navigation, room access, meal hand-off, dish collection).
- **Perform**: deterministic, single-procedure actions (loading, placing, signaling, logging, alerting, the concrete act of cooperating).

**Step 7 — Identify "cross-cutting" goals.**
"Track when/where retrieved" and "alert if wrong meal" are monitoring goals layered on top of the delivery act, not alternative delivery strategies — I placed tracking at the G1 level (applies regardless of delivery mode) and indicate/monitor/alert nested only under the tray-fetch branch (table delivery is unambiguous — the robot itself places the single correct meal).

**Step 8 — Flag inferences.**
The text states door-opening cooperation explicitly only in the dish-retrieval paragraph. Physically, a robot delivering food also needs to get through a closed inpatient-room door. I include "Access Room" under the delivery thread too, but flag it explicitly as an **engineering inference**, not a literal requirement — transparency on this point matters for a mission designer handing this model to a requirements reviewer.

**Step 9 — Decompose to atomic, assignable tasks.**
Every leaf goal is realized by exactly one atomic task (AT#), with Location and robot cardinality assigned according to whether the action is solo, two-robot, or robot+human.

## 6. Logical Relationships

### Goal → Goal

- **G0 -> G1, G2 [AND](parallel)**
  - *Justification*: Delivery and dish retrieval are both mandatory, independently-triggered capabilities (kitchen request vs. room call); nothing restricts them from running concurrently across different rooms/robots.

- **G1 -> G1.1, G1.2, G1.3, G1.4, G1.5, G1.6 [AND](sequential)**
  - *Justification*: "Delivery is made on an order-by-order basis" defines one linear process per order; each phase (receive → load → navigate → access → deliver → track) is a precondition for the next, no step is optional.

- **G1.4 -> G1.4.1, G1.4.2 [AND] FALLBACK(G1.4.1, G1.4.2)**
  - *Justification*: Door opening needs *some* cooperator; robot-cooperation is attempted first (keeps staff free), falling back to human cooperation if no robot is available. FALLBACK forces AND per the notation rule.

- **G1.5 -> G1.5.1, G1.5.2 [AND] FALLBACK(G1.5.1, G1.5.2)**
  - *Justification*: "can be delivered into a room table... or... fetched from the robot's tray" — table delivery is the autonomous, cooperation-free option (gated by manipulation skill) and is the preferred path; tray retrieval is the fallback requiring cooperation.

- **G1.5.2 -> G1.5.2.1, G1.5.2.2, G1.5.2.3, G1.5.2.4, G1.5.2.5 [AND](sequential)**
  - *Justification*: Must know who *can* retrieve before securing them, must secure an agent before indicating the meal, and must detect what was retrieved before deciding to alert — each is a strict precondition of the next.

- **G1.5.2.2 -> G1.5.2.2.1, G1.5.2.2.2, G1.5.2.2.3, G1.5.2.2.4 [AND] FALLBACK(…1,…2,…3,…4)**
  - *Justification*: "requires cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot," combined with "some inpatients could be unable... a companion... or nurse could not be available" — a direct textual description of an ordered escalation chain under uncertainty/unavailability.

- **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3, G2.4 [AND](sequential)**
  - *Justification*: Mirrors G1's linear structure — trigger, move, access, act — "as with delivery" is stated explicitly in the text.

- **G2.3 -> G2.3.1, G2.3.2 [AND](sequential)**
  - *Justification*: The caller's self-reported ability to open the door must be known *before* deciding whether to invoke the robot/human cooperation fallback — "that person can signal if they can open the door when the robot comes to it" implies this check happens first.

- **G2.3.2 -> G2.3.2.1, G2.3.2.2, G2.3.2.3 [AND] FALLBACK(…1,…2,…3)**
  - *Justification*: Combines the caller's self-service option with the general "cooperation with another robot or a human" clause; self-service is tried first (avoids any cooperation overhead), then robot cooperation, then human — least-cost-first escalation.

- **G2.4 -> G2.4.1, G2.4.2, G2.4.3 [AND] FALLBACK(…1,…2,…3)**
  - *Justification*: "the dish retrieve can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots or with the cooperation of robots and a human" — order preserved directly from the text, representing increasing assistance need.

## 7. Design Notes, Assumptions & Risks (for reviewer transparency)

1. **Inference flagged**: G1.4 (door access during delivery) is not explicitly stated in the text for the *delivery* thread — only for dish retrieval. I included it for physical completeness but recommend validating with stakeholders whether inpatient-room doors are normally open during meal service.
2. **Uncertainty handling**: G1.5.2.1 and G2.3.1 are modeled as *Query* goals rather than hard preconditions specifically because the text calls out uncertainty explicitly — this should drive a probabilistic/confidence-threshold implementation (e.g., re-querying at the door, or asking the inpatient directly if the record is inconclusive) rather than a brittle boolean check.
3. **Fallback ordering is a design choice, not fully dictated by the text.** I preserved the order the alternatives are listed in the mission description throughout (inpatient→companion→nurse→robot; unassisted→two-robot→robot+human; robot→human for doors) as the most defensible, text-grounded heuristic. A requirements review should confirm this ordering matches actual hospital operating preferences (e.g., some hospitals may prefer nurse-first over companion-first for medical reasons).
4. **Multi-robot emphasis**: Three goals (G1.4.1/G2.3.2.2, G1.5.2.2.4, G2.4.2) are genuinely multi-robot cooperative tasks requiring synchronized multi-agent planning/communication — these are the highest-risk/highest-engineering-effort nodes in the model and warrant early prototyping.
5. **Reused tasks (AT3, AT4, AT5)** demonstrate that the two mission threads (delivery, retrieval) share underlying navigation and door-cooperation capabilities — a strong argument for implementing these as reusable, parameterized robot skills rather than duplicating logic per mission thread.
