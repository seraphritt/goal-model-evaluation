As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital robotic assistance system. Below is my comprehensive reasoning, step-by-step reflection, and the resulting formal goal model decomposition, goal table, task table, summary, and logical relationships.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Reflections

1. **Mission Scope and Decomposition**:
* The mission encompasses two primary service workflows: **Food Delivery** and **Dirty Dish Retrieval**.
* These operate on an event-driven basis (kitchen delivery requests and room pickup calls).


2. **Handling Uncertainty and Alternative Delivery Modalities (`FALLBACK` vs. `OR`)**:
* Food delivery features two distinct methods: table placement (requiring a manipulation robot skill) and tray fetching (requiring cooperation).
* Tray fetching depends on patient capabilities and companion/nurse availability, which are subject to uncertainty and retrieved from inpatient records.
* Because tray retrieval relies on external human/robot actors and may fail if companions are unavailable, we model delivery choices using a **FALLBACK** relationship where table delivery or cooperative tray fetching serves as alternatives.


3. **Multi-Robot Cooperation and Coordination**:
* The system explicitly requires multi-robot and human-robot cooperation for several operations: tray fetching, dish retrieval, room door opening, and multi-robot task distribution.
* Tasks must capture the number of robots (1 or 2+) and operational locations (`hospital` or `kitchen`).


4. **Tracking, Verification, and Safety**:
* The robot handles multiple meals simultaneously. This necessitates state tracking (when and where meals were retrieved) and active error detection/alerting if a wrong meal is accessed.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: The main system mission G1 is composed of executing the food delivery service (G2) followed sequentially by dirty dish retrieval service (G3) during hospital operations.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3, G2.4 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Food delivery requires a sequential workflow: processing requests (G2.1), navigating to the room (G2.2), executing the meal delivery (G2.3), and tracking/verifying distribution (G2.4).


* **G2.3 -> G2.3.1, G2.3.2 [FALLBACK(G2.3.1, G2.3.2)][AND]**
* **Justification**: The robot attempts table placement (G2.3.1); if unavailable or unfeasible, it falls back to tray fetching with cooperation (G2.3.2).


* **G2.3.2 -> G2.3.2.1, G2.3.2.2 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Tray fetching requires first assessing patient/companion retrieval capabilities from records (G2.3.2.1) before executing the cooperative retrieval action (G2.3.2.2).


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2, G3.3 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Dish retrieval must follow a structured sequence: receiving the pickup call (G3.1), opening/accessing the room door (G3.2), and collecting the dirty dishes (G3.3).


* **G2.3.1 -> AT4 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Table delivery directly relies on task AT4 (special manipulation robot skill).


* **G2.3.2.2 -> AT3, AT6 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Cooperative tray retrieval requires door access (AT3) and cooperative tray unloading assistance (AT6).


* **G3.2 -> AT3 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Room door access management directly maps to task AT3 involving robot or human cooperation.


* **G3.3 -> AT9 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Dish collection from the room maps directly to task AT9 (unassisted, two-robot, or human-robot cooperation).
