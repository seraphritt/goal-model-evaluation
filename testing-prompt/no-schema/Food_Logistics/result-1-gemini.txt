## Mission Designer Reasoning & Step-by-Step Thinking

As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), decomposing a natural language mission description into a structured goal and task model requires rigorous analysis of functional dependencies, environmental uncertainties, multi-agent cooperation requirements, and hardware capabilities.

### Step 1: Functional Domain Decomposition

The mission description outlines two primary operational pillars within a hospital environment:

1. **Food Delivery Service**: Initiated by kitchen orders, traversing from the kitchen to an inpatient room, resolving door access, evaluating patient/companion/nurse capabilities under uncertainty, managing multi-meal tracking and error alerting, and executing handoff (either via a specialized manipulation robot placing food on a table, or via tray fetching requiring human or robot cooperation).
2. **Dish Collection Service**: Initiated by room call signals (including door-opening capability indicators), navigating to the room, cooperating for door opening, and collecting dirty dishes (unassisted, dual-robot, or robot-human cooperative).

### Step 2: Identification of Uncertainties and Complexities

* **Uncertainty in Retrieval Capability**: The ability of an inpatient, companion, or nurse to fetch meals from the robot tray depends on patient records and companion presence, which is subject to uncertainty. This necessitates a query/evaluation goal.
* **Cooperation Dynamics**: Door opening, tray fetching, and dish collection can involve heterogeneous agents (multiple robots, humans, nurses, companion visitors). This requires flexible fallback or alternative execution paths (`FALLBACK` or `OR` relations).
* **Multi-Meal Tracking**: The robot can carry multiple meals, requiring tracking of retrieval time/location and error alerting for mismatched meals.

### Step 3: Goal Model & Task Mapping Strategy

* **Achieve Goals** represent target states to be reached (e.g., successful navigation, meal delivery, dish collection).
* **Query Goals** represent information retrieval processes (e.g., querying patient records under uncertainty, receiving kitchen delivery requests).
* **Tasks** represent atomic or composite operational units executed by specific numbers of robots at defined locations.

## Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The overarching hospital service requires executing food delivery first, followed by or interleaved with dirty dishes collection as requested.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3, G2.4, G2.5 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Food delivery must follow a logical sequence: processing the order, navigating to the room, coordinating door access, executing the handover, and monitoring/verifying retrieval.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2, G3.3, G3.4 [AND] [(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Dish collection requires receiving a pickup call, navigating to the room, coordinating door access, and collecting the dishes.


* **G2.3 -> G2.3.1, G2.3.2 [FALLBACK(G2.3.1 AND G2.3.2) OR]**
* **Justification**: Opening the door can involve human cooperation or another robot cooperation; if one cooperation vector fails or is unavailable, the system falls back to the alternative cooperative agent.


* **G2.4 -> G2.4.1, G2.4.2 [FALLBACK(G2.4.1 AND G2.4.2) OR]**
* **Justification**: Delivery modalities are mutually exclusive based on patient capability: either direct table placement via robot manipulation or tray fetching with cooperation.


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2, AT3, AT4, AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Achieving the delivery goal requires fetching the order, navigating, querying records under uncertainty, opening doors, placing/fetching meals, and tracking/verifying retrieval.


* **G3 -> AT4, AT8, AT9 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Achieving dish collection requires coordinating door opening, receiving pickup calls, and executing multi-modal dish collection.
