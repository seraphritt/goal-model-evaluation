As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems, I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital food delivery and dish retrieval system. Below is the step-by-step reasoning, followed by the formal goal model tables, task tables, summary table, and logical relationships.

### Reasoning and Step-by-Step Thinking

1. **Mission Analysis & Operational Scope**:
* The mission takes place in a hospital environment involving service robots, kitchen staff, inpatients, companion visitors, and nurses.
* It covers two primary operational workflows: **Food Delivery** (from kitchen to room table or tray fetching) and **Dirty Dish Retrieval** (from room back to kitchen/service area).
* Both workflows require handling uncertainty (e.g., patient capability, companion availability), multi-robot and human-robot cooperation (e.g., door opening, tray fetching, dish pickup), and tracking/alerting (tracking meal retrieval, alerting wrong meal pickups).


2. **Goal Decomposition**:
* **G1**: Root mission goal to manage the complete lifecycle of food delivery and dish retrieval.
* **G2**: Order processing and handling kitchen requests.
* **G3**: Context assessment (querying inpatient records and room presence under uncertainty).
* **G4**: Food delivery execution, branching into direct table placement via manipulation skill or tray fetching via cooperation (fallback or OR relation).
* **G5**: Post-delivery tracking and safety validation (tracking time/location, alerting on wrong meal retrieval).
* **G6**: Dirty dish retrieval (handling call signals, door opening cooperation, and unassisted or cooperative dish pickup).


3. **Task Breakdown**:
* Tasks (`T1` to `T10`) map directly to the concrete actions required by robots and humans to fulfill the goals, specifying location, robot count, and relational dependencies.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G5, G6 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The root mission goal G1 requires sequential execution through order processing (G2), context assessment (G3), delivery execution (G4), validation/tracking (G5), and subsequent dish retrieval (G6).


* **G2 -> T1, T3 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Processing kitchen delivery requests requires first receiving the order (T1) and then loading the corresponding meals onto the robot in the kitchen (T3).


* **G3 -> T2 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Assessing context is directly achieved by querying inpatient records and companion presence (T2).


* **G4 -> G4.1, G4.2 [FALLBACK(G4.1 AND G4.2)][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: The system attempts direct table delivery (G4.1); if the inpatient is unable or unassisted, it falls back to cooperative tray fetching (G4.2).


* **G4 -> T4, T5, T6, T7 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Executing food delivery requires navigating to the room (T4), opening the door (T5), and then performing either table placement (T6) or tray fetching (T7).


* **G5 -> T8 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Monitoring and validating meal retrieval is executed via tracking and alerting tasks (T8).


* **G6 -> T9, T5, T10 [AND][(sequential)]**
* **Justification**: Retrieving dirty dishes requires receiving a pick-up call (T9), opening the room door cooperatively (T5), and collecting the dishes unassisted or cooperatively (T10).
