As a mission designer specialized in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), I have analyzed the natural language mission description for the hospital autonomous assistance service (food delivery and dirty dish retrieval). Below is the step-by-step reasoning followed by the formal goal model decomposition, task mapping, summary table, and logical relationships.

---

### Step-by-Step Reasoning & Mission Analysis

1. **Mission Decomposition Strategy**:
The primary mission is bifurcated into two main operational pillars derived from the description: **Food Delivery Service** and **Dirty Dish Retrieval Service**. Both pillars share common foundational capabilities (navigation, door opening, multi-robot/human cooperation) but have distinct workflows, triggers, and uncertainty handling mechanisms.
2. **Handling Uncertainty and Adaptability**:
* *Food Delivery*: The system cannot assume a uniform delivery method. It must query patient records and check companion presence under uncertainty to decide whether the robot can place food directly on the table (requiring manipulation skills) or if it must prompt tray fetching.
* *Tray Fetching*: Because tray fetching depends on external agents (inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot) whose availability is uncertain, a **FALLBACK** strategy is essential. If primary tray fetching cooperation fails or is unavailable, alternative assistive configurations or methods must be tried.
* *Multiple Meals & Verification*: The robot handles bulk transport. Tracking requires monitoring timestamps and locations, while safety constraints mandate alerting on incorrect meal retrievals.


3. **Cooperation and Door Opening**:
* Door opening and dish retrieval both involve collaborative modalities (unassisted, two robots, or robot-human cooperation). These require flexible task allocation depending on resource availability in the hospital environment.

### Logical Relationships

* **G1 -> G2, G3 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: The top-level service mission requires executing food delivery (G2) followed or interleaved with dirty dish retrieval (G3) sequentially as orders and pickups occur.


* **G2 -> G2.1, G2.2, G2.3, G2.4, G2.5 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Delivering food requires a sequential workflow: processing the request (G2.1), navigating to the room (G2.2), assessing patient retrieval capabilities (G2.3), executing the appropriate delivery method (G2.4), and tracking/verifying the retrieval (G2.5).


* **G2 -> AT1, AT2, AT3, AT4, AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Food delivery goals are operationalized by executing listening to requests (AT1), navigating (AT2), opening doors (AT3), querying records (AT4), placing food (AT5), assisting tray fetching (AT6), and tracking/verifying meals (AT7).


* **G2.4 -> G2.4.1, G2.4.2 [FALLBACK(G2.4.1 AND G2.4.2)]**
* **Justification**: The system attempts direct table placement first (or vice versa); if table manipulation or patient fetching conditions dictate a fallback, it switches to the alternative delivery path to ensure robust completion.


* **G3 -> G3.1, G3.2, G3.3 [AND][;]**
* **Justification**: Retrieving dirty dishes follows a sequential order: receiving the call and signal (G3.1), navigating to the room and opening the door (G3.2), and executing the dish collection (G3.3).


* **G3 -> AT2, AT3, AT8, AT9 [AND]**
* **Justification**: Dish retrieval goals are achieved through navigation (AT2), cooperative door opening (AT3), listening to room calls (AT8), and collecting dirty dishes (AT9).
