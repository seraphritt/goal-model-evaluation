### Logical Relationships (Tree Overview)

```
G1 -> G1.1, G1.2, G1.3 [AND][Sequential ';']
    G1.3 -> G1.3a, G1.3b [OR][Fallback(G1.3a,G1.3b)]
        G1.3a -> AT2, AT3, AT4, AT5, AT6 [AND][Sequential ';']
        G1.3b -> AT1 [AND][Single task]
    G1.1 -> AT11 [AND]
    G1.2 -> AT12 [AND]

G2 -> AT8, AT7, AT10 [AND][Sequential ';']
```

**Justifications for the logical structure**

- **Sequential (‘;’) in G1** – The robot must first obtain the order and the retrieval feasibility before deciding how to deliver; violating this order would lead to failed delivery attempts.  
- **Fallback (OR) in G1.3** – The system attempts tray delivery only if it is viable; otherwise, it safely falls back to table delivery.  
- **Sequential in G1.3a** – After depositing the meal on the tray, the robot must wait for the patient to retrieve it, notify them, record the event, and be ready to alert if the wrong meal is taken.  
- **Sequential in G2** – Door opening must happen before the robot can pick up dishes, and the dishes must be transported only after they have been collected.  
- **AND relations** – All child goals/tasks of a parent must be completed for the parent to succeed (e.g., the order must be known *and* the feasibility must be checked).  
- **Number of Robots** – Tasks that involve heavy lifting or safety constraints (dish pickup, door opening) allow a range of one to two robots to accommodate variability in room layouts or workload. Tasks that are purely information‑retrieval or simple manipulations need only a single robot.

This goal model captures the multi‑robot coordination, information uncertainty, and safety requirements described in the mission while providing a clear decomposition suitable for planning and execution.
