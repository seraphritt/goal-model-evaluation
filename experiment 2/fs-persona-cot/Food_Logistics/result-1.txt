## Logical Relationships

| **Parent Goal** | **Children** | **Relation** | **Runtime** | **Justification** |
|-----------------|--------------|--------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **G1** | G1.1, G1.2 | AND | `G1.1; G1.2` | Both food delivery and dish retrieval must be completed for the mission to succeed. |
| **G1.1** | G1.1.1, G1.1.2 | AND | `FALLBACK(G1.1.1, G1.1.2)` | Either direct table delivery or tray delivery must succeed; fallback ensures alternative is tried if the first fails. |
| **G1.1.1** | AT1 | AND | `-` | Direct delivery is a single action. |
| **G1.1.2** | G1.1.2.1, G1.1.2.3, G1.1.2.2 | AND | `G1.1.2.1; G1.1.2.3; G1.1.2.2` | Tray delivery requires (1) checking if patient can fetch, (2) opening the door, then (3) coordinating the fetch. |
| **G1.1.2.1** | AT3 | AND | `-` | Query must precede fetch coordination. |
| **G1.1.2.3** | AT10, AT11, AT12 | OR | `AT10; AT11; AT12` | Door can be opened by robot alone, robot‑robot, or robot‑human. |
| **G1.1.2.2** | AT4, AT5, AT6, AT7, AT8, AT9 | OR (first four) & AND (last two) | `AT4; AT5; AT6; AT7; AT8; AT9` | Fetch can be performed by any capable partner; monitoring tasks (detect wrong meal, track retrieval) must follow. |
| **G1.2** | G1.2.1, G1.2.2, G1.2.3 | AND | `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G1.2.1, G1.2.2), G1.2.3)` | One of the three retrieval methods must succeed. |
| **G1.2.1** | AT13, AT16 | AND | `AT13; AT16` | Unassisted retrieval must pick up dishes and then transport them. |
| **G1.2.2** | AT14, AT16 | AND | `AT14; AT16` | Two robots must cooperate to pick up dishes and then transport them. |
| **G1.2.3** | AT15, AT16 | AND | `AT15; AT16` | Robot must collaborate with a human to pick up dishes and then transport them. |

**Justification of Relations**

- **AND** relations are used when all sub‑goals or tasks are required to satisfy the parent goal (e.g., retrieving dishes and then transporting them).
- **OR** relations capture alternative ways to achieve a sub‑goal (e.g., any of the fetch partners can retrieve the food; any door‑opening method can be used).
- **Fallback** operators are employed when a primary method may fail (e.g., direct table delivery may fail due to patient’s inability to receive food; the robot then falls back to tray delivery). The fallback ensures a graceful degradation of the mission.

---

**Notes on Uncertainty**

The mission description notes uncertainty about the patient’s ability to fetch food and the availability of companions or nurses. These uncertainties are handled by the *Query Fetch Capability* goal (a query goal) and the *Coordinate Fetch* goal’s OR decomposition. The fallback strategy at the higher level ensures the system still completes the delivery if the chosen method fails.
