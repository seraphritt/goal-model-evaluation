# Goal Model for the Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Retrieval Multi-Robot System

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning & Design Rationale

Before building the tree, I went through the description multiple times to extract **intentions** (goals), **operational steps** (tasks), **alternatives** (OR), **joint requirements** (AND), **ordering constraints** (sequential/fallback), and **independence** (parallel). Key reflections:

1. **Two independent top-level responsibilities.** The text describes two things "the system should" do: deliver food, and retrieve dirty dishes. These are triggered by different events (a kitchen request vs. a call from someone in the room) and nothing in the text makes one depend on the other finishing first → **AND** decomposition at the root, with a **parallel (#)** runtime (they are possible to run concurrently across different orders/rooms, not obligatory to interleave).

2. **Zero-robot exclusion.** I deliberately did **not** create nodes for "kitchen sends a request" or "someone calls a robot" as standalone goals/tasks, since these are external triggering events, not actions performed by a robot. I kept only the robot-performed reaction to them (e.g., receiving/using the door-opening signal).

3. **OR vs. AND vs. Fallback — the central judgment call.**
   - *"delivered into a room table... **or** fetched from the robot's tray"* → explicit "or" → **OR** decomposition. No explicit statement of which is attempted first, so I used **parallel (#)** runtime (alternatives, no mandated order) rather than FALLBACK — the instructions are explicit that FALLBACK is reserved for cases where the text states an order/condition.
   - *"requires cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or **another robot**"* combined with *"Some inpatients could be unable... a companion visitor or nurse could **not be available**"* → this **does** give an explicit condition (inability/unavailability) and an explicit order of preference (inpatient → companion → nurse → robot, the last option clearly being the resort when humans aren't available). This matches the FALLBACK definition ("explicitly specifies order... related to a condition... IF-statement") almost exactly, so I modeled this as a **nested FALLBACK chain**, not a flat OR with parallel runtime.
   - *"can signal **if** they can open the door"* → an explicit IF-condition again → **FALLBACK** between human-opens and robot-opens.
   - *"unassisted, with cooperation of two robots, or with cooperation of robots and a human"* → explicit "or" list, **no** stated preference/condition → **OR** decomposition with **parallel** runtime.

4. **Why some goals needed a Query sub-goal.** The sentence *"The information about if the inpatient, companion or nurse can... retrieve the food... can be obtained based on the inpatient record... and the presence of a companion... This information is subject to uncertainty"* is a self-contained information-gathering requirement, independent of acting on it. I modeled it as a dedicated **Query Goal**, decomposed into two independent (parallel) information sources: the patient record and companion-presence detection. The explicit mention of *uncertainty* is also my justification for keeping the FALLBACK chain **even after** the query — the query informs the first guess, but the fallback still protects against the information being wrong.

5. **Tracking is scoped to tray retrieval only.** *"It should track when and where each meal was retrieved"* sits inside the paragraph about multiple meals/tray fetching and uses the verb "retrieved" (which the text reserves for the tray-fetch flow, as opposed to "delivered" for the table flow). I therefore attached tracking only under the tray-fetch branch, not under table delivery.

6. **Indication is scoped to person retrieval only.** *"in the case of a person retrieving the meal, the robot should indicate..."* explicitly excludes the robot-cooperation case, so I isolated a "person-based retrieval" sub-branch (indicate → cooperate) separate from the "robot cooperation" leaf.

7. **Kitchen as an explicit location.** The description says food comes "from the kitchen," so I added a loading task located at the kitchen (sequential, before the delivery-mode choice) — this is the only place "kitchen" is used as a Location, and it's explicitly grounded in the text.

8. **Minimum two children per decomposition.** Every AND/OR decomposition below has at least two children, as required. Where the text only implied a single operational step (e.g., table delivery), I placed that single task as a sibling leaf next to the other branch instead of forcing it into its own artificial sub-tree.

9. **Goal typing.** All composite intentions here are state-oriented (reach a condition: meal delivered, dishes retrieved, door open, information recorded) → **Achieve**. The only pure information-gathering node (availability determination from two sources) is a **Query** goal. I found no case where an action is executed "without necessarily requiring that the state... is reached" strongly enough to warrant a **Perform** goal at the Goal level — simple one-shot physical acts without further decomposition were instead modeled as **Tasks** (which don't carry a goal-type classification), consistent with the formalism.

## 6. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G10 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both meal delivery and dish retrieval are mandated ("The system should deliver food...", "Dirty dishes should be retrieved..."); they are triggered by independent events (kitchen request vs. a room call), so no sequencing is imposed.

- **G2 -> AT1, G3 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The meal must be loaded "from the kitchen" before the delivery mode can be executed — an inherent precedence.

- **G3 -> AT2, G4 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "The food can be delivered into a room table... or... fetched from the robot's tray" — explicit alternative with no stated order of attempt.

- **G4 -> G5, G6, G9 [AND] (sequential: (G5;G6);G9)**
  - **Justification**: Availability must be determined before retrieval is attempted, and the retrieval event must be logged after it happens — all three are required, in this necessary order.

- **G5 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The inpatient-record check and companion-presence detection are both explicitly cited as inputs to the availability determination, with no stated order between them.

- **G6 -> G7, AT9 [OR] FALLBACK(G7, AT9)**
  - **Justification**: The text frames robot-robot cooperation as the option used when people can't help ("Some inpatients could be unable...", "a companion visitor or nurse could not be available"), explicitly conditioning the choice — a fallback, not a free alternative.

- **G7 -> AT5, G8 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: "in the case of a person retrieving the meal the robot should indicate which meal" — indication must precede the person's retrieval action.

- **G8 -> AT6, AT7, AT8 [OR] FALLBACK(FALLBACK(AT6, AT7), AT8)**
  - **Justification**: The list order (inpatient, companion, nurse) together with the explicit statement that each may be unable/unavailable establishes a conditioned attempt order, matching the fallback pattern exactly.

- **G9 -> AT10, AT11 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "track when and where" names two independent, jointly required pieces of information with no stated order.

- **G10 -> G11, G13 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The robot needs room access (door open) before dishes can physically be collected — a necessary precedence implied by the text's own ordering ("As with delivery... Opening the room door...").

- **G11 -> AT12, G12 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The door-opening signal must be received before the robot can decide/execute how the door gets opened.

- **G12 -> AT13, AT14 [OR] FALLBACK(AT13, AT14)**
  - **Justification**: "that person can signal **if** they can open the door" is an explicit IF-condition: human cooperation is attempted when signaled possible; robot cooperation is the fallback otherwise.

- **G13 -> AT15, AT16, AT17 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots, or with the cooperation of robots and a human" is an explicit alternative list with no stated order or condition between the three modes.
