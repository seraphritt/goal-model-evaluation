# Mission Analysis: Hospital Food Delivery & Dish Retrieval Multi-Robot System

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the tree, I parsed the description into discrete intentions and asked three questions for each: **(1) Is this an intention (Goal) or a concrete operational step (Task)? (2) Does the text state an explicit order/condition (→ FALLBACK) or just complementary/alternative work (→ AND/OR with parallel or sequential)? (3) Is a robot actually involved (zero-robot rule)?**

1. **Two top-level responsibilities emerge**: "deliver food" and "retrieve dirty dishes." Nothing in the text orders one before the other, and they are triggered independently (kitchen request vs. room call) → they are **AND**-required (both are core system duties) but run **in parallel (#)**.

2. **"order-by-order basis in response to kitchen delivery requests"** tells me the request is received *before* delivery happens — an explicit temporal dependency, but not a failure-triggered alternative, so it's **sequential (;)**, not fallback.

3. **The fetch mechanism** ("cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot") combined with **"some inpatients could be unable... a companion or nurse could not be available"** is the clearest FALLBACK signal in the whole text: it explicitly states a *normal attempt* (human retrieval, inpatient first) and *conditional alternates* triggered by inability/unavailability, cascading down to a robot-robot cooperation as the last resort. Per the rules, FALLBACK forces an **AND** relation (both branches must exist in the plan; execution order is behavioral, not structural).

4. **The information about who can retrieve food** is explicitly described as being obtained ("based on the inpatient record... and the presence of a companion") and explicitly "subject to uncertainty" — this is a textbook **Query Goal** (it only retrieves knowledge, it doesn't act on the world).

5. **"the robot should indicate which meal should be retrieved"** is an action that either happens or doesn't, without a persistent target state being mandatory — this fits **Perform Goal** far better than Achieve.

6. **Tracking (log) and alerting on a wrong meal** are two distinct statements. Tracking always happens (no condition) → ordinary task. Alerting is explicitly conditioned ("if a wrong meal is retrieved") → **FALLBACK** between "confirm correct meal" and "alert."

7. **Dish retrieval modes** ("unassisted, two robots, or robots and a human") carry **no explicit priority/condition** — this is a plain **OR** with alternatives that could in principle be chosen independently (parallel, since nothing sequences them).

8. **Door opening tied to the call scenario** ("that person can signal if they can open the door") *is* conditionally phrased — the human's signal determines whether human-opens-door succeeds, and failure/inability falls back to robot-robot cooperation. This is again **FALLBACK**.

9. I kept every decomposition to a **minimum of two children** (per the AND/OR definition) — this forced me to either merge singleton steps directly into their parent's child list (e.g., "receive request" sits beside "deliver order" rather than being an orphan wrapper) or keep atomic Query/Perform goals as true leaves (since Goals, unlike Tasks, don't require Location/Robot count).

10. I verified the **zero-robot rule**: every task I kept has at least one robot physically involved (even "a person opens the door" requires the waiting/coordinating robot), so nothing purely human-only made it into the tree.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G12 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Food delivery and dish retrieval are both mandatory system duties with independent triggers (kitchen request vs. room call); nothing orders one before the other.

- **G2 -> AT1, G3 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Delivery is explicitly "in response to kitchen delivery requests," so the request must be received before the delivery act proceeds.

- **G3 -> AT2, G4, G10 [AND] (sequential: AT2 ; (G4 ; G10))**
  - **Justification**: Transport, fetch, and monitor/verify are all required and occur in that natural causal order (arrive, then hand off, then confirm).

- **G4 -> G5, G6 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The system must determine who is capable of retrieving before attempting the retrieval.

- **G5 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Based on the inpatient record... **and** the presence of a companion" — both checks are joined by "and" with no stated order, so they can be performed simultaneously.

- **G6 -> G7, AT8 [AND] FALLBACK(G7, AT8)**
  - **Justification**: Human retrieval is attempted first; only if no human can help (inpatient unable, companion/nurse unavailable) does the system fall back to another robot. FALLBACK forces AND per the modeling rule.

- **G7 -> G8, G9 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The meal must be indicated before a human can correctly retrieve it.

- **G9 -> AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND] FALLBACK(FALLBACK(AT5, AT6), AT7)**
  - **Justification**: The inpatient is the primary intended retriever; the text explicitly raises inability (inpatient), then unavailability (companion), then implicitly nurse, in that cascading order.

- **G10 -> AT9, G11 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Logging retrieval data and verifying correctness are two independent monitoring duties mentioned in separate, unordered sentences.

- **G11 -> AT10, AT11 [AND] FALLBACK(AT10, AT11)**
  - **Justification**: "The robot should alert **if** a wrong meal is retrieved" is an explicit condition-triggered alternative to the normal (correct) outcome.

- **G12 -> AT12, G13, G14 [AND] (sequential: AT12 ; (G13 ; G14))**
  - **Justification**: A call must be received, the door opened, and only then can dishes be retrieved — the same explicit causal chain as the delivery branch.

- **G13 -> AT13, AT14 [AND] FALLBACK(AT13, AT14)**
  - **Justification**: "That person can signal if they can open the door" conditions whether the human-open branch succeeds; robot-robot cooperation is the explicit fallback.

- **G14 -> AT15, AT16, AT17 [OR] (parallel: AT15 # (AT16 # AT17))**
  - **Justification**: The three retrieval modes are presented as plain alternatives with no condition or priority language (unlike the meal-fetch case) — any one suffices, with no implied order.

**Design notes worth flagging explicitly:**
- I distinguished **OR** (G14, plain alternatives) from **FALLBACK** (G6, G9, G11, G13) strictly based on whether the text used condition/exception language ("could be unable," "could not be available," "if a wrong meal," "can signal if they can") — only the latter justifies FALLBACK, per the rule that FALLBACK must not be inferred without explicit ordering/conditioning in the text.
- Every FALLBACK node was marked **AND** rather than **OR**, per the explicit rule that FALLBACK pairs are always AND-connected structurally, even though their *execution* is alternative-like.
- I avoided inventing locations: every task uses only "kitchen" or "inpatient room," the only locations named in the description.
