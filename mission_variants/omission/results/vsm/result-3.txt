# Multi-Robot Mission Goal Model — Hospital Vital Signs Monitoring

Below is my full step-by-step analysis, followed by the Goal Table, Task Table, Summary Table, and the Logical Relationships section.

---

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1 — Identify the root trigger.**
"Every two hours, all patient's vital signs should be checked" is the mission's overarching intentional objective → root goal **G1**, an *Achieve* goal (there is a target state: all patients checked within the cycle, scaled or escalated as needed).

**Step 2 — Find the explicit FALLBACK conditions (IF-statements / exception handling).**
I scanned the text for explicit "if X fails/is not the case, then Y" patterns, since the instructions require FALLBACK *only* when order is explicit:
- "If the patient is not available, the robot should come back in 5 minutes" → FALLBACK(check availability, wait & retry).
- "If there is not enough battery... recharge... and reassign" → FALLBACK(check battery, recharge+reassign).
- "The failure of collecting any necessary vital signs should trigger a set of questions" → FALLBACK(measure via device, ask assessment questions) — applied per condition-type.
- "Additional robots can be assigned to help in case of impossibility of performing the mission in the required time" → FALLBACK(normal round, add robots).
- "The responsible manager... must be alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission" → FALLBACK(add robots, alert manager) — nested fallback.

**Step 3 — Find explicit SEQUENTIAL orderings.**
- "approach the patient, announce the procedure..., and provide instructions" → explicit left-to-right order.
- "Before entering the room..." / "Once inside the room..." / "Once the patient is checked..." → explicit 3-phase temporal order (pre-entry; in-room; post-room).
- "All patients in high risk must be checked in the first 15 minutes" → explicit priority ordering relative to the rest of the round.
- "mark it as done and proceed to the next room" → explicit order (mark, then move).

**Step 4 — Find PARALLEL opportunities (no explicit order, concurrency plausible).**
- Availability check and battery check (no order stated between the two different concerns).
- Recharge and reassignment of tasks ("recharge... and the tasks need to be reassigned" — no order given).
- Sterilization and actuator disinfection (both exit-hygiene actions, no order given).
- The multiple vital-sign measurements within one condition (heart rate, BP, temperature, O2 — no order given).
- The three condition-based vital checks (infectious/post-surgical/diabetic) — a patient could have more than one condition, and nothing orders them relative to each other.
- The two identification methods (ask vs. scan) are true *alternatives* (OR), modeled with "#" since neither is mandated over the other and both could, in principle, be attempted without a fixed order.

**Step 5 — Avoid redundancy.**
"Visit every occupied room" and "high-risk patients checked within 15 minutes" describe the **same physical procedure** (pre-entry checks → in-room procedure → wrap-up) applied to two different room subsets (high-risk vs. remaining), not two different intentions. Rather than duplicating the whole subtree, I built it once (G4, G9, G17) and reused it as children of both **G3** (high-risk subset) and **G19** (remaining subset). Likewise, "Measure Heart Rate," "Measure Blood Oxygenation," and "Ask Assessment Questions" are reused across the infectious/post-surgical/diabetic branches instead of being re-created.

**Step 6 — AND vs OR.**
OR is reserved strictly for genuine alternatives (identifying the patient by asking OR scanning). Everywhere else — jointly required conditions, fallback pairs (which are always AND per the rules), and condition-dependent-but-jointly-required checks — AND is used, since a patient with multiple conditions (e.g., infectious **and** diabetic) needs **all** applicable checks satisfied, not just one.

**Step 7 — Goal types.**
*Query* was used only for "Identify Correct Patient" (pure information retrieval: *who* is the patient). *Perform* was used for atomic action-execution goals with no persistent target state (approach/announce/instruct, measuring via device, recharge+reassign, hygiene, dispatching robots, alerting). *Achieve* was used wherever a persistent target condition is being pursued (availability confirmed, battery sufficient, vitals obtained, rooms checked, mission completed).

**Step 8 — Zero-robot exclusion check.**
Every leaf task was checked against "is this physically/operationally executed by a robot?" All tasks, including coordination ones (reassigning tasks, dispatching robots, alerting the manager), are attributed to the robot(s) themselves (Number of Robots ≥ 1) since the mission text frames the whole system (including fleet coordination) as robot-executed; nothing was added that has zero robots attached.

**Step 9 — Locations.**
Only the two location terms explicitly present in the text were used: **Room** (hospital room — for all physically in-room/at-room actions) and **Hospital** (the only site-wide term, from "Hospital rooms...", generalized to cover site-wide coordination actions like reassignment, dispatch, and manager alerts, which the text does not tie to any specific room).

## 5. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G20, G21 [AND][FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G2,G20),G21)]**
  - **Justification**: All three are required for the mission's full behavior space (normal execution, scaling, escalation). FALLBACK is used because the text explicitly conditions G20 on time-infeasibility and G21 on total infeasibility, nested exactly as the text nests them (try normal round → else add robots → else alert manager).

- **G2 -> G3, G19 [AND][(sequential) G3;G19]**
  - **Justification**: Both room subsets must be fully checked (AND). Sequential because the text explicitly demands high-risk patients be checked "in the first 15 minutes," i.e., prioritized ahead of the rest of the round.

- **G3 -> G4, G9, G17 [AND][(sequential) G4;G9;G17]**
  - **Justification**: All three phases (prepare, perform, complete) are mandatory and explicitly ordered in the text ("Before entering..."; "Once inside..."; "Once the patient is checked..."). G3 reuses the same subtree as G19 to avoid duplicating the identical room-visiting intention.

- **G19 -> G4, G9, G17 [AND][(sequential) G4;G9;G17]**
  - **Justification**: Identical reasoning to G3; the same three-phase procedure applies to the remaining (non-high-risk) occupied rooms, with Location restricted to that subset at the task level.

- **G4 -> G5, G7 [AND][(parallel) G5#G7]**
  - **Justification**: Both the patient-side (availability/identity) and robot-side (battery) conditions must hold before entry (AND). No order is stated between them, and they concern independent actors, so parallel execution is plausible.

- **G5 -> G6, AT3, AT4 [AND][(sequential + fallback) G6;FALLBACK(AT3,AT4)]**
  - **Justification**: Identification must occur before "the patient's" availability can be checked (sequential, explicit via "So the robot has to identify the correct patient").
