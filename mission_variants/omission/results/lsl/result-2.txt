## Step 1 – Extracting all actions from the description

Before building the tree, I went sentence by sentence and tagged each action with **who performs it**, since the rule "goals/tasks not performed by any robot must not appear in the model" is the single biggest filter here.

| 10 | "The time ... should be tracked ... according to laboratory protocols" | System/robot (tied to the transporting robot's trip) | ✅ Yes | → Task (Track time) |

**Key modeling consequence:** because fragment #6 (human picking) has zero robots, I did **not** create an OR branch "Human picks OR Robot processes" — the human leaf is simply never instantiated, leaving only the robotic-arm processing chain (#8) in the tree. This matches the instruction that OR-decomposition requires at least two *robot-eligible* alternatives; here there is only one.

I also treated #9 and #10 as robotic/system responsibilities rather than discarding them, since in a multi-robot system the pick-location/timestamp data and elapsed-time data are natural by-products that the robots (arm + transporter) already possess (they scan the barcode and know their own position/clock) — discarding them would remove two laboratory-protocol requirements that are explicitly in scope for the system.

No sentence contains an explicit "if X fails, then Y" / exception clause, so **no FALLBACK relation is used anywhere** in this model — only AND/OR decomposition plus Sequential (`;`) and Parallel (`#`) runtime operators are needed.

## Step 2 – Building the hierarchy (depth-first)

Reasoning for structure:
- **G1 (root)** must cover the whole mission: transport → process → ensure compliance. These three are **jointly necessary** → AND.
- Transport physically must **precede** processing (can't scan/sort/load a sample that hasn't arrived) → Sequential.
- Compliance logging/time-tracking happens **throughout** the mission (time starts ticking at collection, the pick-log is written during processing) → runs in **Parallel** to the transport→process sequence.
- Inside "Process Sample at Laboratory," the text gives an explicit order ("scans ..., sorts, and loads") and logically the drawer must be opened before anything can be scanned → **Sequential**, AND decomposition (every step is mandatory, none is optional).
- "Log pick info" and "Track delivery time" are independent record-keeping concerns with no stated order between them → **Parallel**, AND decomposition (both are required for protocol assurance).

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> AT1, G2, G3 [AND] (AT1;G2)#G3**
  - **Justification**: All three branches are explicitly required by the mission text (transport, processing, and compliance assurance) — AND. Transport must physically complete before processing can start (Sequential), while compliance tracking (timing starts at collection, logging occurs during processing) spans the whole mission and so runs concurrently with the transport→process chain (Parallel). Parentheses are required since two different operators (`;` and `#`) are combined.

- **G2 -> AT2, AT3, AT4, AT5 [AND] (AT2;AT3;AT4;AT5)**
  - **Justification**: The description lists these as one unbroken, mandatory chain ("scans the barcode ..., sorts, and loads"), with drawer-opening as the necessary precondition for all of them. All four are required (AND) and must occur in the stated order (Sequential).

- **G3 -> G4, G5 [AND] (G4#G5)**
  - **Justification**: Logging the pick event and tracking delivery time are two separate protocol obligations stated in separate sentences, with no dependency or ordering between them — both mandatory (AND), executed independently/concurrently (Parallel).

- **G4 -> AT6 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: A single task fully realizes this goal — securely recording where/when the sample was picked is the entirety of what the text asks for, so the task's success is both necessary and sufficient for the goal.

- **G5 -> AT7 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Likewise, one task (timestamping collection and delivery) is both necessary and sufficient to satisfy the "track the time" requirement, so it is not split further.

### Notes on exclusions (traceability)
For completeness/auditability, these description fragments were intentionally **left out of the goal model** because they are performed exclusively by humans (zero robots), per the modeling rule:
- Nurse requesting a delivery.
- Barcode identification applied before collection (actor unspecified, not attributed to a robot).
- Nurse entering sample data into the track system.
- Laboratory personnel manually picking the sample (the human alternative to the robotic-arm processing chain in G2).

If any of these actions is later confirmed to involve a robot (e.g., an automated barcode printer, or a robotic hand-off station), they could be reintroduced as tasks/goals and would most likely attach as an **OR alternative** to G2 (human path vs. robot path) or as a precursor task to AT1.
