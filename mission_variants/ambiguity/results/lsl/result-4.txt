# Mission Analysis: Lab Sample Transport & Processing

## Step 1 — Identifying the Agents (Robots vs. Humans)

Before decomposing anything, I need to separate human-only actions from robot-performed ones, since the rule states that **goals/tasks with zero robot involvement must not appear in the model**.

| Actor | Robot? | Actions in text |
|---|---|---|
| Nurse | ❌ Human | Requests delivery, inserts data in track system, (implicitly) applies barcode, collects sample |
| Technician | ❌ Human | Positions transport robot beside robotic arm |
| **Transport robot** | ✅ Robot | Carries the locked drawer from patient floor to laboratory |
| **Robotic arm** | ✅ Robot | Opens drawer, unloads/picks up sample, scans barcode, sorts, loads into analysis machine |

**Consequence:** The nurse's request, the barcode assignment, the data entry into the track system, and the technician's positioning action are all excluded as standalone nodes — they are zero-robot. They remain only as *contextual triggers/preconditions* explained in justifications, not as formal nodes. Everything that remains in the model is anchored to either the **transport robot** or the **robotic arm**.

## Step 2 — Avoiding Redundancy

The text says the robotic arm "can unload the sample" and then, in the next sentence, "picks up samples." These describe the **same physical event** (taking the sample out of the drawer), so per the anti-redundancy rule I merge them into a single task (`AT3`) rather than creating two goals/tasks with identical intention.

Similarly, the authorization clause ("only authorized personnel **or** specific robots in specific locations...") is not modeled as an OR-decomposition in my tree — it's a permission *list* of alternative actors, not an alternative in the mission's goal structure. Since only the robot-performed branch qualifies for inclusion, it is folded into the justification of the "open drawer" task rather than creating a spurious OR branch.

## Step 3 — Sequence vs. Parallel vs. Fallback

- No conditional/exception language ("if X fails, then Y") appears anywhere in the description → **no FALLBACK relation is used anywhere in this model.**
- Where the text narrates one action strictly after another with physical/logical necessity (transport before processing; open before removing; scan→sort→load), I use **sequential (`;`)**.
- Where two requirements co-occur without a stated order (e.g., time-tracking alongside physical transport; logging alongside continued processing), I use **parallel (`#`)**.

## Step 4 — Resulting Tree (overview)

```
G1 Deliver and Process Lab Sample (Achieve) [AND, G2;G4]
├── G2 Transport Sample to Laboratory (Achieve) [AND, AT1#G3]
│   ├── AT1 Transport Locked Drawer (Patient Floor → Laboratory)
│   └── G3 Track Sample Transport Time (Query) [leaf]
└── G4 Process Sample at Laboratory (Achieve) [AND, G5;(G6#(AT4;AT5;AT6))]
    ├── G5 Securely Access and Unload Sample from Drawer (Achieve) [AND, AT2;AT3]
    │   ├── AT2 Open Locked Drawer
    │   └── AT3 Pick Up Sample from Drawer
    ├── G6 Log Sample Pick Information Securely (Achieve) [leaf]
    ├── AT4 Scan Sample Barcode
    ├── AT5 Sort Sample
    └── AT6 Load Sample into Analysis Machine Entry-Module
```

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G4 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Both transport and processing are mandatory (removing either breaks the mission), and the description narrates transport first ("transported from patient floors to the laboratory") before processing ("In the laboratory…"). Physically, the sample must arrive before it can be handled, so sequential execution is required rather than parallel or fallback (no failure/alternative condition is stated).

- **G2 -> AT1, G3 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The drawer must be physically transported **and** its transit time must be tracked, and the text gives no order between these — the clock runs *alongside* the physical movement, from collection to delivery, with no dependency forcing one to finish before the other starts.

- **G4 -> G5, G6, AT4, AT5, AT6 [AND] (sequential + parallel): `G5;(G6#(AT4;AT5;AT6))`**
  - **Justification**: Access/unload (G5) must happen before anything else in this branch, since the sample can't be scanned, sorted, or logged before it exists outside the drawer — hence the leading `;`. Once unloaded, logging the pick's location/time (G6) can proceed independently of, and concurrently with, the scan→sort→load chain, since the text presents logging as a separate requirement with no stated blocking relationship to the rest of the pipeline — hence `G6#(...)`.

- **G5 -> AT2, AT3 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The drawer must be opened before the sample can be removed from it — an explicit, physically necessary order, matching the sequential operator's definition (not fallback, since there's no "if opening fails" alternative stated).

- **G4 -> AT4, AT5, AT6 [AND] (sequential): `AT4;AT5;AT6`**
  - **Justification**: The description lists these as one continuous, ordered action by the robotic arm ("picks up … scans … sorts, and loads …"). This is a plain narrated sequence of steps with no branching or parallel opportunity mentioned, so sequential is the correct (and only correct) operator.

### Notes on exclusions (for transparency)
The following mission elements were deliberately **not** modeled as goals/tasks, since they involve zero robots: the nurse's delivery request, barcode assignment prior to collection, the nurse's data entry into the track system, and the technician manually positioning the transport robot. These are preserved only as narrative context that motivates G2 (the request triggers transport) and G5 (positioning is the real-world precondition that lets the robotic arm reach the drawer) — they are not independently justifiable nodes under the multi-robot modeling rule.
