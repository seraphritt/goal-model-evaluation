# Mission Analysis: Lab Sample Transport Goal Model

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1 — Identify all actors and actions in the description.**
I listed every action mentioned: (1) nurse requests delivery, (2) sample barcoded before collection, (3) nurse inserts data into track system, (4) sample transported in a locked drawer, (5) sample picked by robotic arm *or* lab personnel, (6) only "suitable" personnel/robots may open the locked drawer, (7) robotic arm picks → scans → sorts → loads into the analysis machine's entry-module, (8) pick location/time logged securely, (9) elapsed collection-to-delivery time tracked against protocol.

**Step 2 — Apply the "zero-robot" exclusion rule.**
Actions (1), (2) and (3) are performed exclusively by the **nurse** (a human, not a robot), so per the rule *"goals and tasks that are not performed by any robot must not be on the goal model"* they are **excluded as nodes** — though they remain important *triggers/preconditions* for the mission (I reference them in justifications only). Likewise, in action (5) "robotic arm **or** laboratory personnel" and in (6) "personnel **or** robots," the personnel branch is zero-robot and is dropped; only the robot branch survives. Because the non-robot alternative disappears, these are **not** modeled as OR-decompositions (an OR needs ≥2 real children) — they collapse into direct robot tasks.

**Step 3 — Avoid redundancy.**
The nurse's initial barcode *assignment* (action 2) and the robotic arm's barcode *scanning* (action 7) are different intentions (assign ID vs. read ID for sorting) — no duplication, and only the robot-performed one is modeled.

**Step 4 — Disambiguate "picked" vs. "collection."**
The text uses two different words for two different events: "picked" appears directly after the robotic-arm action list (pick/scan/sort/load) → it refers to the **arm's pick at the laboratory**. "Collection" appears in the final sentence, paired with "laboratory delivery," as the two temporal endpoints of the whole mission → it refers to the **initial gathering at the patient floor**. This separation lets me assign the *logging* task to the lab pick event and the *tracking* task to the full floor→lab time span, without merging two distinct intentions into one node.

**Step 5 — Group actions into goals (AND/OR).**
Three robot-relevant clusters emerged: (a) physically moving the locked drawer (floor→lab), (b) processing the sample once at the lab (access control + pick/scan/sort/load), (c) securing/monitoring the whole handling process (logging + time-tracking). All three are jointly necessary for mission success → **AND**. Within (b), opening the drawer must physically precede picking, and pick/scan/sort/load are explicitly listed in that order → **AND**, all sequential. Within (c), logging a pick event and tracking total elapsed time are independent, unordered requirements → **AND**, parallel.

**Step 6 — Runtime annotations.**
No conditional/failure language ("if," "in case," "otherwise") appears anywhere in the description, so **FALLBACK is never used**. Where the text gives an explicit action order (the arm's four actions) or a clear logical precedence (open-before-pick, transport-before-processing), I used **sequential (;)**. Where two requirements are independent and can co-occur (logging vs. tracking; these two vs. the overall delivery), I used **parallel (#)**.

**Step 7 — Goal types.**
All three goals describe a **state to be reached** through planning/execution (a delivered sample, a processed sample, a logged/verified record) rather than a single atomic action (Perform) or a pure information retrieval with no resulting world change (Query) — so all three are classified as **Achieve**, each with an explicit target condition.

**Step 8 — Tasks.**
Each task is a concrete, assignable, single-robot operation with a location drawn verbatim from the text ("patient floor(s)," "laboratory") and an explicit robot count inferred from the singular references ("a robotic arm," "the robot") — one robot per task, consistent with a multi-robot *system* composed of specialized, single-unit transport and manipulation robots.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> AT1, G2, G3 [AND] [(AT1;G2)#G3]**
  - **Justification**: Transporting the drawer, processing the sample, and tracking/logging its handling are all mandatory ("should be transported," "should be logged," "should be tracked") — none substitutes for another, so AND. The sample must physically arrive (AT1) before it can be processed (G2), so these two are sequential; G3's logging/tracking spans the entire collection-to-delivery window and therefore runs in parallel with the (AT1;G2) sequence rather than strictly after it.

- **G2 -> AT2, AT3, AT4, AT5, AT6 [AND] [sequential: AT2;AT3;AT4;AT5;AT6]**
  - **Justification**: The description explicitly enumerates "picks up... scans... sorts, and loads" as one continuous, ordered chain performed by the robotic arm, each step necessary for the next (you cannot scan a sample not yet picked, etc.). The drawer must be opened (AT2) before anything inside it can be picked (AT3), so it is placed first in the sequence. All steps are required (AND), none optional.

- **G3 -> AT7, AT8 [AND] [parallel: AT7#AT8]**
  - **Justification**: Logging the pick event and tracking overall elapsed delivery time are two separate obligations stated independently in the text, with no sequencing or dependency between them — both can be computed/recorded concurrently, so AND with a parallel (#) runtime.

### Note on excluded (zero-robot) elements
For transparency: the nurse's request for delivery, the pre-collection barcode assignment, and the nurse's data entry into the track system are all human-only actions and were deliberately **omitted** as nodes per the zero-robot rule — they function only as real-world *triggers* that precede G1's execution, not as goal-model elements. Similarly, "laboratory personnel" as an alternative to the robotic arm, and "personnel" as an alternative to a suitable robot for opening the drawer, were dropped rather than modeled as OR-branches, since an OR requires at least one further valid (robot) alternative beyond the one already chosen — here only the robot-side branch qualifies for inclusion.
