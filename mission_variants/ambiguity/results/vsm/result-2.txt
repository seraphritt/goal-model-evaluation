# Mission Analysis — Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the formal model, here is how I read the mission description and the decisions that follow from it.

**1. Finding the root objective.** The mission is triggered on a cycle ("every two hours") and has two explicit, independent success conditions stated in the text: (a) *all occupied rooms get visited/checked*, and (b) *high-risk patients specifically get checked within the first 15 minutes*. Since both are stated as separate obligations with no dependency between them, I made them two AND-connected children of a root goal, related in **parallel** (no order or failure condition links them).

**2. Finding the escalation chains (FALLBACK).** Three distinct places in the text give an explicit "normal path → IF it fails → alternative" structure:
   - Room un-availability → "come back after a short period."
   - Battery insufficiency → "recharge … tasks reassigned."
   - Vital-sign collection failure → "set of questions" → **if that also fails** (non-response) → "alert … nurse/doctor."
   - Mission-level: normal robot assignment → "additional robots" → **if that also fails** → "alert … manager."
   
   The last two are *three-stage* escalations, which per the rules must be modeled as **nested FALLBACK** (`FALLBACK(FALLBACK(exp1,exp2),exp3)`), exactly as the two-step examples in the prompt illustrate.

**3. Sequential vs. parallel.** Wherever the text uses explicit temporal markers ("before entering," "once inside," "once checked... then proceed") I used **sequential**. Wherever two things are joined by "and" with no stated order and no failure condition (e.g., checking availability *and* battery; the four infectious-disease vitals; asking *vs.* scanning), I used **parallel**, since the definition of parallel is "possibility, not obligation" and nothing forces an order.

**4. OR vs AND.** The three patient-condition categories (infectious, post-surgical, diabetic) are **OR**-related: a patient may belong to one, several, or none of these categories, so these are alternative condition-driven branches, not a mandatory joint requirement for every patient. Everything explicitly joined by "and" (e.g., the 4 infectious vitals, pre-entry checks) is **AND**.

**5. Avoiding redundancy.** "Heart rate" and "blood oxygenation" are required by *both* the infectious-disease and post-surgical categories. Rather than duplicating these as two separate tasks, I reuse the same task nodes (AT8, AT11) under both parent goals.

**6. Zero-robot filtering.** "Assign additional robots" and "alert the manager" are kept in the model under the assumption that the detecting robot itself executes the reassignment call / sends the communication (a robot-performed action), which satisfies the "must be performed by a robot" constraint — this assumption is stated explicitly in the relevant justifications.

**7. Goal typing.** Following the worked example in the prompt ("Check Room Patients" = Achieve even though it involves information gathering), all "check X" goals that represent a full physical/procedural effort are typed **Achieve**. Goals that are a single binary status lookup before deciding what to do next ("check if patient is available," "check if battery is sufficient," "identify correct patient") are typed **Query**, since their sole purpose is retrieving a fact, not changing world state. Goals that are single, unconditional actions with no target state ("ask questions," "assign robots," "alert manager," "return later") are typed **Perform**.

**8. Locations.** Only "room" and "hospital" are cited in the text. I never invented a charging-station or other unseen location — generic/fleet-level actions are tagged **Hospital**, room-bound actions are tagged **Room**, and the inter-room travel task is tagged **Rooms** (the occupied-room collection).
