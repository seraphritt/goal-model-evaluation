# Goal Model for the Hospital Vital‑Signs‑Checking Mission

## 1. Step‑by‑Step Reasoning and Reflections

Before building the tables, here is the reasoning path I followed, including the trade‑offs I had to resolve.

**Step 1 — Find the mission‑level goal and its "safety net."**
The description opens with the periodic trigger ("every two hours… checked") and closes with an escalation chain for resourcing ("additional robots can be assigned… manager alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission"). The escalation chain has an **explicit order** (try with current robots → add robots → alert manager only if that *still* fails), which is the textbook definition of **FALLBACK**, and it must be nested because FALLBACK is binary (`FALLBACK(FALLBACK(exp1,exp2),exp3)`). This becomes the top of the tree (G1), with its two "escape hatches" built as tasks rather than goals, since assigning robots and alerting a manager are atomic coordination acts, not objectives that further decompose.

**Step 2 — Separate "visiting" from "checking".**
"The assigned robot should visit every occupied room" is a distinct intention (coverage of the hospital) from the detailed in‑room procedure. I kept these as two goals (G2 "Visit Occupied Rooms" and G3 "Check Patient in Room") to avoid collapsing two different intentions into one, per the non‑redundancy rule.

**Step 3 — Handle the high‑risk 15‑minute rule without duplicating the whole sub‑tree.**
"All patients in high risk must be checked in the first 15 minutes" does **not** introduce a new *intention* ("check the patient") — it only constrains *when/in what order* the already‑defined checking procedure (G3) runs. Re‑creating a full parallel copy of G3 just for high‑risk rooms would violate "each distinct intention should be represented by only one goal." Instead, I modeled the ordering constraint as a lightweight scheduling **task** (AT1, "Prioritize High‑Risk Patient Rooms") that runs **sequentially before** the single, reusable G3 process. This keeps one intention ("check patient in room") reusable for every room, while still explicitly capturing the timing requirement.

**Step 4 — Pre‑entry checks.**
"Check if the patient is available… **and** if there is enough battery" — joined by *and*, no stated order → **parallel (#)**. Each check has its own explicit failure‑triggered alternative ("if not available… if not enough battery…") → two separate **FALLBACK** constructs, combined in parallel.

**Step 5 — Patient identification.**
"Either by asking or by scanning the barcode" is an explicit **OR** particle ("either…or") describing true alternative methods, not a timing/failure relation → **OR decomposition**, no stated order between the two options → parallel runtime.

**Step 6 — In‑room introduction.**
"Approach… announce… provide instructions" is listed as a plain narrative sequence with no condition or failure branching → **sequential (;)**, **AND** (all three are required), classified as **Perform** goals/tasks since they are actions executed, not states queried or achieved independently.

**Step 7 — Vital signs collection.**
The three patient categories (infectious, post‑surgical, diabetic) each require a different, specific set of signs. Since a mission‑level (not per‑patient) model cannot assume a single patient belongs to only one category, I treated the three category‑checklists as **OR** children (the fulfillment of whichever branch(es) apply is sufficient for the parent), while the signs *within* a category are conjunctive → **AND**. This also lets me reuse the physical measurement tasks (heart‑rate, oxygenation) across categories instead of duplicating them as new intentions.

**Step 8 — Failure handling.**
"Failure of collecting… triggers questions" → FALLBACK #1. "In case of non‑response… alert" → FALLBACK #2, nested on top of #1, exactly matching the `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(exp1,exp2),exp3)` pattern given in the instructions.

**Step 9 — Hygiene rules.**
Disinfecting actuators is triggered by "any duties with patient contact" — since vital‑sign collection is physical contact, this follows it **sequentially**, every time. Sterilization is triggered specifically "when a robot comes out of a room" with an infectious‑disease patient — this is a mandatory, conditionally‑applicable step placed **sequentially** between marking the patient checked and moving to the next room (I treat the infectious‑disease condition as a guard noted in the justification, consistent with how the vitals branches are guarded).

**Step 10 — Zero‑robot / redundancy audit.**
Every leaf task in the final tree is executed by at least one robot (no "zero‑robot" tasks were introduced, so none were pruned). I checked every goal pair for duplicated intent (e.g., G2 vs G3, G5 vs G6, G12/G13/G14) — each represents a genuinely distinct objective, so none were merged beyond what Step 3 already resolved.

**Step 11 — Goal typing.**
I used **Achieve** whenever the robot must reach/maintain a world state that can succeed or fail independently of a single action (availability, battery, identification, vital‑sign sets, overall mission/room completion); **Perform** whenever the "goal" is simply the execution of an action sequence with no independent target state to monitor (introduction, recharge+reassign); **Query** whenever the sole purpose is information retrieval with no action requirement to reach a world state (assessment questions — the goal succeeds once the information, whatever it is, has been obtained).

## 5. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, AT23, AT24 [AND] [FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G2, AT23), AT24)]**
  - **Justification**: The mission is attempted normally (G2); if it cannot be completed in time, additional robots are assigned (AT23); only if *no* robot combination works is the manager alerted (AT24). This exact two‑step escalation matches the nested‑FALLBACK example, and FALLBACK pairs are always AND‑related.

- **G2 -> AT1, G3 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The visiting order must first be set so high‑risk rooms are handled within 15 minutes (AT1), then the (reusable) checking procedure (G3) is applied room by room; both are required, in this order.

- **G3 -> G4, G8, G9, G10, AT19, G16 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The description narrates these phases of a room visit in a fixed order — readiness, identification, introduction, vitals, hygiene, close‑out — and all are mandatory for a valid room check.

- **G4 -> G5, G6 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "check if the patient is available **and** if there is enough battery" — a conjunction with no stated precedence, so both are necessary but un‑ordered.

- **G5 -> AT2, AT3 [AND] [FALLBACK(AT2, AT3)]**
  - **Justification**: Checking availability is attempted first; only on failure ("if the patient is not available") does the reschedule action occur.

- **G6 -> AT4, G7 [AND] [FALLBACK(AT4, G7)]**
  - **Justification**: Checking the battery is attempted first; only on failure ("if there is not enough battery") does recharge/reassignment occur.

- **G7 -> AT5, AT6 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "the robot should recharge **and** the tasks need to be reassigned" — conjunction, no stated order; recharging concerns this robot while reassignment concerns other robots, making them naturally independent/parallel.

- **G8 -> AT7, AT8 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "either by asking or by scanning" is an explicit alternative (OR) particle; either path suffices to identify the patient, and no order is prescribed between the two options.

- **G9 -> AT9, AT10, AT11 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: All three communicative actions are required, and the text lists them in the fixed order "approach… announce… provide instructions."

- **G10 -> G11, G15, AT18 [AND] [FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G11, G15), AT18)]**
  - **Justification**: Direct vitals collection is tried first; on failure of any sign, assessment questions are asked; if the patient still does not respond, an alert is sent — a direct instance of the nested‑FALLBACK pattern.

- **G11 -> G12, G13, G14 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Which vitals are required depends on the patient's medical condition(s); these are condition‑triggered alternative checklists rather than universally mandatory siblings, and multiple can be relevant simultaneously with no prescribed order, so OR with parallel runtime best captures "fulfilled by whichever branch(es) apply."

- **G12 -> AT12, AT13, AT14, AT15 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "must be checked for heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, and blood oxygenation" is a conjunctive (all‑required) list with no stated order among the four measurements.

- **G13 -> AT12, AT15 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "must be checked for heart rate and blood oxygenation" — both required, no order; tasks reused from G12 since they are physically the same action.

- **G14 -> AT16 [AND, trivial]**
  - **Justification**: A single required measurement ("checked for glucose levels") — the decomposition is degenerate (one child), so no combinatory operator is needed.

- **G15 -> AT17 [AND, trivial]**
  - **Justification**: A single required action (asking the status questions) fulfills this query goal.

- **G16 -> AT20, AT21, AT22 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: "mark it as done and proceed to the next room," combined with "when a robot comes out of a room… sterilization must be performed," gives an explicit close‑out order: mark done → sterilize (if applicable) → proceed.

---

A couple of modeling notes worth flagging explicitly: (1) I treated the infectious‑disease/post‑surgical/diabetes vitals branches and the sterilization step as *condition‑guarded* members of an AND/OR structure rather than inventing a fourth operator for "conditional applicability," since the framework only defines AND, OR, and FALLBACK — the guard is captured in each node's text/justification instead. (2) I did not have access to any external source for this analysis — it's based solely on the mission text you supplied, so there are no citations to double‑check here.
