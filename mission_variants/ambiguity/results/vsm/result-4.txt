# Goal Model Design — Multi‑Robot Hospital Vital‑Signs Mission

Below is my full reasoning process followed by the complete goal model (goals, tasks, summary, and logical relationships), as requested.

---

## 1. Step‑by‑Step Reasoning

**Step 1 — Identify the mission trigger and top‑level intention.**
The description opens with a periodic trigger ("every two hours... all patient's vital signs should be checked") and a consequence ("the assigned robot should visit every occupied room"). This gives a single root intention: *reach a state where every patient has been checked*. Since this is a state to be brought about through planning/acting (not a simple action, not a query), the root is an **Achieve Goal**.

**Step 2 — Separate the "normal flow" from the "capacity/escalation" concern.**
Two paragraphs are structurally different from the room-visiting procedure: "Additional robots can be assigned... manager alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission." This is explicitly ordered (first try with assigned robot(s), then add robots, then escalate to a human) → a textbook nested **FALLBACK**. I modeled it as a sibling branch of the room-visiting goal, not nested inside it, because it is a mission-level capacity concern, not a per-room concern.

**Step 3 — Decompose "visit every occupied room" into its operational phases.**
Reading the room procedure narrative in order, I identified six AND-required phases: identify the patient → pre-entry checks → engage/inform the patient → collect vital signs → post-visit hygiene → finalize/move on. I used **sequential (;)** because the text narrates them as an ordered procedure ("Before entering... Once inside... Once the patient is checked...").

*Assumption flagged*: The text places "the robot has to identify the correct patient" right after explaining why (multiple beds) in the same sentence block as the pre‑entry checks. I inferred identification must happen **before** the availability check, since you cannot check "if the patient is available" without first knowing *which* patient you are addressing. I call this out explicitly as an inference, not an explicit statement.

**Step 4 — Model each IF-statement as FALLBACK, not as a condition/branch.**
Per the rules, FALLBACK is mandatory wherever the text gives a normal action and an explicit alternative triggered by failure/condition:
- "If the patient is not available → come back later" → FALLBACK.
- "If not enough battery → recharge + reassign" → FALLBACK.
- "Failure of collecting vital signs → ask questions" → FALLBACK.
- "In case of non-response → alert nurse/doctor" → FALLBACK, nested inside the previous one.
- "Impossibility of performing mission in time → additional robots" → FALLBACK.
- "No combination of robots can complete mission → alert manager" → FALLBACK, nested.

Everywhere a FALLBACK appears, I forced an **AND** decomposition on its two branches, per the given rule, even though only one branch may actually execute at runtime — this is a decomposition-type convention, not an execution guarantee.

**Step 5 — Use OR only for genuine alternatives (not for execution order).**
Two places use real "either/or" alternatives:
- "either by asking or by scanning the patient's barcode" → OR (two interchangeable methods, no stated priority → I used `#` for the runtime, since no order is given and FALLBACK cannot be assumed without an explicit attempt-order).
- The three condition-based vital-sign requirement sets (infectious / post-surgical / diabetic) are **not mutually exclusive in real life** (a patient could be diabetic *and* post-surgical), so fulfilling any applicable branch is sufficient — I modeled this as OR with parallel runtime (`#`), reflecting "possible simultaneity, no obligation."

**Step 6 — Avoid mixing AND and OR under one parent.**
Because "Collect Vital Signs" needed both (a) an OR between condition-specific measurement sets, and (b) an AND for the fallback-to-questions-to-alert chain, I could not flatten these into one goal (a single parent cannot have mixed AND/OR children). I introduced an intermediate goal ("Measure Condition-Specific Vital Signs") purely to isolate the OR-only decomposition from the AND-only fallback chain above it. The same reasoning applies to "Ensure Sufficient Battery", where I kept the query + recharge + reassignment as three AND-siblings (rather than inventing an unnecessary extra goal), since recharge and reassignment don't need to be isolated from a different decomposition type at that level.

**Step 7 — Avoid redundant intentions.**
"Patients in high risk must be checked in the first 15 minutes" describes the *same* intention as "visit every occupied room" (checking a patient), just with a scheduling priority constraint. Creating a separate goal for it would duplicate the "visit/check a room" intention. I therefore treat it as a **scheduling policy annotation** on the "Visit Occupied Rooms" goal rather than a new node — this follows the explicit instruction to avoid duplicating goals with the same underlying intention.

**Step 8 — Exclude non-robot and merge duplicate leaf actions.**
Every leaf task in the final tree is performed by a robot (physical check, communication, movement, self-maintenance) — nothing with "zero robots" was modeled (e.g., the patient's own biological response to questions is not a task, since the patient is not a robot). Heart-rate and blood-oxygenation checks appear twice (once for infectious-disease patients, once for post-surgical patients) because they occur inside two different, non-overlapping condition-specific branches of the tree (a tree cannot share one leaf under two parents) — I flagged this explicitly rather than silently duplicating it.

**Step 9 — Location and robot-count assignment.**
The description cites exactly two locations: "hospital room(s)" / "occupied room" and "the site" (from "responsible manager for the site"). I never invented a location (e.g., no fabricated "charging station"): room-bound actions use **Occupied Room**; fleet/mission-level actions (recharge, reassignment, adding robots, manager alert) use **Hospital/Site**, since the description does not tie them to a specific room. Most tasks use **1 robot** ("the assigned robot" is always singular in the text); reassignment and additional-robot-assignment use a **range [1, n]** since the number of robots involved is explicitly variable ("additional robots", "combination of robots").

## 6. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, AT21, AT22 [AND][FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G2, AT21), AT22)]**
  - **Justification**: The description gives an explicit, ordered escalation: attempt the mission with the currently assigned robot(s) (G2); if it can't be completed in time, add robots (AT21); if still impossible, alert the manager (AT22). This is a textbook nested fallback, and FALLBACK pairs are always AND by definition.

- **G2 -> G3, G4, G9, G10, G15, G16 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: These are the six jointly-required phases of a room visit, narrated in this order in the text (identify → pre-checks → engage → collect vitals → hygiene → finalize). All must occur for a room to be considered "visited," hence AND; the narrative gives an explicit order, hence sequential.

- **G3 -> AT1, AT2 [OR](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Either by asking or by scanning" is an explicit alternative — OR. No priority/attempt-order is stated, so FALLBACK cannot be assumed; `#` reflects that either method (without obligation of a fixed order) resolves the goal.

- **G4 -> G5, G7 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both checks are explicitly required before entry ("and"); no order between them is given, so parallel.

- **G5 -> G6, AT3 [AND][FALLBACK(G6, AT3)]**
  - **Justification**: "If the patient is not available, the robot should come back" is an explicit condition-triggered alternative to the availability check — FALLBACK, hence AND.

- **G7 -> G8, AT4, AT5 [AND][FALLBACK(G8, AT4#AT5)]**
  - **Justification**: "If there is not enough battery, the robot should recharge and the tasks need to be reassigned" is an explicit condition-triggered alternative to the battery check — FALLBACK (hence AND at this level); recharge and reassignment have no stated order between them, so parallel within the fallback's second branch.

- **G9 -> AT6, AT7, AT8 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: All three actions are explicitly required ("approach... announce... and provide instructions"), listed and physically necessitated in this order.

- **G10 -> G11, G14, AT16 [AND][FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G11, G14), AT16)]**
  - **Justification**: Explicit two-level escalation: attempt direct measurement (G11); on failure, ask assessment questions (G14); on non-response, alert staff (AT16). Nested FALLBACK, hence AND.

- **G11 -> G12, G13, AT15 [OR](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The three condition profiles (infectious, post-surgical, diabetic) are not described as mutually exclusive; fulfilling whichever applies to the patient is sufficient — genuine OR, with parallel runtime since multiple could apply at once with no stated precedence.

- **G12 -> AT9, AT10, AT11, AT12 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: All four signs are explicitly and jointly required for infectious-disease patients; no order is stated among them.

- **G13 -> AT13, AT14 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both signs explicitly and jointly required for post-surgical patients; no order stated.

- **G15 -> AT17, AT18 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Sterilization and actuator disinfection are independent, condition-triggered hygiene requirements with no stated order between them.

- **G16 -> AT19, AT20 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: "Mark it as done and proceed to the next room" explicitly orders marking before moving on.

## 7. Key Modeling Notes & Assumptions (transparency)

1. **"Every two hours" and "high-risk patients checked within 15 minutes"** are treated as temporal/scheduling constraints on G1/G2 rather than separate goal nodes, to avoid duplicating the single underlying intention of "visit/check a room's patient."
2. **Order of Identify-Patient vs. Pre-Entry-Checks** is an inference (identification is needed to know *whom* to check availability for), not an explicit statement in the text — flagged for your review in case the intended order is reversed.
3. **Duplicate leaf tasks** (heart rate and oxygenation appear under both G12 and G13) are intentional: the tree structure forbids a single leaf having two parents, and these checks live in two separate OR-branches.
4. **No fabricated locations**: only "Occupied Room" and "Hospital/Site" are used, since those are the only locations cited in the text; fleet-level tasks (recharge, reassignment, additional robots, manager alert) default to "Hospital" rather than an invented charging bay or office.
