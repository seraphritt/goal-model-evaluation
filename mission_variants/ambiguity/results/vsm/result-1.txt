# Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the tables, here is the analytical process I followed, including the key decisions that shape the model.

**1. Identifying the top-level trigger.** The mission is periodic ("every two hours"), but periodicity is a *scheduling/trigger* property of the whole mission, not an AND/OR relation between sub-goals — so it is **not** represented as a tree node. It is noted as a property of the root goal instead.

**2. Finding the three top-level concerns.** Reading the whole text, three independent concerns emerge at mission level:
 (a) actually visiting rooms and checking patients;
 (b) what to do if the mission can't be finished in time (assign more robots);
 (c) what to do if even that doesn't work (alert the manager).
 (b) and (c) are explicitly conditional ("in case of impossibility...", "if no combination of robots can complete the mission") → this is the textbook trigger for **FALLBACK**, nested because there are two sequential escalation levels: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(VisitRooms, AddRobots), AlertManager)`.

**3. High-risk-first scheduling.** "All patients in high risk must be checked in the first 15 minutes" is a scheduling constraint, not a failure-triggered alternative (no IF-condition), so it does **not** get FALLBACK. I modeled it as a dedicated **Query** goal ("determine occupied rooms + risk classification") that must happen *before* the visiting loop — a sequential, not fallback, relation, since the order is inferable (you must know the order before executing it) but not failure-driven.

**4. Per-room protocol is a single reusable process.** To avoid redundancy (as instructed), I modeled "visit a room" as **one** goal subtree, applied to each occupied room in priority order, rather than duplicating the whole subtree for "high-risk rooms" vs "other rooms."

**5. Patient identification.** "either by asking or by scanning the barcode" is an explicit **OR** (alternative sufficient methods), with no stated order between them → parallel/no-order runtime.

**6. Two IF-triggered retries before entry.** "If the patient is not available → come back in 5 min" and "If there is not enough battery → recharge and reassign" are both explicit condition-triggered alternatives → **FALLBACK** in each case. Per the FALLBACK rule, each pair is AND-connected structurally even though only one branch executes at runtime.

**7. Required vital signs are patient-condition-dependent, not alternatives.** Infectious/post-surgical/diabetic requirements are not mutually exclusive (a patient could be both infectious and diabetic), so they are **not** an OR. I introduced one **Query** goal that determines which conditions apply, feeding one **Achieve** goal that measures the applicable signs (AND-decomposed over all five possible measurements, since conjunction — "checked for X, Y, Z, and W" — is explicit in the text for each condition group). This avoids creating three redundant "check infectious/post-surgical/diabetic vitals" goals that would overlap on shared measurements (e.g., heart rate appears in two groups).

**8. Escalation chain for failed vitals.** "Failure to collect → ask questions" then "no answer → alert nurse/doctor" is two chained, explicitly condition-triggered alternatives → nested **FALLBACK**.

**9. Sterilization vs. actuator disinfection** are two separate, independently conditioned tasks (infectious-room exit vs. any patient contact) — both AND-ed in, each justified by its own explicit IF-clause in the text, placed where the text situates them (disinfection right after in-room contact, sterilization at room exit).

**10. Goal typing.** I used **Achieve** wherever a state must be reached (possibly through retries/planning): mission completion, room-visit completion, patient readiness, battery sufficiency, vitals collected. I used **Query** wherever the goal only gathers information without changing world state: determining room/risk lists, determining which conditions apply. I used **Perform** wherever the goal is satisfied simply by executing a fixed action sequence regardless of a further world-state target: approaching/briefing the patient, and the room-exit actions (sterilize/move on).

**11. Zero-robot exclusion.** Every leaf task is something the robot itself executes (measuring, asking, scanning, moving, recharging, alerting, sterilizing, disinfecting). No purely administrative/non-robot actions were included.

**12. Default robot count.** The text consistently says "**the** assigned robot" (singular) for the normal flow, so Number of Robots = 1 everywhere except the two tasks that explicitly deal with scaling the team ("additional robots," "reassigned" tasks across the team), which I gave as a range **[1, n]**, n being the maximum robots obtainable.

# Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G17, AT29 [AND] FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G2, G17), AT29)**
 - **Justification**: Visiting rooms is the normal path; reassigning robots is the explicit IF-triggered alternative ("in case of impossibility of performing the mission in the required time"); alerting the manager is the further explicit IF-triggered alternative ("if no combination of robots can complete the mission"). FALLBACK pairs are always AND-connected.

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: The visit order must be determined before rooms can be visited in that order — an inferable, non-conditional dependency, hence sequential rather than fallback.

- **G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND] (parallel)**
 - **Justification**: Both facts (occupancy, risk level) are needed to build the order; neither depends on the other, so no order is imposed.

- **G4 -> G5, G10, G16 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: The text explicitly sequences these phases: "Before entering the room..." / "Once inside the room..." / "When a robot comes out of a room...".

- **G5 -> G6, G9 [AND] (parallel)**
 - **Justification**: Both preconditions are presented together with no stated precedence, and they are logically independent (patient status vs. robot battery).

- **G6 -> G7, G8 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: You must know which patient you're dealing with (identification) before checking *that* patient's availability, especially in multi-bed rooms.

- **G7 -> AT3, AT4 [OR] (parallel)**
 - **Justification**: "Either by asking or by scanning the barcode" is an explicit alternative — either method suffices; no order is stated.

- **G8 -> AT5, AT6 [AND] FALLBACK(AT5, AT6)**
 - **Justification**: "If the patient is not available, the robot should come back in 5 minutes" is an explicit IF-condition triggering an alternative action.

- **G9 -> AT7, AT8 [AND] FALLBACK(AT7, AT8)**
 - **Justification**: "If there is not enough battery, the robot should recharge and the tasks need to be reassigned" is an explicit IF-condition triggering an alternative action.

- **G10 -> G11, G12, AT23, AT24 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: The text's flow is: brief the patient → collect vitals (with escalation) → disinfect actuators "afterwards" (contact occurs during collection) → "once the patient is checked," mark as done.

- **G11 -> AT9, AT10, AT11 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: The text lists these three actions in an explicit left-to-right order: "approach the patient, announce the procedure..., and provide instructions."

- **G12 -> G13, G14, G15, AT22 [AND] G13 ; (FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G14, G15), AT22))**
 - **Justification**: Required signs must be known before attempting measurement (sequential prefix). "Failure of collecting any necessary vital signs should trigger a set of questions" and "if the patient does not answer the questions, an alert should be sent" are two chained, explicit IF-triggered alternatives → nested FALLBACK.

- **G13 -> AT12, AT13, AT14 [AND] (parallel)**
 - **Justification**: All three condition checks are needed to assemble the complete requirement list; they are independent facts with no stated order.

- **G14 -> AT15, AT16, AT17, AT18, AT19 [AND] (parallel)**
 - **Justification**: The text specifies conjunctive requirements per condition group (e.g., "heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, and blood oxygenation"); no order is given for which measurement comes first.

- **G15 -> AT20, AT21 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: Questions must be posed before a response can be recorded — an inferable, non-conditional order.

- **G16 -> AT25, AT26 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: Sterilization is tied to the moment the robot "comes out of" the room, which necessarily precedes moving on to the next room.

- **G17 -> AT27, AT28 [AND] (sequential)**
 - **Justification**: Robots must be assigned to the mission before pending visits can be reallocated among the (now larger) team.

---

**Key modeling assumptions worth flagging:** (1) the 2-hour periodicity is treated as a scheduling trigger for the whole model rather than a tree relation; (2) "the assigned robot" is read as implying a single robot by default, with team-scaling confined to G17/AT27/AT28; (3) applicability of specific vital-sign and condition-check tasks is patient-dependent, handled by the preceding Query goals (G3, G13) rather than by duplicating goals per condition type, to respect the no-redundancy rule.
