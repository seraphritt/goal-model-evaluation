# Mission Goal Model: Multi-Robot Hospital Vital-Signs Check

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the tables, here is the reasoning I followed to decompose the mission:

1. **Find the mission's top intention.** The description opens with the real objective: "every two hours, all patients' vital signs should be checked." Visiting rooms is just the *means*. So the root goal is an **Achieve Goal** (a state to reach: "all patients checked"), not simply "visit rooms."

2. **Separate the "happy path" from the "exception path."** Every time the text uses an explicit conditional ("If the patient is not available...", "If there is not enough battery...", "The failure of collecting...", "In case of non-response...", "in case of impossibility...", "if no combination of robots can complete the mission") I treated it as an explicit order between a normal attempt and an alternative — textbook **FALLBACK**. I never used FALLBACK where the text just lists requirements without an explicit "if/then" trigger.

3. **Distinguish AND from OR by the connecting particle and by *applicability*.**
   - Conjunctive requirements ("battery check **and** availability check", "heart rate, blood pressure, temperature **and** oxygenation") → **AND**.
   - Explicit alternatives ("**either** by asking **or** by scanning") → **OR**.
   - Condition-dependent requirements (infectious vs. post-surgical vs. diabetic vitals; sterilization vs. actuator disinfection) are **OR**, because which branch(es) apply depends on the specific patient/room — a patient could trigger one, two, or even all branches, which matches the inclusive reading of OR ("fulfilling one child is sufficient," not "exactly one").

4. **Use PARALLEL as the default "no stated order" case**, and **SEQUENTIAL** only where the narrative gives a clear procedural order (e.g., "approach… announce… provide instructions"; "mark it as done **and** proceed to next room"). I avoided inventing orders the text doesn't support.

5. **Avoid redundancy.** Heart rate and blood oxygenation are required by *both* the infectious-disease and post-surgical profiles. Rather than creating two separate "Measure Heart Rate" tasks, I reused the same task node (`AT13`, `AT16`) under both parent goals — this is explicitly encouraged ("the more relationships the better") and avoids duplicating the same intention.

6. **Collapse single-action "goals" into Tasks.** Since every Goal needs at least two children to be decomposed, anything that reduces to one atomic action (e.g., "wait and retry," "alert the manager," "measure glucose") is modeled directly as a **Task**, not wrapped in an artificial Goal.

7. **Zero-robot rule.** I kept "assign additional robots" and "alert the manager/nurse" in the model because, in a multi-robot system, these communication/coordination actions are themselves executed by a robot (the robot at the bedside, or a coordinating robot in the fleet) — I treat this as an explicit assumption (see below), otherwise these mission-critical behaviors from the text would have to be dropped entirely.

8. **Location taxonomy.** The description only grounds two spatial contexts: the **Room** (a specific patient's room — all touch/identification/measurement/communication happens here) and the **Hospital**/site (facility-wide concerns: occupancy data, charging stations, fleet reassignment, inter-room transit, manager/staff notification). I did not invent any location not implied by the text.

9. **Number of Robots.** Per-room tasks are executed by the single robot assigned to that room (the text never asks for more than one robot *inside* a room). Fleet-level coordination tasks (reassigning tasks, adding robots) are modeled as a *range* `[1,n]` because the text says "tasks" (plural) and "additional robots" without fixing a count — the exact number is a function of how many robots the fleet can spare.

### Key assumptions (stated explicitly since the text is silent on them)
- A "coordinating" robot (or the bedside robot acting in that role) is the executor of fleet-level actions (reassignment, additional-robot dispatch, manager alert) — satisfying the "no zero-robot nodes" constraint.
- Sterilization and actuator disinfection are both physically performed by the visiting robot itself, at/near the room it is leaving.
- "Come back after a short period" and "recharge" are not modeled as explicit loops (goal models in this formalism are static trees, not state machines); the retry is implicit in the Fallback alternative.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, AT25, AT26 [AND] FALLBACK(G2, FALLBACK(AT25, AT26))**
  - **Justification**: Normal operation is visiting all rooms (G2); if that's not achievable in time, the fleet first tries adding robots (AT25), and only escalates to the manager (AT26) if that also fails — matching the text's explicit two-tier conditional.

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: You must know which rooms are occupied (and their priority) before you can tour them; this is a necessary operational order even though not phrased as an "if/then," so sequential rather than fallback.

- **G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Occupancy and risk data are both required to build the visit order; no sequence between the two queries is implied.

- **G4 -> G5, G10, G11, G16, AT23, AT24 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The text narrates the per-room procedure as an explicit sequence of phases (pre-entry checks → patient engagement → vitals collection/escalation → hygiene → wrap-up), so each phase must complete before the next starts.

- **G5 -> G6, G7, G8 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: All three pre-entry conditions ("available," "enough battery," "correct patient") are mandatory, but the text gives no order among them.

- **G6 -> AT3, AT4 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "either by asking or by scanning" is an explicit alternative (OR); no stated preference between the two, so parallel rather than fallback.

- **G7 -> AT5, AT6 [AND] FALLBACK(AT5, AT6)**
  - **Justification**: Checking availability is the normal path; waiting and retrying is the explicit alternative triggered "if the patient is not available."

- **G8 -> AT7, G9 [AND] FALLBACK(AT7, G9)**
  - **Justification**: Checking battery is the normal path; recharging/reassigning is the explicit alternative triggered "if there is not enough battery."

- **G9 -> AT8, AT9 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both recharging and reassignment are required together, but neither is stated as happening before the other.

- **G10 -> AT10, AT11, AT12 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The sentence lists "approach... announce... provide instructions" in that explicit order.

- **G11 -> G12, G15, AT20 [AND] FALLBACK(G12, FALLBACK(G15, AT20))**
  - **Justification**: Collecting vitals is attempted first; on failure, questioning is attempted; on non-response during questioning, staff are alerted — a clean two-level nested fallback, exactly as the text describes it.

- **G12 -> G13, G14, AT17 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Which vitals are needed depends on the patient's condition(s); a patient could trigger more than one branch, so OR (inclusive) fits better than AND (not everyone needs every branch) or a fallback (none of these are failure-triggered alternatives to each other).

- **G13 -> AT13, AT14, AT15, AT16 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: All four vitals are explicitly and jointly required for infectious-disease patients; no measurement order given.

- **G14 -> AT13, AT16 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both vitals explicitly and jointly required for post-surgical patients; reuses the same measurement tasks as G13 rather than duplicating them.

- **G15 -> AT18, AT19 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: A response can only be evaluated once the questions have been asked.

- **G16 -> AT21, AT22 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Sterilization and actuator disinfection are triggered by two independent, unrelated conditions (infectious-disease room exposure vs. any physical contact) — a room could trigger one, the other, both, or neither, matching inclusive OR; no order is implied.

---

**One point worth flagging for discussion**: the "additional robots" and "alert the manager/staff" nodes (AT25, AT26, AT20) required an assumption that these coordination actions are executed *by* a robot in the fleet, since the "zero-robot" exclusion rule would otherwise force dropping meaningful parts of the mission description. If your framework treats fleet coordination and staff notification as outside the robot layer entirely, those three tasks (and their parent fallbacks) should be removed, and G1/G11 would become simple Achieve goals without the time-escalation and non-response branches — happy to produce that alternate, stricter version if useful.
