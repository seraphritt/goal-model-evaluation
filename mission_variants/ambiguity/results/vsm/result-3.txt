# Goal Model for the Hospital Vital-Signs Checking Mission

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**1. Identify the recurring mission and its "shape."**
The description is a *periodic* mission ("every two hours... all vitals checked"), executed by potentially *multiple* robots over a *set* of occupied rooms. This immediately tells me the root goal must be an **Achieve** goal (a state — "all patients checked, on time" — that the robots plan and act to reach), and that most of the tree below it will be a *generic* "per-room" procedure applied over a room collection, rather than one goal per physical room (this keeps the model free of redundancy).

**2. Separate the "normal path" from the "contingency path."**
Two sentences stand apart from the room-by-room procedure because they describe mission-level feasibility, not room-level behavior: "*Additional robots can be assigned... in case of impossibility...*" and "*the manager must be alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission*". Both are explicit IF-triggered alternatives to the main plan (visit all rooms with the currently assigned robots), so I modeled them as a **nested FALLBACK** at the very top of the tree.

**3. Find every other explicit IF-trigger (Fallback candidates).**
I went through the text looking strictly for stated "normal behavior, then alternative-on-failure" patterns, since the prompt is explicit that Fallback must **not** be used unless the order/condition is actually stated:
- Patient not available → come back in 5 min. ✅ Fallback.
- Not enough battery → recharge + reassign. ✅ Fallback.
- Vital-sign collection fails → ask questions. ✅ Fallback.
- Patient doesn't answer questions → alert nurse/doctor. ✅ Fallback (nested inside the previous one).
I deliberately did **not** use Fallback for the "ask vs. scan barcode" choice (no stated priority — modeled as OR/parallel) or for the condition-based vital-sign sets (no failure/alternative logic — modeled as OR, since applicability depends on patient condition, not on an attempt failing).

**4. Find explicit AND/OR particles for Goal/Task decomposition.**
"Either by asking or by scanning" → explicit OR. "Checked for heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, and blood oxygenation" → looks like AND, but because the *same* vital-sign tasks are shared across three different conditions (infectious, post-surgical, diabetic), I treated the condition→vital-signs mapping as an **OR** over the five distinct measurement tasks (at least one — typically a condition-specific subset — must be fulfilled) rather than duplicating "Measure Heart Rate" and "Measure Blood Oxygenation" as separate nodes under three condition-specific goals. This follows the explicit non-redundancy rule.

**5. Find explicit sequencing vs. true parallelism.**
"Approach, announce, provide instructions" and "mark as done and proceed" are both written as ordered action chains with clear physical/logical dependency → Sequential. "Check patient availability... and if there is enough battery" has no stated order between the two checks themselves → Parallel. However, because rooms "may have one or more beds," the robot logically cannot check *a specific patient's* availability without first knowing *which* patient it is — so I sequenced "Identify Patient" before the parallel availability/battery pair, and justified this inference explicitly rather than hiding it.

**6. Ground every goal in at least one robot-executed task.**
Per the "zero-robot" rule, I made sure every leaf goal bottoms out in concrete tasks with a Location (only "room"/"occupied room" and "hospital" are ever cited — I never invented a location) and a Number of Robots (singular "the assigned robot" ⇒ 1 for in-room tasks; "additional robots can be assigned" ⇒ a variable range for the escalation tasks).

**7. Avoid duplicate intentions.**
Local battery-triggered task reassignment (one robot's remaining room tasks handed to another robot) and mission-level "assign additional robots" (bringing more robots into the fleet because of a time/feasibility problem) look superficially similar but serve different intentions (local recovery vs. global feasibility) — I kept them as two separate, non-redundant tasks/goals.

## Notes on Key Design Choices
- **Non-redundancy**: The condition-specific vital-sign requirements (infectious, post-surgical, diabetic) were *not* modeled as three separate goal subtrees (which would have duplicated "Measure Heart Rate" and "Measure Blood Oxygenation" three times); instead, a single OR-decomposed goal (G11) over five distinct tasks captures all condition-dependent combinations.
- **Zero-robot rule**: Every goal bottoms out in at least one robot-executed task; nothing purely administrative/non-robotic was added to the tree.
- **Fallback discipline**: Fallback was used *only* where the text gives an explicit "normal case / IF-triggered alternative" pattern (4 cases + 1 mission-level nested case) — never for the barcode/ask alternative or the condition-based vitals, since no failure/order is stated there.
