# Multi-Robot Mission Goal Model — Hospital Vital-Signs Checking Mission

Below is my full analysis: first the reasoning process I followed to get from free text to a goal tree, then the formal Goal table, Task table, Summary table, and the Logical Relationships listing.

---

## 1. Step-by-Step Reasoning Process

**Step 1 — Extract atomic intentions.**
I re-read the description sentence by sentence and tagged every verb phrase that represents either (a) a state the system wants to bring about ("vital signs checked," "patient available," "battery sufficient," "mission completed in time"), or (b) a concrete action a robot performs ("approach," "announce," "scan barcode," "recharge," "sterilize"). States became **Goal** candidates; concrete, assignable actions became **Task** candidates.

**Step 2 — Check the "zero-robot" exclusion rule.**
Every candidate was checked against "who performs this?". Even actions whose *recipient* is a human (alerting a nurse, alerting the manager) are *performed* by the robot (it is the one sending/raising the alert), so they stay. I found no candidate that had literally no robot involved, so nothing was dropped for this reason.

**Step 3 — Avoid duplicate intentions.**
"Check heart rate" appears for both infectious-disease and post-surgical patients. These are *the same physical action* invoked in two different conditional branches, not two different intentions — I kept them as two task instances (different parents, different context) rather than inventing two different goals, since the redundancy rule targets goals with the same *intention*, not tasks that are reused in different branches of a tree.

**Step 4 — Decide AND vs OR for each decomposition.**
I used **AND** wherever the text uses conjunctions ("heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, **and** blood oxygenation," "recharge **and** the tasks need to be reassigned") or where two sub-requirements are jointly necessary (identify patient **and** check availability **and** check battery). I used **OR** only where the text literally gives alternatives ("either by asking **or** by scanning," and the three patient-category vital-sign rules, which are *alternative applicability conditions*, not jointly mandatory for every patient).

**Step 5 — Decide runtime annotations, with special discipline on FALLBACK.**
Per the rules, FALLBACK is reserved strictly for places where the text gives an explicit IF/condition/failure that triggers an alternative. I found exactly four such places:
- Patient not available → come back later
- Battery insufficient → recharge & reassign
- Vital-sign collection fails → ask questions
- No response to questions → alert nurse/doctor
- (and, at the mission level) impossibility of completing in time → add robots → still impossible → alert manager

Everywhere else (e.g., ask vs. scan; measuring HR/BP/Temp/SpO2; sterilizing vs. disinfecting), the text gives **no explicit order or preference**, so I deliberately avoided FALLBACK there (even though one could *read* an implicit order into it) and used **parallel (#)** instead, since the definition allows — but does not require — simultaneous execution, which best represents "no mandated order." Where the narrative explicitly walks through a sequence of actions (before entering → identify/verify → enter → approach → announce → instruct → collect vitals → finalize → next room), I used **sequential (;)**.

**Step 6 — Decide Goal Types.**
- Goals with a clear state to reach ("all rooms checked," "patient available," "battery sufficient," "mission completed in time") → **Achieve**.
- The "Fetch Occupied Rooms" goal is pure information retrieval (matches the worked example in the prompt) → **Query**.
- The "approach / announce / instruct" bundle has no target *state* beyond executing the actions themselves — it either gets performed or it fails, with no complex condition to plan toward → **Perform**.

**Step 7 — Resolve "atomic" goals with only one natural child.**
Rules require AND/OR decomposition with a *minimum of two children*. Some natural candidates (e.g., "check glucose") only map to a single action. Rather than artificially forcing a one-child "goal," I demoted these to plain **Tasks** sitting directly as OR-siblings of their richer neighbors (e.g., `AT20 Measure Blood Glucose` sits as a sibling of the goals `G13 Infectious Disease Vitals` and `G14 Post-Surgical Vitals` under `G12`). This preserves the "minimum two children" rule at every decomposition point while staying faithful to the text.

**Step 8 — Decide Location and Number of Robots for tasks.**
I only used locations that are explicitly named or directly implied by the text: "Hospital," "Occupied Room" (single instance, the room currently being visited) and "Occupied Rooms" (the collection, when a task spans/compares across rooms). I did not invent a "charging station," "nurse station," etc., since those aren't named. For Number of Robots, the baseline is **1** (the text consistently says "the assigned robot," singular) except for fleet-level/resource-management tasks (reassigning pending tasks, monitoring timing feasibility, assigning additional robots), where I used the **[1,n]** variable notation, since the text explicitly treats these as scalable ("additional robots can be assigned," "no combination of robots").

**Step 9 — Reflect on the top-level structure (G2 vs. G17).**
This was the trickiest call. "Additional robots... in case of impossibility" *sounds* like a FALLBACK candidate at the mission level. But on reflection, assigning additional robots and alerting the manager are not alternatives performed *instead of* visiting rooms (G2) — they are a concurrent oversight process that augments G2 while it is running. The FALLBACK logic genuinely lives *inside* that oversight process (try with current robots → add robots if infeasible → alert manager if still infeasible), so I nested the FALLBACK **inside G17** and related **G2 and G17 to each other at the top level with parallel (#)**, since nothing in the text says one must finish before the other starts — they coexist for the whole mission duration. I flag this explicitly in the justification below since it's the least "obvious" choice in the model.

## 5. Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G17 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Room-visiting and feasibility oversight are both necessary for a responsible mission, and the text gives no indication that one must finish before the other starts — feasibility is monitored *throughout* execution.

- **G2 -> G3, G4, G5 [AND] (sequential: G3;(G4;G5))**
  - **Justification**: You must know which rooms are occupied (G3) before you can prioritize among them (G4), and prioritize before actually looping through them (G5) — both a logical and textually implied order.

- **G4 -> AT1, AT2 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: High-risk rooms must be identified before the visiting order can be scheduled.

- **G5 -> G6, G10, G11, G15 [AND] (sequential: G6;(G10;(G11;G15)))**
  - **Justification**: This mirrors the description's explicit narrative order: pre-entry checks, then in-room preparation, then vitals collection, then finalization.

- **G6 -> G7, G8, G9 [AND] ((G7;G8)#G9)**
  - **Justification**: Identifying the patient must precede checking *their* availability (sequential), while the battery check is independent of patient identity and can proceed in parallel.

- **G7 -> AT3, AT4 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Either by asking or by scanning" is an explicit alternative with no stated priority order — OR decomposition, parallel runtime, no FALLBACK since no failure-triggered order is given.

- **G8 -> AT5, AT6 [AND] (FALLBACK(AT5, AT6))**
  - **Justification**: Explicit IF-condition in the text ("If the patient is not available...") triggering a named alternative action.

- **G9 -> AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND] (FALLBACK(AT7, (AT8#AT9)))**
  - **Justification**: Explicit IF-condition ("If there is not enough battery..."); recharge and reassignment are listed jointly with no order, hence parallel within the fallback branch.

- **G10 -> AT10, AT11, AT12, AT13 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The text lists "approach... announce... provide instructions" in explicit order, with entry as the implied first step.

- **G11 -> G12, AT21, AT22 [AND] (FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G12, AT21), AT22))**
  - **Justification**: Two explicit, nested conditions in the text: failure to collect vitals triggers questions; non-response to questions triggers an alert to the nurse/doctor.

- **G12 -> G13, G14, AT20 [OR] (parallel: (G13#G14)#AT20)**
  - **Justification**: Each branch applies conditionally depending on the patient's actual medical category (not jointly mandatory for every patient), so OR is the correct decomposition; no order is implied among the branches.

- **G13 -> AT14, AT15, AT16, AT17 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The text explicitly conjoins all four measurements ("heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, and blood oxygenation"), with no stated measurement order.

- **G14 -> AT18, AT19 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both measurements explicitly required together, no order stated.

- **G15 -> AT23, G16, AT26 [AND] (sequential: AT23;(G16;AT26))**
  - **Justification**: "Mark it as done and proceed to the next room" gives explicit order; decontamination logically occurs as the robot exits, between the two.

- **G16 -> AT24, AT25 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Sterilization and disinfection serve different triggers (exiting an infectious room vs. patient contact) and can both apply at once — they're joint obligations, not alternatives, so AND; no order is stated between them.

- **G17 -> AT27, AT28, AT29 [AND] (FALLBACK(FALLBACK(AT27, AT28), AT29))**
  - **Justification**: Explicit nested conditions: additional robots are assigned only "in case of impossibility," and the manager is alerted only if "no combination of robots" can complete the mission.

### A closing reflection
The two choices I'd flag as most interpretive (rather than directly textual) are: (1) treating "Monitor Timing Feasibility" (AT27) as an inferred task, since the mission text never names a monitoring step explicitly, only its triggered consequences; and (2) using **parallel** rather than **fallback** between G2 and G17 at the top level — I believe this is correct because the fallback logic the text actually specifies is a chain of *responses to infeasibility* (add robots → alert manager), not a "try G2, then try something else entirely" structure. If your intended semantics differ (e.g., you want G17 to only ever activate after G2 is confirmed un-completable, rather than continuously), that one relationship is the place to revisit.
