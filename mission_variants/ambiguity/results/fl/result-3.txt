# Mission Decomposition: Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Retrieval

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**1. Identifying the top-level responsibilities.** The description names two independent, recurring services the robot system must provide: (a) delivering food from the kitchen to a room, triggered "on an order-by-order basis in response to kitchen delivery requests," and (b) retrieving dirty dishes from the room, triggered when "someone in the room can call a robot to pick up the dishes." Since neither service depends on the other's completion and both are core system responsibilities, I modeled a root goal that AND-decomposes into these two, executed in **parallel** (no explicit order ties them together).

**2. Food delivery — explicit "from the kitchen" phrasing.** The phrase "deliver food **from** the kitchen **to** an inpatient room" implies two distinct phases: collecting the meal at the kitchen, then delivering it at the room. I split this into a sequential pair, since physically the robot cannot deliver what it hasn't collected.

**3. Delivery method — explicit OR.** "The food can be delivered into a room table by the robot ... **or** the food can be fetched from the robot's tray" is a textbook OR-decomposition between two alternative delivery mechanisms.

**4. Tray retrieval — cooperation options (explicit OR).** "Fetching from the robot tray requires cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, **or** another robot" is another explicit OR. I grouped the three human options under one sub-goal (since they share the "indicate + present" procedure) and kept robot-robot cooperation as a separate alternative, because it does not require the "indicate meal" step (a robot doesn't need the visual/verbal indication a human does).

**5. Capability check — explicit Query + explicit AND.** "The information about if the inpatient, a companion visitor or nurse can be able to retrieve ... can be obtained based on the inpatient record **according to** information about the patient **and** the presence of a companion into the room" is explicitly framed as information-gathering (Query Goal), with two AND-ed information sources ("and").

**6. Indicate meal — explicit, conditioned on human retrieval.** "The robot can carry multiple meals, in the case of a person retrieving the meal the robot should indicate which meal should be retrieved" — this only applies to human cooperation, and logically must happen before/at the point the tray is offered, hence sequential.

**7. Tracking — explicit AND ("when and where").** "It should track when and where each meal was retrieved" gives two AND-ed properties to log.

**8. Alert — explicit FALLBACK.** "The robot should generate an alert if the meal retrieval is not performed correctly" is an explicit IF-triggered exception-handling behavior — the textbook case for the FALLBACK operator per the instructions. I nested it as `FALLBACK((Retrieve ; Track), Alert)` because tracking only makes sense after a successful retrieval; if retrieval fails, the alternative branch (alert) fires instead.

**9. Dish retrieval — explicit OR + door-opening sub-problem.** "The dish retrieve can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots or with the cooperation of robots and a human" is a 3-way OR. "Opening the room door can involve cooperation with another robot or a human" is a separate OR, logically prerequisite to collecting dishes (must access the room first) — sequential.

**10. Door-opening capability — mirrors the capability-check pattern.** "That person can signal if they can open the door when the robot comes to it" is information the robot needs before choosing *how* to open the door, so I modeled it analogously to the food-retrieval capability check: a Query goal feeding sequentially into the door-opening action.

**11. Zero-robot filtering.** I deliberately phrased every leaf task from the robot's operational perspective (e.g., "Present Tray to Inpatient" rather than "Inpatient takes food") so that every node involves at least one robot performing an action, per the instruction to exclude zero-robot nodes.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G13 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Food delivery and dish retrieval are both described as core, independently triggered system services (kitchen requests vs. room calls); nothing in the text sequences one before the other.

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: "Deliver food **from** the kitchen **to** an inpatient room" requires collecting the meal before it can be delivered.

- **G3 -> AT1 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: A single operational step fulfills the goal; trivially AND since the one child is required.

- **G4 -> G5, G6 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "The food can be delivered into a room table ... **or** ... fetched from the robot's tray" — either method suffices, no stated order between them.

- **G5 -> AT2 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single required action (place food on table).

- **G6 -> G7, G8, G11, G12 [AND] (FALLBACK((G8 ; G11), G12), with G7 first: G7 ; FALLBACK((G8 ; G11), G12))**
  - **Justification**: All four elements are jointly necessary to fully realize tray-based delivery. Capability must be checked before attempting retrieval (sequential). Retrieval is attempted first and, if successful, followed by tracking (sequential "G8 ; G11"); the alert is the explicit IF-triggered alternative pursued only when retrieval "is not performed correctly" (FALLBACK), and per the FALLBACK rule the two branches are AND-connected to the parent.

- **G7 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Capability info is obtained "according to information about the patient **and** the presence of a companion" — both sources are required, with no stated order.

- **G8 -> G9, AT9 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, **or** another robot" — human cooperation (grouped) and robot cooperation are alternative, unordered means.

- **G9 -> AT5, G10 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Since "the robot can carry multiple meals," it must indicate the correct meal before/while presenting the tray, so the retrieving human knows which to take — this ordering is required for the step to make sense.

- **G10 -> AT6, AT7, AT8 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Explicit three-way alternative ("the inpatient, a companion visitor, or nurse"); which applies depends on the capability query, with no fixed order among them.

- **AT9 is a direct OR-alternative task under G8** (see above); it requires 2 robots per the explicit "another robot" cooperation requirement.

- **G11 -> AT10, AT11 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Track **when and where**" names two properties that must both be logged, with no stated order.

- **G12 -> AT12 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single required action (generate the alert).

- **G13 -> G14, G17 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The robot must gain access to the room (open the door) before it can physically collect dishes from it — a necessary precondition, not stated as optional.

- **G14 -> G15, G16 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Mirroring the food-retrieval pattern, the robot must first learn whether the occupant can open the door (query) before deciding/executing how the door will be opened.

- **G15 -> AT13 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Single required action (receive the occupant's signal), directly reflecting "that person can signal if they can open the door."

- **G16 -> AT14, AT15 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Opening the room door can involve cooperation with another robot **or** a human" — two alternative, unordered methods.

- **G17 -> AT16, AT17, AT18 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "The dish retrieve can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots **or** with the cooperation of robots and a human" — three explicit, unordered alternatives.

### A few modeling notes worth flagging
- I treated the "uncertainty" remark ("This information is subject to uncertainty") as a property of the Query goal **G7** rather than a separate node, since it doesn't introduce a new action or decomposition — it only qualifies the reliability of the queried data.
- The "kitchen collection" phase (**G3/AT1**) is an inference from the directional phrase "from the kitchen to an inpatient room" rather than an explicitly detailed procedure; I flagged this so you can verify it matches your intended scope — it could be dropped if you want the model to start strictly at the room-delivery stage.
- Door-opening capability (**G15**) was modeled by analogy to the food-retrieval capability check (**G7**) to keep the two symmetric service branches consistent, since the mission describes a structurally similar "signal before acting" pattern for both.
