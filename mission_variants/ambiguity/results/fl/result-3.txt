# Goal Model Analysis — Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Pickup Multi-Robot System

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

Before building the tables, here is my decomposition logic, walked through the description section by section.

**1. Overall mission (root).** The description covers two symmetric but distinct phases: delivering food and retrieving dirty dishes ("As with delivery, the dish retrieve can occur..." explicitly draws this parallel). I made these the two main AND-branches of a root goal. I treat their execution as **sequential**, not parallel: dishes only become "dirty" and ready for pickup *after* a meal has been delivered and consumed, and the text itself narrates delivery first, pickup second ("At pickup..."). This is an inference from causal/narrative order rather than an explicit "first...then" sentence, so I flag it as a modeling assumption.

**2. Transport before hand-off.** "Deliver food from the kitchen to an inpatient room" cites two explicit locations (kitchen, inpatient room), implying movement. I added a transport task that must happen **before** the hand-off mechanism is chosen — sequential, because you cannot place/fetch a meal that hasn't arrived yet.

**3. Table vs. Tray — OR, not fallback.** "The food can be delivered into a room table... **or** the food can be fetched from the robot's tray" is a plain alternative (explicit "or" particle), with no stated priority or failure-triggered order between the two — so this is an **OR decomposition**, not a fallback. I used **parallel** runtime because nothing forces an order between the two (choice depends on robot skill, not on a failed attempt).

**4. Who fetches from the tray — OR, not fallback.** "the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot" is an enumerated list joined by "or," with no stated attempt order ("try inpatient first, then companion..."). The text only explains *why* alternatives exist (unavailability), not *in what order* they're tried. Per the fallback rule, since the order is not explicitly stated, I must **not** use FALLBACK here — I used **OR + parallel**.

**5. Availability information — Query goal, sequenced before retrieval.** "The information... can be obtained based on the inpatient record... and the presence of a companion... subject to uncertainty" is explicitly knowledge-gathering, not action — a textbook **Query goal**. It logically must happen before the robot proceeds with a specific retrieval agent, so I sequenced it before the OR choice.

**6. Indicate meal → Alert wrong meal = genuine FALLBACK.** This is the one clear case where the description gives a normal behavior ("the robot should indicate which meal should be retrieved") followed by an alternative behavior explicitly triggered by a failure condition ("alert **if a wrong meal is retrieved**"). This matches the FALLBACK definition precisely, so I used `FALLBACK(Indicate, Alert)` with the mandatory AND relation between the two.

**7. Tracking retrieval.** "track when and where each meal was retrieved" applies generally (not only to human retrieval), so I placed it as a logging task sibling to the retrieval-agent goal, sequenced **after** retrieval (you log an event after it happens).

**8. Dish pickup — three OR alternatives.** "unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots, or with the cooperation of robots and a human" is explicitly OR, parallel (no stated priority).

**9. Door opening.** "Opening the room door can involve cooperation with another robot or a human" is another explicit OR/parallel pair. The door-signal query ("that person can signal if they can open the door") is a genuine Query-like input needed by the robot, mirroring the capability-check pattern used for tray retrieval. I sequenced door access before dish retrieval as the most defensible reading, though the text doesn't pin this down with full certainty — flagged below.

**10. Zero-robot exclusions.** I deliberately excluded: the kitchen issuing a delivery request, the patient placing dishes on the cart, and a person calling a robot — these are performed by humans/external systems, not robots, so per the rules they cannot appear as nodes (only the robot's *response* to these stimuli, e.g. querying the door signal, is included).

**Key assumptions flagged for review:**
- G1 and G11→G14 sequencing (door-before-retrieval) are logical inferences, not explicit statements — you may prefer "parallel" if your intended architecture treats these as independent.
- AT17 (robot+human dish cooperation) uses plural "robots" ambiguously; I modeled it as a range [1,2], inferring the upper bound from the "two robots" alternative mentioned in the same sentence.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G11 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Dish pickup presupposes a prior delivery (dishes get dirty only after a meal is delivered/consumed); the text also narrates delivery before pickup ("At pickup...").

- **G2 -> AT1, G3 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The meal must physically arrive in the room (AT1) before any hand-off mechanism (G3) can occur.

- **G3 -> AT2, G4 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "delivered into a room table... **or** fetched from the robot's tray" is an explicit either/or with no stated priority.

- **G4 -> G5, G6 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Capability/availability must be known (G5) before the retrieval is actually carried out (G6); the uncertainty described is resolved first.

- **G5 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both information sources (record, companion presence) are named together with no stated order and can be checked concurrently.

- **G6 -> G7, AT11 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: You can only log "when and where" a retrieval happened after the retrieval itself occurs.

- **G7 -> G8, AT10 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, **or another robot**" — human vs. robot cooperation are explicit alternatives, no priority stated.

- **G8 -> G9, G10 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: A specific human retriever must be engaged (G9) before the robot can monitor/ensure correctness of that retrieval (G10).

- **G9 -> AT5, AT6, AT7 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The three human agents are listed as a plain enumerated alternative ("the inpatient, a companion, a nurse"), no order given.

- **G10 -> AT8, AT9 [AND] FALLBACK(AT8, AT9)**
  - **Justification**: Indicating the meal is the normal behavior; alerting is the explicitly failure-triggered alternative ("**if** a wrong meal is retrieved"). Per the rules, FALLBACK mandates an AND relation between its two operands.

- **G11 -> G12, G14 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Door access is treated as a precondition for reaching/retrieving the dishes (reasoned inference; flagged as an assumption since the text doesn't state this order explicitly).

- **G12 -> AT12, G13 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The robot must first learn whether the person can/will open the door (AT12) before selecting the appropriate opening-cooperation mode (G13).

- **G13 -> AT13, AT14 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "can involve cooperation with another robot **or** a human" — explicit alternative, no priority.

- **G14 -> AT15, AT16, AT17 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots, or with the cooperation of robots and a human" — three explicit alternatives, no stated priority or failure-condition between them.
