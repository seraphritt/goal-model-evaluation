# Mission Analysis: Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Retrieval Multi‑Robot System

## Part 1 — Step‑by‑Step Reasoning

**Step 1 — Identify the top-level missions.**
The description names two clearly separable responsibilities of the system: (a) *deliver food from the kitchen to the room* and (b) *retrieve dirty dishes from the room*. Nothing in the text says one must wait for the other — they are triggered by different events (a kitchen request vs. a room call) — so I treat them as two AND-required sub-missions of one root goal, executed **in parallel** (`#`), not sequentially.

**Step 2 — Find explicit alternatives → OR decompositions.**
The text uses explicit "or" constructs twice at the method level:
- "delivered into a room table... **or**... fetched from the robot's tray" → OR.
- "cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, **or** another robot" → I split this first into *person* vs *robot* cooperation (a natural grouping the text itself makes by separating "the inpatient, a companion, a nurse" from "another robot"), then further OR among the three humans.
- "unassisted, with... two robots, **or**... robots and a human" → OR (dish retrieval).
- "cooperation with another robot **or** a human" (door) → OR.

**Step 3 — Find information-gathering (uncertain) goals → Query goals.**
Two places explicitly describe *obtaining information*, not acting:
- capability of inpatient/companion/nurse to retrieve food, "based on the inpatient record... subject to uncertainty."
- whether the caller "can open the door" (a signal, not an action).
These must precede the decision/action they inform, so they are placed as **sequential prerequisites**, not as OR alternatives to the actions they enable.

**Step 4 — Find conditional/failure-triggered behavior → FALLBACK.**
Only one place has the normal-behavior/exception-behavior pattern required by the FALLBACK rule: *"The robot should generate an alert **if** the meal retrieval is **not performed correctly**."* Normal path = retrieve (and track); exception path = alert. Everything else (uncertainty about availability, door cooperation) is *described as a possibility*, not as an explicit first-attempt/then-alternative order, so FALLBACK was **not** applied there — only OR.

**Step 5 — Find sequential (but not fallback) dependencies.**
Two places need "do X then Y" even though no explicit failure/condition is stated:
- "indicate which meal should be retrieved" must logically precede the person's retrieval action.
- Receiving the call/door-signal must precede opening the door, which must precede retrieving dishes (a physical necessity of entering a room).
These are modeled with `;` rather than FALLBACK, because the text gives no "if it fails, do the other" structure — it gives a process order.

**Step 6 — Strip non-robot elements and avoid redundancy.**
"The robot can carry multiple meals" is a *capability/attribute*, not an intention — excluded as a node, but used to justify the "Indicate Meal" goal. The “order-by-order” trigger and “kitchen delivery request” are context, not a distinct robot-performed intention beyond what G2 already captures — excluded as a separate node to avoid redundancy. All four cooperator types for meal retrieval are kept distinct because they denote materially different operational tasks (different robot counts / different parties), not redundant intentions.

**Step 7 — Map leaf goals to Tasks with Location and Robot Number.**
Every concrete, physically executable action (placing food, querying a record, indicating, retrieving with X, opening a door, recording, alerting) becomes a Task. Locations are restricted to what the text names: **kitchen**, **inpatient room** (incl. room table / room door). Robot numbers are derived from the cooperation description: a human cooperator → 1 robot; "another robot" / "two robots" → 2 robots; "unassisted" → 1 robot.

## Part 6 — Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G13 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The mission names two independent, continuously-provided services (meal delivery and dish retrieval), each triggered by its own event (kitchen request vs. room call). Both are required for the full mission, and nothing ties their timing together, so AND + parallel.

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "The food can be delivered into a room table... **or**... fetched from the robot's tray" is an explicit alternative; fulfilling either delivers the order. No preference order is stated, so no fallback is used — `#` simply denotes that no obligatory sequence is imposed between the two alternative methods.

- **G4 -> G5, G6, G11, G12 [AND] (G5;FALLBACK((G6;G11), G12))**
  - **Justification**: All four are necessary facets of managing a tray-based retrieval: you must know who's available (G5) before attempting cooperation (G6); once attempted, either the retrieval succeeds and is logged (G6;G11) or it fails and triggers the alert (G12) — the explicit "if the meal retrieval is not performed correctly" condition is the textbook FALLBACK trigger. Per the rules, the fallback pair is AND-connected into the parent decomposition.

- **G6 -> G7, G10 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The text separates cooperators into persons ("the inpatient, a companion, a nurse") and "another robot" — two independent, mutually sufficient paths with no stated order of preference.

- **G7 -> G8, G9 [AND] (sequential: G8;G9)**
  - **Justification**: The robot must indicate which meal belongs to the inpatient before that meal can be correctly retrieved by a person — a necessary process order, even though it isn't phrased as a conditional/failure case (hence sequential, not fallback).

- **G9 -> AT4, AT5, AT6 [OR]**
  - **Justification**: "Cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, [or] a nurse" lists three alternative human retrievers; any single one fulfills the goal.

- **G13 -> G14, G15, G16 [AND] (sequential: G14;G15;G16)**
  - **Justification**: All three are required to complete a dish pickup, and physical/logical necessity fixes their order: the call and door-signal must be received before the robot can decide how to open the door, and the door must be open before dishes can be retrieved. This order is inferred from process necessity, not an explicit "if/then," so it is modeled as sequential rather than fallback.

- **G15 -> AT11, AT12 [OR]**
  - **Justification**: "Opening the room door can involve cooperation with another robot **or** a human" — an explicit two-way alternative.

- **G16 -> AT13, AT14, AT15 [OR]**
  - **Justification**: "The dish retrieve can occur unassisted, with the cooperation of two robots, or with the cooperation of robots and a human" — an explicit three-way alternative.

- **G3 -> AT1 [AND]**, **G5 -> AT2 [AND]**, **G8 -> AT3 [AND]**, **G10 -> AT7 [AND]**, **G11 -> AT8 [AND]**, **G12 -> AT9 [AND]**, **G14 -> AT10 [AND]**
  - **Justification**: Each of these goals corresponds to exactly one concrete operational step described in the text; the single task is necessary and sufficient to realize the goal, so the relation is AND by default (no alternative task is given, and none was invented).

### A few reflective notes on contestable choices
- I treated the **"indicate meal" goal (G8)** as applying only to person-cooperation (G7), not robot-cooperation (G10), because the text explicitly scopes it to "the case of a person retrieving the meal" — this avoids duplicating the intention across the OR branches.
- I scoped **tracking (G11) and alerting (G12)** under the whole tray-retrieval cooperation outcome (G6), not under each individual cooperator branch, since the text states these apply to "each meal... retrieved" in general, independent of which cooperator was involved — this is the non-redundancy principle at work.
- The **sequential orders I introduced without explicit "first/then" wording** (G5 before G6/G11/G12; G8 before G9; G14 before G15 before G16) are inferred from process/physical necessity rather than literal textual ordering language; I flagged this explicitly rather than silently using FALLBACK, since FALLBACK requires an explicit condition/failure-triggered alternative, which only genuinely applies to the alert case (G12).
