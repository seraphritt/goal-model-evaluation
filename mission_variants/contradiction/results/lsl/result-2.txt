# Mission Analysis — Lab Sample Transport & Processing

## Step 0 — Reading the mission critically (before building anything)

Before decomposing, I want to flag several judgment calls a mission designer has to make explicit, because the goal model's correctness depends entirely on them.

**(a) A direct contradiction in the requirements.**
> "Only authorized personnel or specific robots in specific locations should be able to open the locked drawers. **However**, any person or robot must be allowed to open the locked drawers at any location, regardless of authorization."

These two sentences cannot both hold — the second one, read literally, erases the access-control requirement stated one sentence earlier, and makes the word "secure" in "secure locked drawers" meaningless. I am **not** silently merging them. As the mission designer, I resolve this by modeling the **restrictive/authorized-access version**, because:
- it is consistent with the system's own stated purpose (*"secure locked drawers"*, *"logged securely"* — security/chain-of-custody is clearly a design driver of this mission);
- an access policy that allows *anyone, anywhere* is operationally meaningless and cannot be the intended behavior of a lab-sample chain-of-custody system.

I flag the second sentence as a **requirements defect that should be clarified with stakeholders** before implementation. It is not represented in the model.

**(b) The "zero-robot" exclusion rule changes two OR-looking sentences into single paths.**
The text contains two "X or Y" constructions:
1. *"the sample can be picked by a robotic arm **or** laboratory personnel"*
2. *"authorized personnel **or** specific robots... should be able to open the locked drawers"*

In both cases one branch (laboratory personnel / authorized personnel) involves **zero robots**, so per the instructions it must not appear in the model. The consequence is important: **neither of these becomes an OR-decomposition** — each collapses to the single robot-feasible branch (robotic arm picks; a specific robot opens the drawer). I actively looked for OR opportunities and found none survive the zero-robot filter — this goal model ends up being **entirely AND-structured**.

**(c) Other zero-robot (human-only) actions, excluded entirely:**
- "A nurse... can request a delivery" — pure human action, no robot involved in receiving/handling it is described, so excluded (it is the *trigger* for the mission, not a node in it).
- "The nurse inserts data about the sample in a track system" — excluded (human, zero robot).
- "Each sample... is identified with a barcode" — a precondition/attribute, not an action by any agent; excluded, but it motivates the later "scan barcode" task.

**(d) Inferred robot: the transport robot.**
The text never says the word "robot" for the floor→lab leg, but the mission is explicitly a multi-robot-systems problem, and *"lab samples should be transported..."* / *"a request for delivery"* is the mission's core statement. In this domain, "transport/delivery of a physical item between locations, requested on demand" is the canonical mobile-robot task. I treat this as a necessary, reasonable inference (not a fabrication) — otherwise the central sentence of the whole mission would have no robot and would have to be deleted, which defeats the purpose of the exercise. The **robotic arm**, by contrast, is explicit in the text.

**(e) No FALLBACK anywhere.**
FALLBACK requires an explicit normal-behavior/alternative-behavior pair triggered by a stated condition, failure, or threshold. No such conditional ("if X fails, then Y") appears anywhere in the description. So, correctly, **no FALLBACK operator is used** in this model.

**(f) Locations.**
Only two locations are explicitly cited in the text: **patient floor** and **laboratory**. I do not invent any others (e.g., "corridor", "charging station").

G1: Deliver Lab Sample to Laboratory  [AND] (G2;G4)
├── G2: Transport Sample to Laboratory  [AND] (AT1;AT2)#G3

│   ├── AT2: Deliver Locked Drawer to Laboratory

└── G4: Process Sample at Laboratory  [AND] AT5;AT6;((AT7;AT8;AT9)#AT10)

Numbering follows strict depth-first order, separately for Goals (G1→G4) and Tasks (AT1→AT10).

## Step 5 — Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G4 [AND] (sequential: G2;G4)**
  - **Justification**: Transport and lab-processing are both mandatory parts of the mission (the sample cannot be processed before it physically arrives, and arriving alone doesn't fulfill the mission — it must also be processed). They are complementary, not alternative, so **AND**. The order is explicit in the narrative (floor → lab → processing), so **sequential**, not parallel or fallback (no failure/alternative condition is stated).

- **G2 -> AT1, AT2, G3 [AND] ((AT1;AT2) # G3)**
  - **Justification**: Collecting and delivering the drawer are both required steps of transport (**AND**), and must happen in that **order** (you cannot deliver before collecting) — hence `AT1;AT2`. Monitoring elapsed time (G3) is a continuous concern spanning the whole collection-to-delivery interval rather than a step that blocks or follows transport, so it is modeled as happening **in parallel** (`#`) with the AT1;AT2 sequence — it doesn't physically move the drawer, it just observes the timeline.

- **G3 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] (sequential: AT3;AT4)**
  - **Justification**: Both timestamps are necessary to compute the required interval (**AND** — neither alone satisfies "track the time between..."). Recording the collection timestamp must chronologically precede recording the delivery timestamp (collection happens first in the real world), so **sequential**, not parallel — this is a factual ordering constraint, not a stated process choice, but it is logically forced by causality (you cannot log a delivery event before it occurs).

- **G4 -> AT5, AT6, AT7, AT8, AT9, AT10 [AND] (AT5;AT6;((AT7;AT8;AT9)#AT10))**
  - **Justification**: All six actions are required for the sample to be fully processed — none is optional or a substitute for another (**AND**). The drawer must be opened before the arm can pick (`AT5;AT6`), and the arm's sentence lists pick→scan→sort→load in that explicit order (`AT7;AT8;AT9`, sequential). Logging "where and when picked" (AT10) depends only on the pick event already having happened (AT6), not on the arm finishing scanning/sorting/loading afterward — so it can proceed **in parallel** with that sequence (`#`), rather than blocking or being blocked by it.

- **G1, G2, G3, G4 — no OR decomposition anywhere**
  - **Justification**: The two "or"-worded sentences in the mission text (*robotic arm or lab personnel*; *authorized personnel or specific robots*) each contain one zero-robot branch, which is excluded per the modeling rules. This leaves a single robot-feasible path in both cases, so no true alternative (OR) survives into the model. This was deliberately checked, not overlooked.
