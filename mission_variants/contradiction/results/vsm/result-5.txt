# Hospital Vital-Signs Monitoring Mission — Goal Model

## 1. Method & Key Reasoning (read this first — it explains the non-obvious choices)

Before building the tables, here is the step-by-step reasoning that shaped the tree. I'm making these decisions explicit because several parts of the description are ambiguous or even self-contradictory, and a goal model forces a binary AND/OR/FALLBACK choice where the prose doesn't.

1. **Top level is a nested FALLBACK, not a flat AND.** The mission has an explicit normal path ("visit every occupied room") plus two explicit condition-triggered escalations: "additional robots can be assigned... in case of impossibility" and "the manager must be alerted if no combination of robots can complete the mission." Per the FALLBACK rule, an explicitly stated normal-behavior/exception-behavior pair *must* use FALLBACK, and since it's binary, the two escalations nest: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(visit‑rooms, assign‑more‑robots), alert‑manager)`.

2. **"Fetch Occupied Rooms" was added even though it isn't a separate sentence.** "The robot should visit every occupied room" presupposes knowing which rooms are occupied. This is also exactly the worked **Query** example given in the prompt ("Fetch Occupied Rooms, queried information: room occupation"), which I take as a strong signal this goal belongs in the model.

3. **The two pre-entry checks (availability, battery) are written as parallel ("check X **and** Y") but had to become a nested sequential/FALLBACK pair.** A goal model is a *tree*: the "normal continuation" after a successful check cannot be the child of two different FALLBACK parents at once. I therefore had to pick an order between the two gates. I chose **availability first, battery second**, justified by economy of action: there is no reason to evaluate battery sufficiency (or trigger a recharge/reassignment) for a room whose patient isn't even available yet. This is a modeling necessity, not something stated in the text — flagged here for transparency.

4. **Contradiction in the source text:** *"If the patient is not available, the robot should come back in 5 minutes. However, if the patient is unavailable, the robot must wait at least 20 minutes before returning."* "Not available" and "unavailable" are the same condition described twice with two different durations (5 min vs. ≥20 min). Rather than silently pick one (which would hide a real spec defect from you), I modeled both as an **OR** of two alternative wait-durations and flagged the inconsistency — you should resolve this with the domain stakeholders.

5. **"High-risk patients checked in the first 15 minutes" was modeled as a scheduling *task*, not a duplicated goal subtree.** Duplicating the entire "visit room" process into a "high-risk" copy and a "regular" copy would violate the no-redundant-goal rule (same intention, two nodes) and bloat the tree. Instead, a single `AT: Schedule Room-Visit Priority` task orders the shared "occupied rooms" collection so high-risk rooms are visited first — this is still a real action performed by the robot/coordinator (satisfies the zero-robot-action rule) without duplicating intention.

6. **Condition-specific vital signs (infectious / post-surgical / diabetic) are modeled as OR, not AND**, because which branch applies is gated by the patient's diagnosis — fulfilling the applicable branch is what's required for *this* patient. (Limitation, flagged honestly: a patient with *two* qualifying conditions, e.g. post-surgical **and** diabetic, would in reality need two branches satisfied; a strict OR under-represents that edge case. A richer "guarded-AND" operator isn't in the allowed operator set, so OR is the closest fit.) Within each branch, the listed signs are AND (jointly mandatory) and parallel in runtime (no order stated).

7. **Heart rate and blood-oxygenation appear as separate task IDs under two different goals** (infectious-disease branch and post-surgical branch). This isn't redundant *goal* duplication (the rule explicitly scopes redundancy to goals with the same intention) — these are two different tasks in two different execution contexts, and a tree node can only have one parent.

8. **Sterilization vs. actuator disinfection are two distinct hygiene requirements, scoped differently.** Sterilization is explicitly tied to leaving an infectious-disease room, so it lives only inside that branch, sequential after the four measurements. Actuator disinfection is a general rule for *any* patient-contact duty, so it's placed once, after vital-sign collection resolves (success or escalated failure), rather than being duplicated in every condition branch.

9. **Goal typing (Perform/Query/Achieve):** checks ("is the patient available," "is there enough battery," "what is the patient's identity," "what is the patient's status for the failed signs") are **Query** — they retrieve information without changing world state. Steps with a clear resultant world-state ("patient prepared," "vitals obtained," "battery restored and tasks reassigned," "additional robots assigned") are **Achieve**. Simple, single discrete actions with no further planning ("send alert," "mark done," "wait," "proceed") are **Perform**.

10. **Zero-robot-action check:** every leaf task is something a robot (the assigned robot, or a coordinating robot/fleet-scheduler) actually executes — nothing purely administrative was left in without a robot performing it.

Numbering below follows depth-first traversal, with Goals (`G#`) and Tasks (`AT#`) numbered independently.

## 5. Logical Relationships

- **G1 → G2, G25, G26 [AND][FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G2,G25),G26)]**
  - Justification: Normal mission flow is "visit occupied rooms"; two explicit, condition-triggered escalations ("additional robots... in case of impossibility," "manager alerted if no combination works") must nest as FALLBACK per the stated rule, and FALLBACK's exp1/exp2 are always AND-related.

- **G2 → G3, AT2, G4 [AND][sequential: G3;AT2;G4]**
  - Justification: You must know occupancy, then set a priority order, then visit — three necessary, explicitly ordered steps.

- **G4 → G5, G23 [AND][FALLBACK(G5,G23)]**
  - Justification: Normal path = verify & proceed; explicit alternative = wait & retry if unavailable.

- **G5 → G6, G7 [AND][sequential: G6;G7]**
  - Justification: "Before entering the room...check if available" states the check precedes the rest; battery gate was placed second as a necessary tree-structural choice (see reasoning §3).

- **G7 → G8, G22 [AND][FALLBACK(G8,G22)]**
  - Justification: Normal path = verify battery & enter; explicit alternative = recharge & reassign if insufficient.

- **G8 → G9, G10 [AND][sequential: G9;G10]**
  - Justification: Battery must be confirmed sufficient before the robot enters and performs the in-room tasks.

- **G10 → G11, G12, G13, G21 [AND][sequential: G11;G12;G13;G21]**
  - Justification: Identification must precede approaching the correct patient (multi-bed rooms); preparation precedes vitals collection; closing out follows collection — a natural, logically ordered pipeline.

- **G11 → AT5, AT6 [OR][parallel: AT5#AT6]**
  - Justification: "either by asking...or by scanning" — explicit alternatives, either suffices; no order of attempt is stated.

- **G12 → AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND][sequential: AT7;AT8;AT9]**
  - Justification: Explicit fixed order in the text.

- **G13 → G14, AT20 [AND][sequential: G14;AT20]**
  - Justification: Disinfection explicitly follows ("afterwards") the contact-bearing vitals-handling process, regardless of its outcome.

- **G14 → G15, G19, G20 [AND][FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G15,G19),G20)]**
  - Justification: Directly mirrors "failure of collecting...triggers questions...in case of non-response, alert" — textbook two-level FALLBACK.

- **G15 → G16, G17, G18 [OR][parallel: (G16#G17)#G18]**
  - Justification: Diagnosis-gated alternative branches; fulfilling the branch(es) relevant to the patient suffices. No stated order, so parallel.

- **G16 → AT10, AT11, AT12, AT13, AT14 [AND][(AT10#AT11#AT12#AT13);AT14]**
  - Justification: Four signs jointly mandatory, unordered among themselves (parallel); sterilization explicitly follows exiting the room (sequential).

- **G17 → AT15, AT16 [AND][parallel: AT15#AT16]**
  - Justification: Both signs jointly mandatory for post-surgical patients, no order stated.

- **G18 → AT17 [AND][-]**
  - Justification: Single required task; no combination operator needed.

- **G19 → AT18 [AND][-]**; **G20 → AT19 [AND][-]**
  - Justification: Each is a single operational action realizing its goal.

- **G21 → AT21, AT22 [AND][sequential: AT21;AT22]**
  - Justification: "mark it as done and proceed to the next room" — explicit order.

- **G22 → AT23, AT24 [AND][parallel: AT23#AT24]**
  - Justification: Both explicitly required ("recharge **and** the tasks need to be reassigned"), no order stated.

- **G23 → G24, AT27 [AND][sequential: G24;AT27]**
  - Justification: Wait, then return — explicit order.

- **G24 → AT25, AT26 [OR][parallel: AT25#AT26]**
  - Justification: Resolves the 5‑min/≥20‑min contradiction as alternative durations rather than discarding one silently.

- **G25 → AT28 [AND][-]**; **G26 → AT29 [AND][-]**
  - Justification: Each is realized by a single coordination/communication action.
