# Multi-Robot Goal Model: Hospital Vital-Signs Round

Before building the tables, here's my step-by-step reading of the description and the design decisions it forced.

## Step 1 — Identifying the top-level intention
The description gives two intertwined obligations: (a) a **periodic mission** ("every two hours, all patients' vitals checked") realized through (b) **visiting every occupied room**. I treat (a) as the root Achieve goal and (b) as its first major sub-goal, because "visit every room" is the *means*, not the *end* — the end is "vitals checked." A second, independent obligation threads through the whole text: the mission must finish **on time**, with a two-step contingency (add robots → alert the manager). That isn't part of the room-visiting logic itself (it's a meta-concern about feasibility), so I modeled it as a parallel sibling of "visit every room," not as a step inside it.

## Step 2 — Resolving the "high risk" / "first 15 minutes" placement
The only patient category the text explicitly ties to "risk" and to a hygiene reaction (sterilization) is **infectious disease**, so I'm assuming "patients in high risk" = "patients subject to infectious diseases." I flag this as an inference, not an explicit statement — worth confirming with the stakeholder.
I also avoided a trap here: if "prioritize high-risk patients" were modeled as *redoing* the whole per-room visit procedure, it would duplicate the main visiting goal. Instead, I scoped it to **scheduling** (identify which rooms are high-risk, order the visit queue so they're covered within 15 minutes) and let the *generic* per-room procedure actually execute the check. That's why "Prioritize High-Risk Patients" is sequential *before* "Perform Room Visit Procedure" — it produces the plan that the latter executes against.

## Step 3 — A genuine contradiction in the source text
> "If the patient is not available, the robot should come back in 5 minutes. However, if the patient is unavailable, the robot must wait at least 20 minutes before returning to the room."

"Not available" and "unavailable" describe the same condition, but the text gives two different wait times (5 vs. ≥20 minutes). Per the "avoid redundancy" rule, I modeled this as a **single** task rather than two. I kept the **20-minute** constraint because it's phrased with a stronger obligation ("must... at least"), but this is a flagged conflict you should resolve with the mission owner — I did not invent a resolution, I'm surfacing it.

## Step 4 — Separating "query" checks from "achieve" checks
Checking *patient identity*, *patient availability*, and *battery level* are information look-ups with no stated sub-procedure (except identity, which has two explicit alternative methods). I kept "Identify Correct Patient" as a Query goal (it has two operational alternatives worth modeling), but simplified "check availability" and "check battery" down to single tasks rather than artificially wrapping each in its own Query goal with only one child — a one-child "decomposition" isn't a real AND/OR split, and the template requires decompositions to have ≥2 members. This keeps the tree honest rather than padded.

## Step 5 — Vital-sign checks and avoiding duplicate task nodes
Heart rate and blood oxygenation are each required by **two** patient conditions (infectious disease *and* post-surgical). Rather than creating two separate "check heart rate" tasks (redundant intention), I created the task once (AT13, AT16) and reused it as a child under both condition-specific goals. This matches the explicit anti-redundancy instruction.

## Step 6 — Fallback vs. parallel vs. sequential, systematically
I only used **FALLBACK** where the text has an explicit "if/however" trigger describing a failure path (patient unavailable → reschedule; battery insufficient → recharge & reassign; vitals-collection failure → ask questions → alert; mission infeasible in time → add robots → alert manager). Everywhere the text lists actions with explicit order ("approach... announce... provide instructions"; "mark it as done and proceed") I used **sequential (;)**. Everywhere multiple things are jointly required with no stated order (four vitals for infectious disease; recharge + reassignment) I used **parallel (#)**, since parallel only asserts *possibility*, not obligation, of simultaneity.

## Step 7 — Hygiene tasks and the "zero robot" rule
Both "sterilize the robot" and "disinfect actuators" are robot-performed actions (robots act on themselves), so they stay in the model. I placed them in "Finalize Room Visit" since they logically close out the room visit (after contact, before leaving), even though the text mentions them in separate sentences.

# Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G14 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Visiting all rooms and guaranteeing on-time mission completion are both mandatory and operate concurrently (the completion-assurance logic monitors/reacts while visits are happening), not in a fixed before/after order.

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The priority schedule (G3) must exist before the visiting procedure (G4) executes against it; G3 only plans, it doesn't re-perform the visit.

- **G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: You must identify high-risk patients before you can schedule them to be visited first.

- **G4 -> G5, G8, G9, G13 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: The description gives an explicit chronology: checks "before entering" → actions "once inside" → implied measurement → actions "once the patient is checked."

- **G5 -> G6, AT5, AT6, AT7, G7 [AND]( FALLBACK((G6;AT5),AT6) # FALLBACK(AT7,G7) )**
  - **Justification**: Two explicit, independent "if" conditions trigger two separate fallback chains (patient unavailability → reschedule; insufficient battery → recharge & reassign); FALLBACK pairs are AND by definition, and the two chains run in parallel since nothing ties their order together.

- **G6 -> AT3, AT4 [OR](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Either by asking or by scanning" — explicit alternative methods, no stated priority between them.

- **G7 -> AT8, AT9 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Should recharge and the tasks need to be reassigned" — both required, no stated order.

- **G8 -> AT10, AT11, AT12 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Explicitly listed in order ("approach... announce... provide instructions"), and that order is also logically required.

- **G9 -> G10, AT18, AT19 [AND]( FALLBACK(G10, FALLBACK(AT18,AT19)) )**
  - **Justification**: Explicit nested conditional chain: collection failure → assessment questions → (if no response) → alert.

- **G10 -> G11, G12, AT17 [OR](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: These correspond to patient conditions that can co-occur (e.g., post-surgical *and* diabetic), so OR (at least one, possibly more) fits better than a mutually exclusive choice; no order is stated.

- **G11 -> AT13, AT14, AT15, AT16 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: All four vitals explicitly required together for infectious-disease patients, no order given.

- **G12 -> AT13, AT16 [AND](parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Both vitals explicitly required for post-surgical patients; tasks reused from G11 rather than duplicated, since they represent the same underlying intention (measuring heart rate / oxygenation).

- **G13 -> AT20, AT21, AT22, AT23 [AND](sequential)**
  - **Justification**: "Mark it as done and proceed to the next room" is explicit sequential order; disinfection and (conditional) sterilization are exit-time hygiene steps that must occur before the robot leaves/transitions.

- **G14 -> AT24, AT25 [AND]( FALLBACK(AT24, AT25) )**
  - **Justification**: Explicit conditional chain — additional robots are tried first; the manager is alerted only if that still isn't enough.
