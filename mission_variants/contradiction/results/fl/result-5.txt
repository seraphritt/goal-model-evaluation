## Step‑by‑Step Reasoning

**1. Identify the mission's independent top-level intentions.**
The description contains two largely independent service flows, each triggered by a different external event: (a) a *kitchen delivery request* triggers a food‑delivery episode, and (b) *someone calling a robot* triggers a dish‑pickup episode. Nothing in the text forces one to happen before/after the other, so I placed both under one root mission goal, related by **AND** (both are core responsibilities) with a **parallel (#)** runtime (no stated order between the two services).

**2. Decompose "Deliver Food" as a necessary sequence.**
To deliver an order the system must: know the order → get the meal to the room → hand it over. The text doesn't use "first/then" wording for this, but the process is logically ordered (you cannot hand over a meal you haven't transported, and cannot transport an order you don't have), so I used **sequential (;)**, not FALLBACK (no failure/condition is stated between these three).

**3. "Table vs tray" is an explicit OR.**
"The food can be delivered into a room table ... **or** the food can be fetched from the robot's tray" is a literal disjunction → **OR decomposition**. No order of preference or failure‑based switching is stated, so runtime = parallel (#), not FALLBACK.

**4. Tray retrieval is the most complex branch.**
It bundles: (a) a **query** for who is able/available to retrieve (explicitly tied to the inpatient record and "subject to uncertainty"), (b) indicating the correct meal, (c) the actual pickup — itself an **OR** over four named cooperators ("inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot"), (d) tracking when/where, and (e) a correctness check that triggers an alert.

**5. The wrong‑meal alert is the one genuine FALLBACK.**
"The robot should alert **if** a wrong meal is retrieved" is an explicit IF‑triggered alternative behavior — exactly what FALLBACK models: attempt correctness (verify), and only if that fails, raise the alert. Per the rules, the two FALLBACK children are AND-related.

**6. Dish collection mirrors delivery, with one hard constraint.**
"The robot must never pick them up inside the room" is not a separate goal — it's a constraint I enforced by fixing every dish‑collection task's **Location** to the corridor, never the room.

**7. Door opening is a separate OR gate from dish retrieval.**
The text lists cooperation modes for *retrieving dishes* (unassisted / two robots / robot+human) separately from cooperation modes for *opening the door* (robot / human). These are two different decision points, so I modeled them as sibling goals, each with its own OR.

**8. Zero‑robot filter.**
Every leaf task keeps at least one robot actively engaged (holding the tray, indicating, monitoring, navigating, pushing/opening), even when a human performs the final physical motion — so nothing purely human-only was added as a node.

**9. Symmetry for traceability.**
Every "alternative" (which cooperator, which door-opening actor, etc.) was given its own intermediate Goal (Perform) before bottoming out in a Task, so every branch is uniformly justified and query/achieve/perform typing stays explicit throughout.
