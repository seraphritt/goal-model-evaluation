# Goal Model Analysis — Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Collection Robot System

## Step‑by‑Step Reasoning

**1. Identify the actors and the "zero‑robot" boundary.**
The description mentions several non‑robot actors: kitchen staff (issue the delivery request), the inpatient, a companion visitor, a nurse, and "someone in the room" (issues the pickup call). Per the rule that *zero‑robot* goals/tasks must not appear in the model, I excluded the triggering events themselves ("a kitchen delivery request is issued", "someone calls a robot") as nodes — they are retained only as *activation conditions* in the justifications of the goals they trigger (G2 and G13).

**2. Identify the two top‑level missions.**
The text describes two independent capabilities: delivering food (paragraphs 1–9) and retrieving dirty dishes (paragraphs 10–12). Nothing in the text orders one relative to the other (they are triggered by different, independent events — a kitchen request vs. a room call), so I modeled them as an **AND** (both capabilities are required of the system) with **parallel** runtime under a root goal G1.

**3. Delivery mode is a clear OR.**
"The food can be delivered into a room table ... **or** the food can be fetched from the robot's tray" is an explicit alternative → **OR**, no stated precedence → parallel ("#"), not FALLBACK (no "try X, then Y on failure" language).

**4. Tray‑retrieval requires a determination step before the act.**
Because availability of the inpatient/companion/nurse is uncertain and must be "obtained based on the inpatient record ... and the presence of a companion," I introduced an explicit **Query goal** (G5) that precedes the **Achieve goal** of fetching (G6). This mirrors how, later, the door‑opening signal is also queried before the door is opened (G15 before G16) — I treated these two situations consistently.

**5. Avoiding redundancy in the retriever OR.**
The sentence "requires cooperation with the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot" could be flattened into four parallel OR tasks, but the subsequent sentence ("in the case of a **person** retrieving the meal, the robot should indicate which meal...") applies only to the three human retrievers, not the robot‑to‑robot case. Flattening would force duplicating "indicate meal" three times (redundant intention) or attaching it incorrectly to the robot branch. I therefore grouped the three human options under one intermediate goal ("Person‑Based Meal Retrieval," G7) that AND‑combines with "Indicate Meal" (G8), while "Robot‑to‑Robot Retrieval" (G10) stands as a separate, simpler OR‑sibling — this keeps each distinct intention represented exactly once.

**6. Minimum two children per decomposition.**
Several single‑sentence actions (e.g., "place meal on table," "indicate which meal," "track when and where," "signal if they can open the door," "alert if wrong meal") needed to be decomposed into at least two concrete, mission‑grounded steps to satisfy the AND/OR minimum‑two‑children rule. I only split where the text itself supplies two distinct sub‑elements (e.g., "when **and** where" → two tasks; robot‑to‑robot cooperation implies positioning *and* transferring; a "signal" implies the robot must ask *and* capture a response). These are flagged as *reasonable, minimal inferences*, never inventing new unstated mechanisms.

**7. No FALLBACK anywhere.**
I scanned explicitly for "first X, then Y if X fails/unavailable" language. The closest candidate ("some inpatients could be unable... a companion or nurse could not be available") describes *conditions used by the query* (G5) to pick the right OR‑branch — it is not a stated trial‑and‑error sequence ("try inpatient, then companion, then nurse"). So this remains an **OR** (G9), not a FALLBACK chain.

**8. ⚠️ Flagged contradiction.**
The text states both *"The robot should alert if a wrong meal is retrieved"* **and**, immediately after, *"if the wrong meal is retrieved, the robot must not generate an alert."* These two sentences directly negate each other and cannot both be true. I modeled the **affirmative** requirement (G12 – Alert on Wrong Meal Retrieval), since an alerting/safety behavior is the more plausible intended feature and the negation looks like a drafting error — but **this inconsistency must be clarified with stakeholders before implementation.**

**9. Uncertainty note.**
"This information is subject to uncertainty" qualifies the *data* used by G5 (Determine Capable Meal Retriever); it is not a separate actionable goal, so I attached it as a property/annotation of G5 rather than a new node.

**10. Locations.**
Only two locations are explicitly named in the text: **Kitchen** (origin, never elaborated with a concrete robot action) and **Inpatient Room** (where every described robot action — delivery, retrieval, door opening, dish collection — takes place). No kitchen‑side task is detailed in the text, so no task was invented there, per the "do not invent locations/actions" instruction.

## Notes, Assumptions & Open Issues

1. **⚠️ Contradiction requiring clarification**: The description states both that the robot *should* alert on a wrong‑meal retrieval and, in the very next sentence, that it *must not*. These cannot both hold. G12/AT15 implement the affirmative version as the (assumed) intended safety behavior; this must be confirmed with the mission stakeholders before finalizing the model.
2. **Zero‑robot events excluded**: "a kitchen delivery request" and "someone in the room can call a robot" are human‑originated triggers, not robot‑performed actions, and are therefore represented only as activating conditions (in G2's and G13's justifications), not as nodes.
3. **Minimal inferred tasks**: AT1 (navigation), AT5 (meal identification), AT10 (positioning), AT14 (comparison), and AT16 (prompting) are not verbatim in the text but are the minimal, physically/logically necessary steps implied by the explicit requirements they support (delivery to a room, indicating a meal among many, robot‑robot cooperation, detecting a "wrong" meal, and the occupant's door signal, respectively).
4. **Location scope**: Only "Kitchen" and "Inpatient Room" appear in the description. Since no kitchen‑side robot action is detailed, every task in this model is located in the Inpatient Room.
5. **Uncertainty**: Treated as a qualifier on G5's data sources rather than a separate goal, since no distinct action is described to "resolve" the uncertainty beyond the record/presence checks already modeled.

Since I don't have access to external references or a verification tool for this kind of modeling notation, you may want to sanity‑check the AND/OR/runtime conventions used here against your specific goal‑modeling framework's documentation before formalizing this further.
