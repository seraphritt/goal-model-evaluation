Below is a complete goal-model decomposition for the hospital food-delivery and dish-retrieval mission. I treat the mission as a **meal-service cycle**: first, the system responds to a kitchen delivery request; later, dirty dishes are retrieved from the inpatient room.

## 1. Design Rationale

The mission has two major objectives:

1. **Deliver the correct food order to the inpatient room.**
2. **Retrieve dirty dishes from the inpatient room.**

The model uses:

* **AND decomposition** when every child must be satisfied for the parent to succeed.
* **OR decomposition** when one alternative is enough.
* **FALLBACK(...)** when the system should try one strategy and use another if the first is unavailable, fails, or is uncertain.
* **Sequential runtime `[;]`** when one step depends on the previous one.
* **Parallel runtime `[#]`** is not heavily used here because most actions depend on prior information, physical navigation, or room access.
* **Query goals** for checking delivery requests, patient ability, companion/nurse availability, and door-opening signals.
* **Achieve goals** for desired world states, such as “food delivered” or “dirty dishes retrieved.”
* **Perform goals** for activity-oriented objectives, such as “coordinate human-assisted door opening.”

# 5. Logical Relationships and Justifications

## Root Level

**G1 -> G2, G16 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The full mission includes both delivering food and retrieving dirty dishes. Dirty dishes logically become available after food delivery, so a sequential relation is appropriate.

---

## Food Delivery Branch

**G2 -> G3, G4, G5, G6, G9, G15 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** A delivery cannot be completed unless the system obtains order context, prepares the payload, navigates to the room, gains access, transfers the food, and records the outcome.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2, AT3 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The robot must first read the kitchen request, then identify patient/room information, then assess whether a human can retrieve the meal. These pieces of information jointly determine the delivery strategy.

**G4 -> AT4, AT5 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The robot must pick up meals before arranging and identifying them on the tray.

**G5 -> AT6, AT7 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** Navigation must happen before the robot can position itself at the room door.

**G6 -> G7, G8 [OR][`FALLBACK(G7, G8)`]**

* **Justification:** Door access can be achieved either by human assistance or robot assistance. Since one access strategy is sufficient, the decomposition is OR. Fallback is suitable because availability is uncertain.

**G7 -> AT8, AT9 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The robot must request human help before entering or proceeding after the human opens the door.

**G8 -> AT10, AT11 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The delivery robot must request another robot and then coordinate door passage with it.

**G9 -> G10, G11, G14 [OR][`FALLBACK(G10, FALLBACK(G11, G14))`]**

* **Justification:** The food can be transferred by table placement, human tray retrieval, or robot tray retrieval. One successful method is sufficient. Fallback is appropriate because manipulation skill, patient ability, human presence, and helper robot availability are uncertain.

**G10 -> AT12, AT13 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** To place the meal on the table, the robot must first manipulate the meal from its tray and then place it safely on the table.

**G11 -> G12, G13 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** Human tray fetch requires both correct meal selection and recording of the retrieval event.

**G12 -> AT14, AT15, AT16 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** The robot must indicate the correct meal, monitor what is taken, and alert if the wrong meal is retrieved.

**G13 -> AT17 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** Tracking when and where the meal was retrieved is explicitly required.

**G14 -> AT18, AT19, AT20 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** Another robot must be called, select the correct meal, and transfer it to the recipient area.

**G15 -> AT21, AT22 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification:** Traceability requires updating the delivery log, and exceptions such as wrong-meal retrieval require notification.

* **Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval requires receiving pickup context, reaching the room, gaining access, collecting dishes, returning them to the cleaning area, and recording completion.

* **Justification:** The robot receives a pickup call and checks whether the caller can open the door. Both pieces of information affect execution.

| Whether a human can open the door          | G6, G17, G19     | Query/door signal plus fallback to robot assistance              |
| Whether correct meal is retrieved          | G12, G15         | Monitoring, alerting, and traceability tasks                     |
| Whether dishes can be collected unassisted | G22              | Fallback among unassisted, two-robot, and human-robot strategies |

This produces a goal model that is operationally useful for multi-robot mission planning while preserving the intentional distinction between **goals** and concrete **robot tasks**.
