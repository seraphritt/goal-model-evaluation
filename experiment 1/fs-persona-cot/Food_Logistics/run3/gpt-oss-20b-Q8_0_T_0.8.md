#### Overall Rationale

* **Sequential vs Parallel** – The top‑level goal is sequential (`;`) because the robot must acquire food before it can transport it, and it must transport before it can deliver. Within each sub‑goal, tasks that must all be performed are decomposed with AND; tasks that offer alternative strategies use OR with a FALLBACK runtime to capture the “try first, fallback to second” semantics described in the mission.
* **Query Goals** – Patient capability and companion presence are modeled as Query goals because the robot needs information from the patient record or room state before deciding on a delivery strategy. The results of these queries directly influence the subsequent perform actions.
* **Perform Goals** – All concrete actions (open door, place food, wait, etc.) are Perform goals because they are achieved by executing actions; failure to execute (e.g., door cannot be opened) leads to a Failed state.
* **Task Locations & Robot Count** – Tasks are assigned to the location where the action physically occurs (kitchen, room, corridor). The number of robots is set to 1 for most tasks, except the dish retrieval that requires two robots for unassisted lifting, reflecting the description that two robots may cooperate.  

This goal model captures the hierarchical structure, alternative strategies, and uncertainty handling required for a multi‑robot food delivery and dish retrieval mission in a hospital setting.
