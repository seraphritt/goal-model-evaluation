## 1. Mission interpretation and modeling rationale

The mission has two main service cycles:

1. **Food delivery cycle**: triggered by a kitchen delivery request, executed order-by-order, and completed when the correct meal is made available to the correct inpatient and the retrieval is tracked.
2. **Dirty-dish retrieval cycle**: triggered after meal usage or by a call from someone in the room, and completed when dirty dishes are collected and returned.

The most important modeling issue is that several parts of the mission are **alternative and uncertain**:

* Food may be placed directly on the table **or** fetched from the robot tray.
* Tray retrieval may be done by the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot.
* The inpatient/companion/nurse availability is uncertain and must be queried.
* Door opening may require cooperation with a human or another robot.
* Dirty dishes may be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or by robot-human cooperation.

Because of this, the model uses:

* **AND** when all child goals/tasks are necessary.
* **OR** when one child alternative is sufficient.
* **Sequential runtime `;`** when the mission text implies ordered phases.
* **Parallel runtime `#`** when information can be obtained simultaneously.
* **`FALLBACK(...)`** when the mission contains uncertainty or possible unavailability of collaborators/resources.

# 5. Logical Relationships

## Main mission decomposition

**G1 -> G2, G18 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: The mission includes both food delivery and dirty-dish retrieval. Food delivery happens first because dirty dishes only exist after food has been delivered/used. Therefore, the relation is AND and the runtime is sequential.

---

## Food delivery decomposition

**G2 -> G3, G4, G7, G8, G9, G12, G17 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: To deliver food, the system must prepare the request, determine context, assign/load the robot, reach the room, access the room, make the meal available, and close the order. These are all required and naturally ordered.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2, AT3 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: A delivery request must be received, registered, and validated before it can be executed.

**G4 -> G5, G6 [AND][parallel `#`]**

* **Justification**: The robot must know both meal/order information and handover context. These can be queried independently and in parallel.

**G5 -> AT4 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Querying the kitchen/order system is necessary to know which meal belongs to which inpatient.

**G6 -> AT5, AT6, AT7 [AND][parallel `#`]**

* **Justification**: The robot must check inpatient ability, companion presence, and nurse availability. These sources are independent and uncertain, so parallel querying improves responsiveness.

**G7 -> AT8, AT9 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: The robot must first be selected and then loaded with the correct meal or meals.

**G8 -> AT10, AT11 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: The robot must navigate to the room and then confirm that the room is correct.

---

## Door access for delivery

**G9 -> G10, G11 [OR][`FALLBACK(G10,G11)`]**

* **Justification**: Opening the room door may involve cooperation with a human or with another robot. Either solution is sufficient. Fallback is appropriate because the human may be unavailable, or the assisting robot may be unavailable.

**G10 -> AT12 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Human-assisted door opening is operationalized by the robot asking a human to open the door.

**G11 -> AT13 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Robot-assisted door opening requires coordination with another robot, so two robots are involved.

---

## Meal availability alternatives

**G12 -> G13, G14 [OR][`FALLBACK(G13,G14)`]**

* **Justification**: The food can be delivered onto a room table or fetched from the robot tray. One successful method is enough to make the meal available. Fallback is useful because table delivery requires a special manipulation skill, while tray retrieval depends on collaborator availability.

**G13 -> AT14, AT15 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Direct table delivery requires verifying manipulation feasibility and then physically placing the meal on the table.

**G14 -> G15, G16 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Tray delivery requires first selecting a collaborator and then supervising the retrieval to ensure the correct meal is taken and logged.

---

## Tray retrieval collaborator selection

**G15 -> AT16, AT17, AT18, AT19 [OR][`FALLBACK(AT16,FALLBACK(AT17,FALLBACK(AT18,AT19)))`]**

* **Justification**: The meal may be retrieved by the inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot. Since some inpatients may be unable and humans may be unavailable, these alternatives should be modeled as fallback options.

**G16 -> AT20, AT21, AT22, AT23 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: The robot must indicate the correct meal, monitor the retrieval, record when and where it occurred, and alert if the wrong meal is retrieved. These are all explicitly required by the mission.

**G17 -> AT24 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: The order-by-order nature of the mission requires closing/reporting the specific delivery order.

---

## Dirty-dish retrieval decomposition

**G18 -> G19, G20, G21, G24, G28 [AND][sequential `;`]**

* **Justification**: Dirty-dish retrieval requires knowing the pickup request, reaching the room, gaining access, collecting the dishes, and returning/logging them.

**G19 -> AT25, AT26 [AND][sequential `;`]**
