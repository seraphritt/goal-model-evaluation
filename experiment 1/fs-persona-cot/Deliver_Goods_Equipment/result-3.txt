**Goal Model – Multi‑Robot Resource Collection & Delivery**

## 1. Goal Tables  

| **Name** | **Text** | **Runtime** | **Goal Type** | **Relation** | **Justification** | **Target/Query (if any)** |
|----------|----------|-------------|---------------|--------------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **G1: Deliver resources to destination** | Bring all requested items from storage to the specified request location. | `;` | Achieve | AND | Collection must finish before delivery can start; sequential execution is required. | target: *All requested items are physically located at the destination.* |
| **G2: Collect required resources** | Acquire all requested items from the relevant storages. | `FALLBACK(G2.1, G2.2)` | Perform | OR | Battery‑low situations require an alternative path: either normal collection or re‑assignment. | – |

| **G2.1.1: Navigate to storage** | Robot moves to the storage containing the requested item(s). | `-` | Perform | AND | This is a leaf; execution is just a navigation action. | – |
| **G2.1.2: Request resource** | Robot sends a message to the storage with exact specifications and waits. | `-` | Perform | AND | Direct communication task. | – |
| **G2.1.3: Wait for retrieval** | Robot remains at the storage until the item(s) are retrieved. | `-` | Perform | AND | Must wait until storage confirms transfer. | – |
| **G2.2: Handle low‑battery during collection** | If battery ≤ 10 %, the robot must recharge and re‑assign the mission. | `;` | Perform | AND | Both actions (recharge & re‑assign) must be carried out. | – |

| **G2.2.2: Re‑assign mission** | The remaining collection work is handed to another robot. | `-` | Perform | AND | A new robot takes over the pending request. | – |
| **G3: Deliver resources** | Move the collected items to the requested location. | `FALLBACK(G3.1, G3.2)` | Perform | OR | Battery‑low during delivery demands an alternative path. | – |
| **G3.1: Normal delivery** | Standard delivery flow: navigate → deliver. | `;` | Perform | AND | Sequential execution is required. | – |
| **G3.1.1: Navigate to destination** | Robot travels from its current location to the request point. | `-` | Perform | AND | Navigation task. | – |
| **G3.1.2: Deliver resource** | Robot places the item(s) at the destination. | `-` | Perform | AND | Physical hand‑over action. | – |
| **G3.2: Handle low‑battery during delivery** | If battery ≤ 30 %, the robot must return to a checkpoint, re‑assign the rest, and trigger an alert on failure. | `;` | Perform | AND | All three steps must be performed in this order. | – |
| **G3.2.1: Return to checkpoint** | Robot goes to a predefined checkpoint to drop the partially‑delivered item(s). | `-` | Perform | AND | Safeguard against loss of items. | – |
| **G3.2.2: Re‑assign mission** | A second robot is notified to finish the remaining deliveries. | `-` | Perform | AND | Continuity of delivery after battery drop. | – |
| **G3.2.3: Trigger alert & report** | If the robot cannot return to the checkpoint, send an alert to the sector manager. | `-` | Perform | AND | Failure condition requires human intervention. | – |

## 4. Logical Relationships  

```
G1 -> G2, G3  [AND][;]  
  Justification: The mission must first collect the resources and then deliver them; the two phases are strictly sequential.

G2 -> G2.1, G2.2  [OR][FALLBACK(G2.1,G2.2)]  
  Justification: If the battery is below 10 % during collection, the robot must follow the low‑battery handling path; otherwise it follows the normal collection path.

G2.1 -> G2.1.1, G2.1.2, G2.1.3  [AND][;]  
  Justification: Navigation, requesting and waiting must occur in order.

G2.2 -> G2.2.1, G2.2.2  [AND][;]  
  Justification: The robot must recharge before handing the job to another robot.

G3 -> G3.1, G3.2  [OR][FALLBACK(G3.1,G3.2)]  
  Justification: Low‑battery during delivery triggers an alternative path; otherwise normal delivery.

G3.1 -> G3.1.1, G3.1.2  [AND][;]  
  Justification: Navigate to destination then deliver.

G3.2 -> G3.2.1, G3.2.2, G3.2.3  [AND][;]  
  Justification: Return to checkpoint, hand over the remaining delivery, and trigger an alert if the return fails.

G2.1.1 -> AT1  [AND]  
G2.1.2 -> AT2  [AND]  
G2.1.3 -> AT3  [AND]  
G2.2.1 -> AT4  [AND]  
G2.2.2 -> AT5  [AND]  
G3.1.1 -> AT6  [AND]  
G3.1.2 -> AT7  [AND]  
G3.2.1 -> AT8  [AND]  
G3.2.2 -> AT5  [AND]  (same re‑assignment task)  
G3.2.3 -> AT9  [AND]  
```

### Overall Rationale  

* **Sequential vs. Parallel** – The high‑level mission is inherently sequential: all items must be collected before any delivery can commence. Within each phase the steps are also ordered, but when multiple robots are available, the *collection* and *delivery* phases themselves can be parallelized (each robot handles a separate storage/destination). The `#` operator was not used because the tasks within a phase are strictly ordered; the `;` operator accurately captures this.  
* **Fallback** – Battery constraints create two natural alternative flows. In both the collection and delivery phases, if the robot’s battery drops below a threshold, it cannot continue safely, so a fallback path (re‑charge / re‑assign) is necessary. The `FALLBACK(exp1,exp2)` operator expresses this binary choice.  
* **AND/OR relations** – The decomposition of goals reflects the mission’s logical structure: all sub‑goals that belong to a phase must be satisfied (AND). Alternative handling of low battery is a distinct branch (OR).  
* **Tasks** – Each leaf goal is mapped to a concrete robot action. Tasks carry location and robot‑count information derived from the mission description.  
* **Goal Types** – The mission’s ultimate objective is to *achieve* a new world state (resources at destination). Collection and delivery flows are *perform* goals because their success is judged by the execution of actions, not by any external observation.

This goal model provides a clear, hierarchical, and justified blueprint for the multi‑robot system to execute the described resource collection and delivery mission.
