## Scope and modeling assumptions

I will model only robot-performed or robot-controlled parts of the mission. Therefore:

- Included robots:
  - A mobile delivery robot with secure locked drawers.
  - A laboratory robotic arm.
- Excluded from the goal model because they are not performed by robots:
  - Nurse collecting the sample.
  - Nurse inserting data in the track system.
  - Laboratory personnel picking samples.
- Human actions are treated as environmental inputs/preconditions, not task nodes.
- The only explicit alternative in the description is: “sample can be picked by a robotic arm or laboratory personnel.” Since laboratory personnel are not robots, the human alternative is excluded. Therefore, no OR decomposition is retained in the robot-only goal model.
- No FALLBACK relation is used because the description does not define a failure-triggered alternative such as “if robotic arm fails, laboratory personnel picks the sample.” It only states an alternative actor, not an ordered fallback behavior.

# 1. Mission decomposition rationale

The mission describes a robot-supported sample logistics workflow:

1. A delivery is requested and sample identity information exists in the tracking system.
2. The sample must be transported in a secure locked drawer.
3. The drawer must move from patient floors to the laboratory.
4. The delivery duration must be tracked against laboratory protocols.
5. In the laboratory, only authorized robots in authorized locations may open the drawer.
6. The robotic arm picks the sample, scans the barcode, sorts it, and loads it into the analysis machine entry-module.
7. The pickup location and time must be securely logged.

This leads to a mostly sequential workflow, with some parallelism:

- Transport and delivery-time tracking can occur in parallel.
- After pickup and barcode scan, sorting/loading and secure logging can proceed in parallel.

# 5. Logical relationships

## Top-level mission

- **G1 -> G2, G3, G4, G7, G9 [AND] [sequential: `G2;G3;G4;G7;G9`]**
  - **Justification:** The robot must first know the delivery request and sample identity, then secure the sample in a locked drawer, transport it to the laboratory while tracking time, open the drawer only after authorization, and finally process the sample with the robotic arm. All are required to satisfy the mission.

---

## Delivery request and sample identity

- **G2 -> AT1, AT2 [AND] [sequential: `AT1;AT2`]**
  - **Justification:** The robot needs the delivery request and the sample barcode/data. The request is handled first so the robot knows which sample information to retrieve or associate.

---

## Locked drawer preparation

- **G3 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] [sequential: `AT3;AT4`]**
  - **Justification:** The secure drawer must be available at the patient floor before it can be locked and verified for transport. Both preparation and locking are needed because samples must travel in secure locked drawers.

---

## Transport and delivery-time tracking

- **G4 -> G5, G6 [AND] [parallel: `G5#G6`]**
  - **Justification:** Physical delivery and time tracking are both mandatory. They can run in parallel because the elapsed delivery time is monitored while the robot is transporting the sample.

- **G5 -> AT5, AT6 [AND] [sequential: `AT5;AT6`]**
  - **Justification:** The robot must first move the locked drawer from patient floors to the laboratory, then confirm arrival.

- **G6 -> AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND] [sequential: `AT7;AT8;AT9`]**
  - **Justification:** To track delivery duration, the robot needs the collection/start time, then the laboratory delivery time, and finally the protocol comparison.

---

## Authorized drawer opening

- **G7 -> G8, AT12 [AND] [sequential: `G8;AT12`]**
  - **Justification:** The drawer must not be opened before authorization and location checks succeed. This follows the requirement that only authorized actors/robots in specific locations may open locked drawers.

- **G8 -> AT10, AT11 [AND] [parallel: `AT10#AT11`]**
  - **Justification:** Robot identity authorization and location authorization are both required. They can be checked in parallel because they are independent security properties.

---

## Robotic arm processing and traceability

- **G9 -> G10, G11, G12 [AND] [mixed: `G10;(G11#G12)`]**
  - **Justification:** The robotic arm must first pick and identify the sample. After the sample is identified, sorting/loading and secure logging can proceed in parallel.

- **G10 -> AT13, AT14 [AND] [sequential: `AT13;AT14`]**
  - **Justification:** The description says the robotic arm picks up samples and scans the barcode. The barcode scan occurs after the sample is picked.

- **G11 -> AT15, AT16 [AND] [sequential: `AT15;AT16`]**
  - **Justification:** The description states that the robotic arm sorts and loads samples into the analysis-machine entry-module. Sorting precedes loading.

- **G12 -> AT17, AT18 [AND] [sequential: `AT17;AT18`]**
  - **Justification:** The robot must capture pickup location/time before writing a secure log entry.

# 6. Notes on excluded alternatives and fallback

- The phrase “the sample can be picked by a robotic arm or laboratory personnel” contains an alternative.
- However, laboratory personnel are not robots, and the instructions require excluding goals/tasks not performed by robots.
- Therefore, the robot-only model includes only the robotic arm branch.
- This is not modeled as FALLBACK because the description does not say that laboratory personnel act only if the robotic arm fails.
- No FALLBACK relation is used anywhere because no explicit failure, exception, threshold-triggered alternative, or recovery order is described.
