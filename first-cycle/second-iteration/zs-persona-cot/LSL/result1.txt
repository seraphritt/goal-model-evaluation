## 1. Modeling assumptions and reasoning summary

1. **Robot-only filtering applied.** 
   The nurse’s sample collection, barcode assignment before collection, nurse data entry, and laboratory personnel pickup are **not included as tasks**, because the instructions say that goals/tasks performed by **zero robots must not be in the goal model**. They are treated as **external preconditions/events**.

2. **Robotic entities included.**
   - A **mobile delivery robot** transports samples in secure locked drawers from **patient floors** to the **laboratory**.
   - A **robotic arm** in the **laboratory** picks, scans, sorts, and loads samples into the **entry-module of the analysis machines**.
   - “Specific robots in specific locations” are modeled as authorized robot access to locked drawers at patient floors and laboratory.

3. **No FALLBACK relation is used.**  
   The description gives alternatives involving humans, e.g., “robotic arm or laboratory personnel,” but it does **not** define a failure-triggered order such as “try robotic arm first, then personnel if it fails.” Therefore, this is not modeled as `FALLBACK(...)`. The human option is also excluded by the zero-robot rule.

4. **Main mission intention.**  
   The mission is decomposed into four jointly required intentions:
   - obtain delivery/sample information from the track system;
   - transport samples securely in locked drawers;
   - perform robotic laboratory intake;
   - maintain traceability and protocol timing.

# 5. Logical relationships

## Top-level relationship

- **G1 → G2, G5, G9, G17 [AND] [`G2;((G5;G9)#G17)`]**
  - **Justification:** The mission is not satisfied by transport alone. The robot must obtain delivery/sample information, securely transport the sample, perform robotic lab intake, and maintain timing/logging. Data retrieval must occur first. Physical transport and intake are sequential, while traceability can operate in parallel with the physical process.

---

## Delivery and sample data

- **G2 → G3, G4 [AND] [`G3;G4`]**
  - **Justification:** The robot needs the delivery request and then the sample tracking data. The request identifies what must be delivered; the sample data supports barcode matching and timing verification.

- **G3 → AT1 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Retrieving the delivery request is the operational robot action that satisfies the query goal.

- **G4 → AT2 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Retrieving barcode, collection time, and protocol data from the track system operationalizes the sample tracking query.

---

## Secure locked-drawer transport

- **G5 → G6, G7, G8 [AND] [`(G6;G7);G8`]**
  - **Justification:** Secure transport requires authorized drawer access, locking/securing the sample, and transportation to the lab. These are complementary and sequentially dependent.

- **G6 → AT3 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** The robot must authenticate and open the drawer at the patient floor because drawer opening is restricted to authorized actors in specific locations.

- **G7 → AT4 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** The sample must be transported in a locked drawer, so the robot must close and lock it before movement.

- **G8 → AT5 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** The transport goal is achieved by moving the locked drawer from patient floors to the laboratory.

---

## Robotic laboratory intake

- **G9 → G10, G11, G14 [AND] [`G10;(G11;G14)`]**
  - **Justification:** The lab drawer must be opened before the robotic arm can pick the sample. Picking/scanning must happen before sorting/loading. All are required by the robotic-arm workflow.

- **G10 → AT6 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** The locked drawer must be opened in the laboratory by a specific authorized robot before robotic sample pickup.

- **G11 → G12, G13 [AND] [`G12;G13`]**
  - **Justification:** The description states that the robotic arm picks up samples and scans barcodes. Pickup precedes barcode scanning.

- **G12 → AT7 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** The robotic arm pickup task directly performs the pickup action.

- **G13 → AT8 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Barcode scanning obtains the sample identity required for sorting and traceability.

- **G14 → G15, G16 [AND] [`G15;G16`]**
  - **Justification:** The robotic arm sorts and then loads samples. Sorting determines the appropriate loading destination.

- **G15 → AT9 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Robotic sorting is the concrete action that satisfies the sorting goal.

- **G16 → AT10 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Loading the sample into the analysis-machine entry-module satisfies the final lab-handling state.

---

## Traceability and protocol timing

- **G17 → G18, G19 [AND] [`G18#G19`]**
  - **Justification:** Both timing compliance and secure pickup logging are required. They are not alternatives. They can execute in parallel with the physical transport/intake workflow.

- **G18 → AT11 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Monitoring elapsed time from collection to lab delivery operationalizes the protocol timing requirement.

- **G19 → AT12 [AND]**
  - **Justification:** Creating a secure log with where and when the sample was picked operationalizes the traceability requirement.
