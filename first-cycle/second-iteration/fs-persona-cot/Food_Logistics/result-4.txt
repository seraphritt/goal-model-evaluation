## Logical Relationships (Parent → Children)

1. **G1 → G1.1, G1.2, G1.3**  
   *Relation:* **AND (sequential)** – All three sub‑tasks must be completed in order before the mission is considered delivered.  
   *Justification:* The robot cannot plan or execute without knowing the order, and cannot execute before planning the route.

2. **G1.3 → G1.3.1, G1.3.2, G1.3.3, G1.3.4, G1.3.5**  
   *Relation:* **AND (sequential)** – Each execution phase must finish before the next begins.  
   *Justification:* The robot must open the door before transporting food, which must happen before delivering, then tracking, and finally dish retrieval.

3. **G1.3.1 → G1.3.1.1, G1.3.1.2, G1.3.1.3**  
   *Relation:* **OR (fallback)** – Try open alone first; if it fails, try human assistance; if that fails, try another robot.  
   *Justification:* Different door‑opening options exist; the robot attempts the simplest first.

4. **G1.3.3 → G1.3.3.1, G1.3.3.2**  
   *Relation:* **OR (fallback)** – Place on table first; if not feasible, provide tray.  
   *Justification:* Direct placement is preferable but may be impossible if the table is obstructed or the robot lacks manipulation capability.

5. **G1.3.3.2 → G1.3.3.2.1, G1.3.3.2.2, G1.3.3.2.3, G1.3.3.2.4**  
   *Relation:* **AND** – All four sub‑goals must be satisfied to successfully give a tray to the patient.  
   *Justification:* The robot must know if the patient can retrieve, request help, deliver the tray, and then indicate the correct meal.

6. **G1.3.5 → G1.3.5.1, G1.3.5.2, G1.3.5.3**  
   *Relation:* **OR** – Any of the dish‑retrieval modes can satisfy the goal.  
   *Justification:* Dish retrieval may occur unassisted, with robot‑robot cooperation, or with human help depending on situation.

7. **Each leaf goal → its corresponding task(s)**  
   *Relation:* **AND** – A leaf goal is achieved by performing the associated task(s).  
   *Justification:* The goal’s satisfaction relies directly on the successful execution of its task.

### Justification Summary

* **Perform** goals are used when the objective is to execute an action (e.g., open door, deliver tray).  
* **Query** goals fetch necessary information that influences decision‑making (e.g., patient retrieval capability).  
* **Achieve** goals set a target state (e.g., path established).  
* **OR** decompositions capture alternative viable strategies; **AND** ensures all required sub‑actions occur.  
* **Fallback** ordering prioritises the simplest or most likely successful method before attempting alternatives.  
* Tasks are tied to a single robot unless cooperation is explicitly required (indicated by `Number of Robots = 2`).  
* Locations are specified in hospital context, matching the mission description.  

This goal model decomposes the complex, multi‑robot kitchen‑to‑room food delivery mission into a clear, executable hierarchy with explicit decision points and cooperation mechanisms.
