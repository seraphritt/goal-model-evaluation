## 2.  Tasks

| **Name** | **Text** | **Relation** | **Location** | **Number of Robots** | **Justification** |
|----------|----------|--------------|--------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **AT1 – Plan Route to Storage** | Compute the optimal path from current position to the target storage, considering estimated waiting time and travel distance. | AND (to G2.1.1) | *storage* | 1 | Planning is mandatory before any movement; only one robot is needed. |
| **AT2 – Navigate to Storage** | Drive the robot along the planned route to the chosen storage. | AND (to G2.1.1) | *storage* | 1 | Execution of the navigation is a concrete, single‑robot action. |
| **AT3 – Send Request Message** | Transmit a request packet to the storage system with precise resource specification. | AND (to G2.2) | *storage* | 1 | Direct communication from the robot to the storage is required. |
| **AT4 – Wait for Retrieval Confirmation** | Block until the storage acknowledges that the resource has been prepared and is ready for pickup. | AND (to G2.3) | *storage* | 1 | The robot must wait; no other robot is needed. |
| **AT5 – Navigate to Delivery Location** | Drive from the current position to the destination point where the resource is to be delivered. | AND (to G3.1.1) | *delivery_location* | 1 | Straightforward navigation; one robot suffices. |
| **AT6 – Deliver Resource** | Physically hand the resource to the requesting agent at the delivery spot. | AND (to G3.2) | *delivery_location* | 1 | The act of delivery is performed by one robot. |
| **AT7 – Return to Checkpoint** | Drive the robot back to the pre‑defined checkpoint if battery is low. | AND (to G3.1.2.1) | *checkpoint* | 1 | A single robot must return safely. |
| **AT8 – Go to Recharge Station** | Drive the robot to the charging dock to replenish its battery. | AND (to G2.1.2) | *recharge_station* | 1 | Battery management is handled by one robot. |
| **AT9 – Trigger Alert & Notify Sector Manager** | Send an alert message and generate a status report to the sector manager upon failure to return to checkpoint. | AND (to G3.1.2.2) | *sector_manager* | 1 | The alert is a single‑robot communication event. |
| **AT10 – Assign Mission to Another Robot After Recharge** | After recharging, broadcast the remaining collection task to another available robot. | AND (to G2.1.2) | *recharge_station* | 1 | The original robot assigns the mission; only one robot is involved. |
| **AT11 – Assign Mission to Another Robot After Checkpoint Failure** | After failing to return, assign the remaining delivery task to a different robot (which knows the checkpoint location). | AND (to G3.1.2.2) | *checkpoint* | 1 | The new robot will take over; only one robot assignment step. |

> *Note:* All tasks are modeled as **AND** relations with their parent goals because each task must be executed for the goal to be satisfied. Only when a **fallback** is required does the relation become **OR** at the goal level (not at the task level).

## 4.  Logical Relationships

| **Parent → Children** | **Relation** | **Runtime** | **Justification** |
|------------------------|--------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **G1 → G2, G3** | AND | `;` (sequential) | Both collection and delivery must occur for overall delivery. |
| **G2 → G2.1, G2.2, G2.3** | AND | `;` | Navigation, request, and wait are all required. |
| **G2.1 → G2.1.1, G2.1.2** | OR | `FALLBACK(G2.1.1, G2.1.2)` | If battery <10 %, recharge; otherwise navigate. |
| **G3 → G3.1, G3.2** | AND | `;` | Navigation (or fallback) and delivery are required. |
| **G3.1 → G3.1.1, G3.1.2** | OR | `FALLBACK(G3.1.1, G3.1.2)` | If battery <30 %, return to checkpoint; else go to delivery. |
| **G3.1.2 → G3.1.2.1, G3.1.2.2** | OR | `FALLBACK(G3.1.2.1, G3.1.2.2)` | If returning fails, trigger alert. |
| **Each leaf goal (e.g., G2.1.1) → corresponding tasks** | AND | `-` | All listed tasks must be executed for the goal to hold. |

---

### Why this decomposition works

1. **Clear Phase Separation** – Collection and delivery are distinct phases with well‑defined pre‑conditions, making them natural AND‑children of the top‑level goal.
2. **Battery‑Aware Fallbacks** – The mission places strict battery thresholds; modelling them as **fallback** goals captures the decision logic in a formal way.
3. **Parallelism** – When multiple items are needed from different storages, the *Number of Robots* field in tasks allows a range (e.g., `[1,n]`), while the AND‑composition of goals still guarantees all tasks complete; the system can instantiate several robot instances concurrently.
4. **Failure Handling** – The alert goal and its child tasks encode the emergency protocol required by the description, ensuring the system can report when a checkpoint return fails.
5. **Extensibility** – Additional resources or storage locations can be added by creating new sub‑goals under *Collect Resources* and corresponding tasks, without altering the core structure.
