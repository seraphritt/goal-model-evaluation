## Logical Relationships (Tree Diagram)

```
G1 -> G2 , G3  [AND][parallel (#)]
   |
   +-- G2 -> G2.1 , G2.2 , G2.3 , G2.4 , G2.5  [AND][sequential (;)]
   |     |
   |     +-- G2.2 -> G2.2.1 , G2.2.2  [OR][fallback]
   |     |
   |     +-- G2.3 -> G2.3.1 , G2.3.2 , G2.3.3 , G2.3.4  [OR][parallel (#?)]
   |
   +-- G3 -> G3.1 , G3.2  [AND][sequential (;)]
        |
        +-- G3.2 -> G3.2.1 , G3.2.2 , G3.2.3  [OR][parallel (#)]

G2.1 -> AT1 [AND]
G2.2.1 -> AT2 [OR]
G2.2.2 -> AT3 [OR]
G2.3.1 -> AT4 [OR]
G2.3.2 -> AT5 [OR]
G2.3.3 -> AT6 [OR]
G2.3.4 -> AT7 [OR]
G2.4 -> AT8 [AND]
G2.5 -> AT9 [AND]
G3.1 -> AT10 [AND]
G3.2.1 -> AT11 [OR]
G3.2.2 -> AT12 [OR]
G3.2.3 -> AT13 [OR]
```

*Justifications of relations*

- **AND** is used when all child sub‑tasks or sub‑goals must succeed for the parent goal to be considered achieved (e.g., G1 requires both delivery and dish clearance).  
- **OR** is used when any one of the child options suffices (e.g., G2.2 offers table or tray delivery; G2.3 allows patient, companion, nurse, or robot to retrieve).  
- **FALLBACK** is employed to model the “try table first, otherwise tray” strategy for delivery.  
- **Parallel (#)** execution is chosen for G1 because meal delivery and dish clearance can be handled concurrently, improving throughput.  
- **Sequential (;)** is used in the delivery and dish clearance branches where logical ordering is required (e.g., query before deliver, deliver before wait for retrieval).  

This goal model provides a clear hierarchical decomposition of the multi‑robot food delivery and dish clearance mission, identifies the necessary decision points (queries), the operational actions (perform goals & tasks), and clarifies the coordination requirements (AND/OR decompositions) between robots, humans, and the environment.
