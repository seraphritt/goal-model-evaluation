## 1. Design rationale from the mission description

I model the mission as one end-to-end workflow for each requested sample or sample batch:

1. **Prepare the sample for trackable delivery**: barcode identification, track-system data entry, and nurse delivery request.
2. **Transport the sample securely and on time**: secure locked drawer transport from patient floors to laboratory while tracking collection-to-delivery duration.
3. **Control laboratory access and pickup**: drawer opening must satisfy authorization/location rules, then the sample is picked by one of the allowed actors, and the pickup event is securely logged.
4. **Process the sample for analysis**: the robotic arm scans the barcode, sorts the sample, and loads it into the entry-module of the analysis machines.

No **FALLBACK** relation is modeled because the description gives alternatives, such as “robotic arm or laboratory personnel,” but does **not** say that one is attempted only after the other fails.

`#` is used where the description does not impose an order and where activities or alternatives may be available without a required sequence. The OR relation still means that only one alternative is sufficient.

## 5. Logical relationships and justifications

- **G1 -> G2, G3, G5, G9 [AND][sequential: `G2;G3;G5;G9`]**  
  - **Justification**: The full mission requires preparation, transport, laboratory pickup, and processing. These phases naturally occur in sequence: the sample must be prepared before transport, transported before laboratory pickup, and picked before analysis-machine loading.

- **G2 -> AT1, AT2, AT3 [AND][mixed sequence/parallel: `AT1;(AT2#AT3)`]**  
  - **Justification**: The barcode must exist before collection. Data insertion and delivery request are both required preparation actions, but the text does not impose a strict order between them, so they are modeled as unordered/possibly parallel after barcode identification.

- **G3 -> AT4, AT5, G4 [AND][mixed sequence/parallel: `AT4;(AT5#G4)`]**  
  - **Justification**: The sample must first be secured in a locked drawer. Transport and time tracking then overlap because duration tracking concerns the interval between collection and laboratory delivery.

- **G4 -> AT6, AT7, AT8 [AND][sequential: `AT6;AT7;AT8`]**  
  - **Justification**: To evaluate collection-to-delivery duration, collection time is needed first, delivery time second, and protocol comparison after both timestamps are available.

- **G5 -> G6, G7, G8 [AND][sequential: `G6;G7;G8`]**  
  - **Justification**: The drawer must be opened under authorization rules before the sample can be picked. The pickup must occur before the system can log where and when it was picked.

- **G6 -> AT9, AT10 [OR][unordered alternatives: `AT9#AT10`]**  
  - **Justification**: The mission explicitly states that only authorized personnel or specific robots in specific locations should open locked drawers. Either alternative satisfies authorized opening. This is not fallback because no failure-based order is described.

- **G7 -> AT11, AT12 [OR][unordered alternatives: `AT11#AT12`]**  
  - **Justification**: The sample can be picked by a robotic arm or by laboratory personnel. Either actor is sufficient for sample pickup, and no primary/backup order is stated.

- **G8 -> AT13, AT14 [AND][sequential: `AT13;AT14`]**  
  - **Justification**: The system must first capture pickup time/location and then store that information securely.

- **G9 -> AT15, AT16, AT17 [AND][sequential: `AT15;AT16;AT17`]**  
  - **Justification**: The robotic arm must scan the barcode, sort the sample, and load it into the entry-module. Sorting depends on sample identification, and loading follows sorting.

## 6. Fallback analysis

No fallback relation is used.

| Candidate alternative | Modeled as | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized personnel vs. specific robots opening drawers | OR | The text says “authorized personnel or specific robots,” but does not say one is tried only if the other fails. |
| Robotic arm vs. laboratory personnel picking samples | OR | The text says the sample “can be picked by a robotic arm or laboratory personnel,” with no failure-triggered ordering. |

Therefore, using `FALLBACK(...)` would add behavior not present in the mission description.
