## 1. Modeling rationale and assumptions

I will provide explicit modeling rationale rather than hidden chain-of-thought. The mission naturally decomposes into four major intentions:

1. **Prepare the sample delivery request and sample identity data**: barcode identification, track-system data, and nurse request.
2. **Transport the sample securely**: locked drawer use, controlled opening, and movement from patient floors to laboratory.
3. **Handle the sample in the laboratory**: the sample may be picked either by a robotic arm or by laboratory personnel.
4. **Maintain traceability and timing compliance**: log where/when pickup happens and track collection-to-delivery time against lab protocols.

Important modeling choices:

- **No FALLBACK relation is used** because the description gives alternatives, but does not specify a failure-triggered ordering such as “try robotic arm first; if it fails, use personnel.” Therefore, alternatives are modeled as **OR decompositions**, not fallback.
- The laboratory pickup alternative is modeled as **OR** because the sample “can be picked by a robotic arm or laboratory personnel.”
- Drawer opening by authorized personnel or specific robots is also modeled as **OR**, because either authorized personnel or specific robots may open drawers, provided they are in specific locations.
- The robotic-arm process is modeled as **sequential** because the description explicitly orders the activities: the arm “picks up samples, scans the barcode, sorts, and loads” them.
- Monitoring/logging and timing compliance are modeled as parallel-capable with the physical mission because they occur alongside the physical delivery lifecycle.
