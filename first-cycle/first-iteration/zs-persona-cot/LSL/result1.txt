Below is a structured goal-model decomposition for the mission. I provide design rationale, but I do not expose hidden chain-of-thought; instead, I give the traceable modelling reasons that justify each goal, task, relation, and runtime structure.

---

## 1. Modelling interpretation and assumptions

| Item | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Mission scope | Transport lab samples from patient floors to the laboratory and support laboratory intake. |
| Main robotic agents | A mobile delivery robot with secure locked drawers, a robotic arm in the laboratory, and robot/software components able to log, query, and enforce access control. |
| Human actors | Nurse, laboratory personnel, authorized personnel. Human actions are represented as environmental/human alternatives when explicitly stated. |
| Locations used | Only locations stated in the mission are used: **patient floors**, **laboratory**, and the route **patient floors to laboratory**. |
| Fallback usage | No fallback relation is used because the description does not specify a normal behavior followed by a failure-triggered alternative. Alternatives such as “robotic arm or laboratory personnel” are modelled as **OR**, not fallback. |
| Parallelism | Logging and timing can occur in parallel with transport/intake because they monitor events that occur during the mission. Authorization checks for identity and location can also be performed in parallel. |
| Sequentiality | Barcode/request precedes delivery; securing precedes delivery; delivery precedes drawer access; drawer access precedes lab intake. Robotic arm actions are explicitly sequential: pick, scan, sort, load. |

# 5. Logical Relationships

## Top-level mission

- **G1 -> G2, G5, G6, G7, G12, G15 [AND][G2;((G5;G6;G7;G12)#G15)]**
  - **Justification:** The whole mission requires identity/request readiness, secure containment, delivery, controlled access, lab intake, and traceability/timing. These are complementary requirements, not alternatives. After request/identity are ready, the physical workflow and monitoring/logging can proceed in parallel.

---

## Request and identification

- **G2 -> G3, G4 [AND][G3;G4]**
  - **Justification:** A sample must have a barcode before collection, and the nurse must insert sample data/request into the tracking system. Both are necessary for a controlled delivery.

- **G3 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** The barcode-before-collection condition is directly stated and is not further decomposed into robotic operations because barcode assignment is described as a pre-collection condition, not as a robot action.

- **G4 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** The tracking-system record is directly stated as a nurse/tracking-system activity. It is modelled as a required state for the robotic mission.

---

## Secure drawer transport

- **G5 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][AT1;AT2]**
  - **Justification:** The drawer must be made available for deposit and then locked/kept locked. Both are required to satisfy “Samples are transported in secure locked drawers.”

- **G6 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][AT3;AT4]**
  - **Justification:** Physical transport from patient floors to laboratory and confirmation of laboratory arrival are both necessary for reliable delivery.

---

## Access control for locked drawers

- **G7 -> G8, G11, AT5 [AND][(G8#G11);AT5]**
  - **Justification:** Drawer opening is allowed only if the opener is authorized and the location is permitted. Authorization and location can be checked in parallel, and the drawer is opened only afterward.

- **G8 -> G9, G10 [OR][G9#G10]**
  - **Justification:** The mission explicitly says drawers may be opened by “authorized personnel or specific robots.” Either condition is sufficient, so this is an OR decomposition.

- **G9 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** This represents the authorized-personnel alternative.

- **G10 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** This represents the specific-robot alternative.

- **G11 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** The location constraint is directly stated: opening must occur only in specific locations.

---

## Laboratory intake

- **G12 -> G13, G14 [OR][G13#G14]**
  - **Justification:** The description states that in the laboratory the sample can be picked by a robotic arm or by laboratory personnel. One intake path is sufficient. This is not fallback because no failure-triggered ordering is specified.

- **G13 -> AT6, AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND][AT6;AT7;AT8;AT9]**
  - **Justification:** The robotic arm sequence is explicitly stated: pick samples, scan barcode, sort, and load into the entry-module. These actions are sequential and all are required for robotic-arm automated intake.

- **G14 [Leaf]**
  - **Justification:** Laboratory personnel pickup is explicitly allowed, but the action is performed by human personnel rather than robots. It is therefore represented as a leaf alternative in the goal model.

---

## Traceability and protocol timing

- **G15 -> G16, G19 [AND][G16#G19]**
  - **Justification:** Secure pickup logging and collection-to-delivery time tracking are both required by the mission. They can be monitored in parallel during the mission.

- **G16 -> G17, G18, AT10 [AND][(G17#G18);AT10]**
  - **Justification:** The secure log must contain both where and when the sample was picked. Location and time can be obtained in parallel, and the log is written afterward.

- **G19 -> G20, G21, AT11 [AND][(G20#G21);AT11]**
  - **Justification:** To check protocol compliance, the system needs both the collection timestamp and laboratory delivery timestamp. Once both are available, it can compute and evaluate the elapsed time.

# 6. Why no FALLBACK relation is used

No mission statement says that one behavior should be attempted first and another only if it fails. For example:

- “robotic arm or laboratory personnel” is an explicit alternative, but no priority/failure order is stated.
- “authorized personnel or specific robots” is an authorization alternative, not a failure recovery behavior.

Therefore, the correct modelling construct is **OR decomposition**, not `FALLBACK(...)`.
