# Goal Model: Periodic Vital Signs Check in a Hospital (Multi-Robot System)

## 1. Step-by-step reasoning

**Step 1: Identify the top-level intention.** The core sentence is "Every two hours, all patients' vital signs should be checked". This is a state the system wants to bring about in each cycle, so the root goal is an **Achieve** goal.

**Step 2: Separate the phases of the mission.** The description contains three distinct phases, which suggests a sequential root:
- *Knowing what to check*: occupied rooms, patients, risk level, required signs (a query).
- *Making sure the mission is feasible*: the assigned robot, additional robots, or an alert to the manager.
- *Executing the round*: visiting rooms and checking patients.

**Step 3: Break down the execution.** Several rules shape the round:
- High-risk patients must be checked in the first 15 minutes, so prioritization comes before visiting.
- Each room visit has entry conditions (patient available, enough battery). If they fail, there are two recovery paths: return in 5 minutes, or recharge and reassign tasks. This is a fallback structure.
- Inside the room the sequence is: identify the patient (scan or ask), approach, announce, instruct, measure, recover from failures, disinfect, mark as done.
- Leaving the room requires sterilization when the patient is infectious.

**Step 4: Find the alternatives (OR / FALLBACK) and the conjunctions (AND).**
- Scan the barcode *or* ask the patient is an OR, ordered as a fallback.
- Measuring failed → ask questions → if there is no response, alert the nurse/doctor is a fallback chain.
- Assigned robot → additional robots → alert the manager is a fallback chain.
- Everything else in a sequence is an AND.

**Step 5: Model conditional requirements as context.** The required vital signs depend on the patient's profile:
- infectious: heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, SpO₂
- post-surgical: heart rate, SpO₂
- diabetic: glucose

I model them as measurement tasks activated by context (the required-signs set computed in AT2). A patient with several conditions gets the union of the sets, and each sign is measured once.

**Step 6: Assign runtime operators, types, locations and robot counts.** Each choice is justified in the tables below.

**Design assumptions** (the description is silent on these):
- A room visit is instantiated once per room in `OccupiedRooms`, and "check patient" once per patient/bed in `PatientsInRoom_r`.
- One robot works inside a room at a time, to limit contamination and space conflicts. Several robots can work on different rooms in parallel when additional robots are assigned.
- Contact-based measurements (cuff, SpO₂ clip, glucose puncture, contact sensors) trigger the "disinfect actuators" rule.

The order is fixed: identify → prepare → measure/recover → disinfect and mark. All parts are required. |
| **G10: Identify Correct Patient** | Identify which patient is in the bed. | FALLBACK(AT12, AT13) | **Query**. Enquired information: identity of the patient in the bed (matches the expected patient record). | OR | The robot identifies "either by asking or by scanning the barcode". Two alternatives, either sufficient (OR). Scanning is tried first because it is automatic and unambiguous. Asking is the fallback when a barcode is missing or unreadable. |
| **G11: Prepare Patient** | Approach the patient, announce the procedure, and give instructions. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: the patient is next to the robot, informed of the procedure, and instructed. | AND | The description lists approach, announce, and instruct in order. The patient cannot be instructed before being approached, and all three are mandatory. |
| **G12: Collect Required Vital Signs** | Collect the required signs and handle any that fail. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: for each required sign, a measured value exists or the patient status was assessed or an alert sent. | AND | The failure-handling behavior ("failure of collecting any necessary vital signs should trigger…") can only start after measurement was attempted. Both parts are needed for the target condition. |

The signs are independent, and the description gives no order, so parallel execution reduces round time (important for the 15-minute rule). All required signs must be attempted, so AND. |
| **G14: Handle Failed Measurements** | For signs that could not be collected, assess the patient by questions, or alert staff. | FALLBACK(G15, AT24) | **Achieve**. Target: for every sign in `FailedSigns`, the patient status was assessed or the nurse/doctor was alerted. | OR | The description gives a primary path (questions) and an alternative for non-response (alert). Either satisfies the goal. It is only active if `FailedSigns` is not empty. |
| **G15: Assess Status by Questions** | Ask a set of questions about the signs that failed and record the answers. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: the patient answered and the status for each failed sign was recorded. | AND | Answers can only be recorded after being asked. It is an Achieve goal because "non-response" means the target was not reached, which triggers the fallback in G14. |
| **G16: Finalize Patient Check** | Disinfect actuators after contact and mark the patient as done. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: actuators disinfected (if contact occurred) and patient marked "done". | AND | The description says actuators must be disinfected "afterwards" (after contact duties), and "once the patient is checked, the robot should mark it as done". Disinfection comes first so that the next patient is not exposed. |
| **G17: Leave Room Safely** | Exit the room and sterilize the robot if the patient had an infectious disease. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: robot is outside the room and sterilized if the room contained an infectious patient. | AND | The rule says "when a robot comes out of a room where a patient subject to infectious disease is being treated, it must be sterilized", so sterilization follows exiting. Sterilization is context-activated (infectious patient present). |
| **G18: Recover from Unmet Entry Conditions** | Handle patient unavailability or low battery. | FALLBACK(G19, G20) | **Achieve**. Target: the blocked visit is either rescheduled for 5 minutes later or the robot is recharged with tasks reassigned. | OR | Two mutually exclusive recovery strategies exist, each tied to a cause (unavailable patient vs. insufficient battery). Only the one matching the cause is needed (OR). |
| **G19: Postpone Visit** | Come back after 5 minutes when the patient was not available. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: the robot returns to the room 5 minutes after finding the patient unavailable, and the visit restarts. | AND | "If the patient is not available, the robot should come back in 5 minutes." The revisit must be scheduled before it can be executed. |
| **G20: Recharge and Reassign** | Recharge the robot and reassign the pending tasks. | parallel (G21 # AT33) | **Achieve**. Target: the robot's battery is restored and its pending room tasks are reassigned. | AND | "The robot should recharge and the tasks need to be reassigned." Both are needed (AND). They are independent, so reassignment can happen while the robot charges, and other robots do not lose time. |
| **G21: Recharge Robot** | Move to the charging station and recharge. | sequential (;) | **Achieve**. Target: battery level ≥ the level required for the pending tasks. | AND | The robot must reach the station before charging. Both steps are required. |

## 5. Logical relationships

**G1 → G2, G3, G4 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The scope (which rooms, patients, and signs) must be known before feasibility can be assessed. Feasibility must be settled before executing. All three are needed for the target "all patients checked every two hours".

**G2 → AT1, AT2 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The room and patient list must be retrieved before risk and required signs can be derived from patient records. Both are needed.

**G3 → AT3, AT4, AT5 [OR][FALLBACK(AT3, FALLBACK(AT4, AT5))]**
- **Justification**: The description gives an escalation order: the assigned robot, then additional robots, then a manager alert when "no combination of robots can complete the mission". Any successful branch satisfies the goal.

**G4 → G5, G6 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The visit order must be established first so high-risk patients are covered in the first 15 minutes. Then the rooms are visited (G6 is instantiated per room in that order, and by several robots in parallel when additional robots are assigned).

**G5 → AT6, AT7 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: Rooms are sorted by risk, then time slots and deadlines are assigned according to that order.

**G6 → G7, G18 [OR][FALLBACK(G7, G18)]**
- **Justification**: The normal visit is preferred. If patient availability or battery fails, a recovery strategy (G18) replaces it. After recovery, the visit is re-instantiated.

**G7 → AT8, G8, AT11, G9, G17 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The robot must reach the room, verify conditions "before entering", enter, check the patients, and then leave. Skipping any step would violate the description.

**G8 → AT9, AT10 [AND][parallel (#)]**
- **Justification**: Availability and battery are independent checks and both must pass. Running them in parallel saves time.

**G9 → G10, G11, G12, G16 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The robot identifies the right patient in a multi-bed room, prepares the patient, collects signs, and then disinfects and marks the patient as done. Each phase depends on the previous one.

**G10 → AT12, AT13 [OR][FALLBACK(AT12, AT13)]**
- **Justification**: "Either by asking or by scanning the barcode". One method is sufficient. Scanning is preferred, and asking is the fallback.

**G11 → AT14, AT15, AT16 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The description lists approach, announce, and instruct in this order, and all three are mandatory.

**G12 → G13, G14 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: Failure handling only makes sense after measurement was attempted. G14 is trivially satisfied when `FailedSigns` is empty.

**G13 → AT17, AT18, AT19, AT20, AT21 [AND][parallel (#)]**
- **Justification**: The tasks are activated by the patient's context (infectious, post-surgical, diabetic). All activated measurements must be attempted, and they are independent, so parallel execution shortens the visit.

**G14 → G15, AT24 [OR][FALLBACK(G15, AT24)]**
- **Justification**: Failed signs first trigger the questions. Only "in case of non-response" is an alert sent to the nurse/doctor.

**G15 → AT22, AT23 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The questions are asked, then the answers are recorded. If the patient does not respond, G15 fails and the fallback in G14 is triggered.

**G16 → AT25, AT26 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The actuators are disinfected "afterwards" (after contact), then the patient is marked as done.

**G17 → AT27, AT28 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The robot exits the room and is then sterilized if the room contained an infectious patient (context condition).

**G18 → G19, G20 [OR][FALLBACK(G19, G20)]**
- **Justification**: Two alternative strategies exist for two causes (unavailable patient, low battery). Only the one applicable to the detected cause is executed.

**G19 → AT29, AT30 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The revisit is scheduled in 5 minutes, and after that time the robot returns to the room.

**G20 → G21, AT33 [AND][parallel (#)]**
- **Justification**: Recharging and task reassignment are both required. They do not depend on each other, so reassigning while charging avoids delays for the other rooms.

**G21 → AT31, AT32 [AND][sequential (;)]**
- **Justification**: The robot must reach the charging station before charging.
