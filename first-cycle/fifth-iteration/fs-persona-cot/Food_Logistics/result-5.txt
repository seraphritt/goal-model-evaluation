Below is a structured goal model grounded in the provided mission description, which defines a goal model as a hierarchical decomposition of goals into sub-goals and tasks with AND/OR relations . The mission scope includes food delivery from the kitchen to an inpatient room, tray/table handoff alternatives, uncertainty about human availability, meal tracking, wrong-meal alerts, door-opening cooperation, and dirty-dish retrieval .

## Modeling rationale

The mission has two mandatory high-level outcomes: deliver requested meals and retrieve dirty dishes. Therefore, the root goal is AND-decomposed into food delivery and dish retrieval. Delivery is sequential because the robot must receive a kitchen request, load/carry meals, assess retrieval feasibility, access the room, perform handoff, and then track/validate retrieval. Several alternatives are modeled with `FALLBACK(...)` because the description contains uncertainty: a patient, companion, or nurse may be unavailable, and some actions may require another robot or a human.

## Logical relationships

**G1 -> G2, G18 [AND][sequential: `G2;G18`]**
Justification: The system must both deliver food and retrieve dirty dishes. Dish retrieval is modeled after delivery because dirty dishes normally exist after meal delivery.

**G2 -> G3, G4, G5, G6, G9 [AND][`G3;G4;(G5#G6);G9`]**
Justification: Delivery must start from a kitchen request, then meals must be loaded and transported. Retrieval capability assessment can occur in parallel with room access preparation, because both are needed before the final handoff.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][sequential]**
Justification: The robot first receives the request, then identifies meal and room mapping.

**G4 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][sequential]**
Justification: Meals must be loaded before they can be carried.

**G5 -> AT5, AT6 [AND][parallel]**
Justification: Patient-record querying and helper-availability checking are complementary information-gathering tasks and can be performed in parallel.

**G6 -> G7, G8 [AND][`FALLBACK(G7,G8)`]**
Justification: Door opening may be achieved with a human or another robot. The fallback relation captures trying one cooperation mode and using the other if it fails.

**G9 -> G10, G11 [AND][`FALLBACK(G10,G11)`]**
Justification: The robot can deliver food to the room table if manipulation is available; otherwise, tray retrieval is used as an alternative.

**G11 -> G12, G13, G14, G15, G16, G17 [AND][`G12;FALLBACK(FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G13,G14),G15),G16);G17`]**
Justification: The robot must first indicate the correct meal, then use one available retrieval actor, then track and validate the retrieval.

**G13/G14/G15/G16 [fallback alternatives]**
Justification: The inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot are alternative cooperators for tray retrieval. Because patient and helper availability are uncertain, fallback is more appropriate than a simple OR.

**G17 -> AT23, AT24 [AND][parallel]**
Justification: Tracking the retrieval event and monitoring for wrong-meal retrieval are both required and can occur at the same time.

**G18 -> G19, G20, G23, G27 [AND][sequential: `G19;G20;G23;G27`]**
Justification: Dish retrieval begins with a pickup request, then room access, then dish pickup, then completion confirmation.

**G20 -> G21, G22 [AND][`FALLBACK(G21,G22)`]**
Justification: Door opening for dish retrieval can be human-assisted or robot-assisted.

**G23 -> G24, G25, G26 [AND][`FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G24,G25),G26)`]**
Justification: Dirty dishes may be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or by robot-human cooperation. Fallback captures escalation when a simpler retrieval mode fails.

**G27 -> AT37, AT38 [AND][sequential]**
Justification: The robot should record pickup completion and physically remove the dirty dishes from the room.
