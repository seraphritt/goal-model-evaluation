For runtime, `#` means parallel, `;` means sequential, and `FALLBACK(x,y)` means try `x`, then `y` only if `x` fails. I use `# over work_units` as shorthand for repeated parallel collect-deliver subtasks when multiple robots are assigned.

## Modeling rationale

The mission has a clear lifecycle: first understand the request, then plan robot allocation and storage order, then execute collection, then execute delivery. The strongest sequential dependency is that delivery cannot begin until resources are retrieved. The strongest parallelism appears when “multiple items are required from different storages”; then several robots may execute collect-deliver work units in parallel. The strongest fallback situations are battery-related: 10% in collection causes return to recharge and reassignment; 30% in delivery causes checkpoint handover and reassignment; failure to checkpoint the resource triggers alert and reporting.

# Key mission-design interpretation

The most important multi-robot design decision is to represent the mission as a set of **work units**, where each work unit corresponds to a resource bundle associated with one or more storage places. If there are multiple independent storage sources, the runtime expression becomes parallel:

`(G12[unit1];G21[unit1]) # (G12[unit2];G21[unit2]) # ... # (G12[unitN];G21[unitN])`

This directly captures the description: multiple robots may be assigned to parallel collect-deliver tasks to reduce the total mission completion time.
