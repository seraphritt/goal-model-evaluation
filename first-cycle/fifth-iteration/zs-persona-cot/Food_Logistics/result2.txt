Below is a goal-model decomposition using only mission locations explicitly mentioned: **kitchen**, **inpatient room**, **room table**, **robot’s tray**, and **room door**.

I treat **FALLBACK** relations as **AND decompositions**, as required by your definition: the system commits to a fallback execution policy, even though only the later branch is executed if the earlier one fails.

## Design rationale

The mission has two major service objectives: **deliver food** and **retrieve dirty dishes**. These are modeled as AND children of the root because the description requires both capabilities: “The system should deliver food…” and “Dirty dishes should be retrieved…”

The delivery process is mostly sequential: receive kitchen request, assess uncertainty, prepare the meal load, access the room, and complete handover. Some steps can happen in parallel, especially **querying patient/assistant availability** while the robot is being prepared.

The mission includes several alternatives: food can be placed on the room table or fetched from the tray; the door can be opened by a human or another robot; dishes can be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or by robot-human cooperation. These alternatives are modeled with **FALLBACK** when the text implies uncertainty or unavailability.

# Logical relationships

* **G1 → G2, G24 [AND][parallel: `G2#G24`]**
  **Justification:** The mission requires both food delivery and dirty dish retrieval. These are independent service requests and may be handled by different robots in parallel.

* **G2 → G3, G4, G7, G8, G12 [AND][sequential/parallel: `G3;(G4#G7);G8;G12`]**
  **Justification:** The robot first receives the kitchen request. Then feasibility assessment and tray preparation can occur in parallel. After that, the robot accesses the room and completes handover.

* **G4 → G5, G6 [AND][parallel: `G5#G6`]**
  **Justification:** Patient capability and helper availability are both needed under uncertainty and can be queried independently.

* **G8 → G9, G10, G11 [AND][sequential + fallback: `G9;FALLBACK(G10,G11)`]**
  **Justification:** The robot must first arrive at the room. Door opening is attempted with human cooperation first, then with robot cooperation if that fails or is unavailable.

* **G12 → G13, G14, G15, G20, G21 [AND][sequential + parallel + fallback: `G13;(G21#FALLBACK(G14,G15));G20`]**
  **Justification:** The correct meal must be identified before handover. Wrong-meal monitoring runs during handover. Table placement is attempted first because it avoids uncertain human fetching ability; tray retrieval is the fallback. Tracking occurs after the handover event.

* **G15 → G16, G17, G18, G19 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G16,G17),G18),G19)`]**
  **Justification:** The tray-fetch actor may be the inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot. Since patient ability and human availability are uncertain, fallback captures robust execution.

* **G21 → G22, G23 [AND][sequential: `G22;G23`]**
  **Justification:** The system must first observe which meal was retrieved before it can determine whether to alert.

* **G24 → G25, G26, G27, G28, G31, G35 [AND][sequential: `G25;G26;G27;G28;G31;G35`]**
  **Justification:** Dish retrieval begins with a call, includes the caller’s door signal, then robot arrival, door access, dish collection, and confirmation of removal.

* **G28 → G29, G30 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G29,G30)`]**
  **Justification:** If the caller can open the door, human cooperation is used. If not, another robot can cooperate to open the door.

* **G31 → G32, G33, G34 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G32,G33),G34)`]**
  **Justification:** Dirty dishes can be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or with human cooperation. Unassisted retrieval is attempted first because it uses the fewest resources.
