Below is a source-grounded goal model for the mission. I treat **food delivery** and **dirty-dish retrieval** as two major system capabilities because the description explicitly requires both meal delivery and dish pickup/retrieval from inpatient rooms.  I use **AND/OR decomposition** and runtime operators according to the provided definitions. 

## Modeling rationale

The mission contains two top-level objectives: deliver requested food orders and retrieve dirty dishes. These can be active independently across rooms, so the root runtime is modeled as **parallel**. Within a single delivery, however, the order must be received, meals must be transported, access must be obtained, a delivery mode must be selected/executed, and retrieval must be monitored, so the delivery workflow is mostly **sequential**.

Uncertainty appears around whether a patient, companion, nurse, or another robot can retrieve food from the tray. Because the description says this information is uncertain, I model these alternatives with **FALLBACK** rather than a simple OR whenever one option may fail at runtime.

## Logical relationships

**G1 -> G2, G17 [AND] [parallel: `G2#G17`]**
**Justification:** The mission requires both delivering food and retrieving dirty dishes. These are separate service capabilities and may occur concurrently for different rooms or requests.

**G2 -> G3, G4, G5, G8, G11, G12, G16 [AND] [sequential with fallback: `G3;G4;G5;G8;FALLBACK(G11,G12);G16`]**
**Justification:** For a delivery request, the robot must obtain the order, carry the meal, access the room, choose a delivery mode, perform delivery, and then monitor correctness and traceability.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND] [sequential: `AT1;AT2`]**
**Justification:** The request must be received before the robot can identify the meal, inpatient, and destination room.

**G4 -> AT3, AT4 [AND] [sequential: `AT3;AT4`]**
**Justification:** The robot must load the meal before it can transport it to the inpatient room.

**G5 -> G6, G7 [AND] [fallback: `FALLBACK(G6,G7)`]**
**Justification:** Door opening may be achieved with a human or another robot. Fallback is used because the availability of a human or robot helper can vary.

**G8 -> G9, G10 [AND] [parallel: `G9#G10`]**
**Justification:** The robot can check table-placement capability and tray-retrieval feasibility independently before deciding the delivery mode.

**G2 -> G11, G12 [AND] [fallback: `FALLBACK(G11,G12)`]**
**Justification:** Table placement is a direct delivery solution if the special manipulation skill is available. Tray retrieval is an alternative if table placement cannot be achieved.

**G12 -> G13, G14, G15 [AND] [fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G13,G14),G15)`]**
**Justification:** The inpatient is the most direct tray retriever, but may be unable. A companion or nurse may help, but may be unavailable. Another robot is modeled as the final fallback.

**G16 -> AT13, AT14 [AND] [parallel: `AT13#AT14`]**
**Justification:** Tracking retrieval and detecting wrong-meal retrieval are monitoring activities that can run together during handoff.

**G17 -> G18, G19, G22 [AND] [sequential: `G18;G19;G22`]**
**Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval requires pickup context, room access, and then the actual retrieval operation.

**G19 -> G20, G21 [AND] [fallback: `FALLBACK(G20,G21)`]**
**Justification:** For dish pickup, the caller may be able to open the door; otherwise, robot cooperation can be used.

**G22 -> G23, G24, G25 [AND] [fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G23,G24),G25)`]**
**Justification:** Dish retrieval can be unassisted, performed by two robots, or performed with human cooperation. Fallback captures escalating cooperation when simpler execution is not feasible.
