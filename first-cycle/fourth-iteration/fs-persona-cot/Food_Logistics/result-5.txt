Here is a source-grounded goal-model decomposition. I use **AND** where all children are required, **OR** where one alternative satisfies the parent, and **FALLBACK** where one option is attempted first and the next is used only if it fails, matching the provided definitions. The mission basis is the food-delivery, tray/table handoff, uncertainty, tracking, alerting, dirty-dish retrieval, and door-opening description.  The modeling rules require hierarchical AND/OR decomposition, runtime annotations, goal types, and task attributes such as location and robot count.   

## 1. Design rationale

The mission has two major operational branches: **food delivery** and **dirty-dish retrieval**. They are both required system capabilities, but in an operational hospital setting they may occur independently: kitchen requests trigger deliveries, while someone in a room may call a robot for dish pickup. Therefore, the root goal uses **AND decomposition** with **parallel runtime**.

For food delivery, the model separates: receiving the kitchen request, carrying meals, opening/accessing the room, deciding the delivery mode, and completing the handoff. The key uncertainty is whether a patient, companion, nurse, or robot can retrieve food from the tray. Because the mission says this information can be derived from inpatient records but is uncertain, I model it with **Query goals** followed by **fallback-based handoff alternatives**. 

For dirty-dish retrieval, the mission explicitly gives three alternatives: unassisted retrieval, two-robot cooperation, or robot-human cooperation. That is naturally modeled as fallback alternatives, because unassisted retrieval can be attempted first, then cooperative alternatives can be used if needed. Door opening is also modeled with fallback because it may involve a human or another robot.

## 5. Logical relationships

**G1 -> G2, G20 [AND][parallel: `G2#G20`]**
**Justification:** The system must support both food delivery and dirty-dish retrieval. These are separate request-driven capabilities: kitchen delivery requests trigger food delivery, while someone in the room can call for dirty-dish pickup.

**G2 -> G3, G4, G5, G8, G11 [AND][sequential: `G3;G4;G5;G8;G11`]**
**Justification:** An order must be known before it can be transported; room access is needed before in-room delivery; the delivery mode must be determined before the final handoff.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][sequential: `AT1;AT2`]**
**Justification:** Receiving the request alone is insufficient; the robot must also extract the order, meal, inpatient, and destination-room information.

**G4 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][sequential: `AT3;AT4`]**
**Justification:** Meals must be loaded before they can be transported to the inpatient room.

**G5 -> G6, G7 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G6,G7)`]**
**Justification:** Door opening can involve a human or another robot. Fallback is appropriate because if one cooperation mode is unavailable, the other may still allow access.

**G8 -> G9, G10 [AND][parallel: `G9#G10`]**
**Justification:** The robot can independently check tray-retrieval feasibility and manipulation-skill availability before selecting the delivery mode.

**G11 -> G12, G13 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G12,G13)`]**
**Justification:** Food may be delivered onto the room table by a manipulation-capable robot, or it may be fetched from the tray. Fallback captures trying one delivery mode and using the other if it fails.

**G13 -> G14, G19 [AND][sequential: `G14;G19`]**
**Justification:** A valid tray retriever must be selected before correct-meal indication, tracking, and wrong-meal monitoring can complete the tray handoff.

**G14 -> G15, G16, G17, G18 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G15,G16),G17),G18)`]**
**Justification:** The mission lists several possible cooperators: inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot. Since some may be unable or unavailable, fallback is more precise than simple OR.

**G19 -> AT16, AT17, AT18 [AND][mixed: `AT16;(AT17#AT18)`]**
**Justification:** The robot must first indicate the correct meal, then tracking and wrong-meal monitoring can occur during/after retrieval.

**G20 -> G21, G22, G25 [AND][sequential: `G21;G22;G25`]**
**Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval begins with a pickup request/context, then requires room access, then executes one of the retrieval modes.

**G22 -> G23, G24 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G23,G24)`]**
**Justification:** If the person in the room can open the door, human cooperation may be used; otherwise another robot can cooperate in door opening.

**G25 -> G26, G27, G28 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G26,G27),G28)`]**
**Justification:** Dirty dishes may be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or by robot-human cooperation. Fallback represents escalating cooperation when simpler retrieval is not feasible.
