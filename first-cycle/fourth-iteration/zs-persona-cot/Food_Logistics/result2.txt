## 1. Modeling Basis

A goal model is a hierarchical decomposition of goals into sub-goals and tasks, using AND/OR relations: OR means one child is sufficient, while AND means all children are required.  Runtime annotations use sequential `;`, parallel `#`, and `FALLBACK(exp1, exp2)`, with leaf goals marked `-` only when they have no children.  Goal types are classified as Perform, Query, or Achieve according to whether the node performs an action, retrieves information, or aims to make a target condition true.  Tasks are concrete operational actions with location and number of robots. 

The mission contains two main service outcomes: delivering food from the kitchen to an inpatient room and retrieving dirty dishes from the room. The mission also includes uncertainty about whether the inpatient or another human can retrieve food, alternatives for food transfer, multi-meal correctness monitoring, door-opening cooperation, and dish-retrieval cooperation modes.

## 5. Logical Relationships and Justifications

**G1 -> G2, G24 [AND][sequential: `G2;G24`]**
**Justification:** The complete mission requires both delivery of food and retrieval of dirty dishes. The sequence is appropriate because dishes become dirty after meal delivery and consumption.

**G2 -> G3, G4, G7, G8, G11, G23 [AND][mixed: `G3;(G4#G7);G8;G11;G23`]**
**Justification:** The delivery mission requires the order to be registered, feasibility to be assessed, food to be transported, room access to be obtained, food to be transferred, and the order to be confirmed. The feasibility query can run in parallel with transport after the kitchen request is known.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][sequential: `AT1;AT2`]**
**Justification:** The robot must first receive the kitchen request, then identify the meal and destination information needed for correct delivery.

**G4 -> G5, G6 [AND][sequential: `G5;G6`]**
**Justification:** Tray handoff feasibility depends on both patient ability and availability of possible cooperators.

**G8 -> G9, G10 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G9,G10)`]**
**Justification:** Door opening can involve either a human or another robot. Fallback is used because one cooperation mode may not be available.

**G11 -> G12, G22 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G12,G22)`]**
**Justification:** The system may first try tray-based handoff when cooperation is feasible. If tray handoff fails because no valid cooperator is available or retrieval is unsafe, table placement with manipulation capability is used as an alternative.

**G12 -> G13, G14, G19 [AND][sequential: `G13;G14;G19`]**
**Justification:** Tray handoff requires the robot to indicate the correct meal, obtain retrieval by a valid actor, and then monitor or record the retrieval.

**G14 -> G15, G16, G17, G18 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G15,G16),G17),G18)`]**
**Justification:** The possible retrieval actors are inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot. Because availability and ability are uncertain, fallback better captures retrying with another actor if the preferred one cannot perform the retrieval.

**G19 -> G20, G21 [AND][parallel: `G20#G21`]**
**Justification:** Tracking the retrieval event and monitoring for wrong-meal retrieval can occur at the same time during the handoff.

**G24 -> G25, G26, G29, G33 [AND][sequential: `G25;G26;G29;G33`]**
**Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval begins with a pickup request, then room access, dish removal, and confirmation.

**G25 -> AT17, AT18 [AND][sequential: `AT17;AT18`]**
**Justification:** When someone calls for dish pickup, that person can also signal whether they can open the door; both pieces of information are needed for planning access.

**G26 -> G27, G28 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G27,G28)`]**
**Justification:** Door opening for dish retrieval may be performed with human cooperation or robot cooperation, so fallback handles unavailability of one mode.

**G29 -> G30, G31, G32 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G30,G31),G32)`]**
**Justification:** Dish retrieval may occur unassisted, with two robots, or with robot-human cooperation. Fallback expresses escalation from the simplest mode to more cooperative modes.

## 6. Main Modeling Decisions

The **top-level decomposition is AND** because the mission is not complete unless both food delivery and dirty-dish retrieval are handled.

The **food-transfer decomposition uses fallback** rather than simple OR because the description introduces uncertainty about whether the patient, companion, or nurse can retrieve the food. A fallback structure captures attempting one operational mode and then switching if it fails.

The **tray-retrieval cooperator decomposition uses fallback** because the possible cooperators are not equally guaranteed. The inpatient may be unable, a companion or nurse may be unavailable, and another robot may be needed.

The **door-opening decomposition uses fallback** because the door may be opened with human cooperation or robot cooperation, depending on availability and signaling.

The **dish-retrieval decomposition uses fallback** because unassisted retrieval is the simplest mode, while two-robot or robot-human cooperation are alternatives when assistance is needed.
