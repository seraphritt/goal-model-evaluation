I modeled the mission as a single inpatient meal-service cycle with two sequential phases: delivery of the requested meal, followed later by dirty-dish retrieval. The decomposition below is grounded only in the supplied mission description: order-triggered delivery, table/tray handoff alternatives, uncertain human assistance, meal identification/tracking, dish pickup modes, and cooperative door opening. 

I cannot provide hidden chain-of-thought or private step-by-step reasoning, but I can provide the explicit modeling rationale and justification for every decomposition, which is what the tables below do.

### Modeling conventions

The source requires AND/OR goal decomposition and runtime annotations using parallel `#`, sequential `;`, and `FALLBACK(...)`; leaf goals use `-`.   One small formal gap exists: the runtime language has no explicit exclusive-choice operator corresponding to an OR decomposition. Therefore, for pure OR alternatives below, `#` means the alternatives may be **considered/enabled concurrently**, while the OR relation means only one branch needs to be selected and successfully executed. I do not interpret this as requiring two physical handoffs simultaneously.

I use `FALLBACK` only where a defensible execution preference can be derived. For room-door opening, human assistance can be known from the caller's signal, so human assistance is attempted first when indicated, with another robot as backup. The source explicitly states that FALLBACK-connected children use an AND decomposition.

# Logical relationships

**G1 → G2, G10 [AND] [sequential: `G2;G10`]**
**Justification:** Meal delivery occurs before dirty dishes become available for later retrieval. Both capabilities are required by the mission.

**G2 → AT1, G3, G4, G5 [AND] [`AT1;(G3#G4);G5`]**
**Justification:** A request triggers the mission. After acceptance, retrieving assistance information does not physically depend on transporting the meal, so the information query and transport can proceed in parallel. Handoff follows arrival.

**G4 → AT2 [AND]**
**Justification:** Transporting the meal from the kitchen to the inpatient room operationalizes the transport goal.

**G5 → G6, G9 [OR] [`G6#G9`]**
**Justification:** Tray retrieval and direct table placement are alternative means of delivering the meal. The mission states that food “can be delivered into a room table … or … fetched from the robot's tray.” 

**G6 → G7, G8 [AND] [sequential: `G7;G8`]**
**Justification:** Tray delivery requires a cooperating retriever, after which the robot supervises meal selection and records the retrieval.

**G7 → AT3, AT4, AT5, AT6 [OR] [`AT3#AT4#AT5#AT6`]**
**Justification:** Inpatient, companion, nurse, and another robot are alternatives; one cooperating actor is sufficient. 

**G8 → AT7, AT8, AT9 [AND] [sequential: `AT7;AT8;AT9`]**
**Justification:** The robot must indicate which meal to take, detect/alert an incorrect retrieval, and track when and where the meal was retrieved. These are complementary requirements rather than alternatives. 

**G9 → AT10 [AND]**
**Justification:** Table delivery is realized through the concrete manipulation action requiring the special robot skill.

**G10 → G11, G12 [AND] [sequential: `G11;G12`]**
**Justification:** The collecting robot needs room access before it can execute dish retrieval.

**G11 → AT11, AT12 [AND] [`FALLBACK(AT11,AT12)`]**
**Justification:** A human can open the door, and a caller can explicitly signal that capability. If the human-assisted opening cannot be achieved, another robot provides the alternative cooperation mechanism. This priority is an operational inference; the mission itself specifies the two mechanisms but not a strict preference. 

**G12 → AT13, AT14, AT15 [OR] [`AT13#AT14#AT15`]**
**Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval may occur unassisted, with two cooperating robots, or through robot-human cooperation. One successful mode is sufficient.
