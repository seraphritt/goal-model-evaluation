Below is a structured goal-model decomposition grounded in the mission description. I use **AND/OR decomposition** as defined in the prompt, and runtime annotations use sequential `;`, parallel `#`, and `FALLBACK(...)` as required.  

## 1. Design rationale

The mission has two main service obligations: **deliver food from the kitchen to an inpatient room** and **retrieve dirty dishes from the room**. Food delivery is triggered by kitchen delivery requests and must handle uncertainty around who can retrieve food from the tray. The model therefore separates request handling, transport, door access, delivery-mode selection, meal correctness, and logging. Dirty-dish retrieval is modeled as a second service because the description explicitly states that dirty dishes should be retrieved and that someone in the room may call a robot for pickup. 

A key design decision is to use **fallback** where the description gives multiple possible operational strategies under uncertainty: table placement versus tray retrieval, human versus robot door-opening help, and unassisted versus cooperative dish retrieval. This is different from a simple OR because fallback captures an execution preference: try one strategy first, then use another if the first fails.

# 5. Logical relationships

* **G1 → G2, G22 [AND][parallel `G2#G22`]**
  **Justification:** The overall mission includes both food delivery and dish retrieval. They are both required service capabilities, and they may be active independently.

* **G2 → G3, G4, G5, G8, G12, G19 [AND][sequential with parallel monitoring: `G3;G4;G5;G8;(G12#G19)`]**
  **Justification:** A food delivery must first obtain the request, then move meals, gain room access, assess tray-retrieval feasibility, complete the handoff, and monitor correctness/logging.

* **G5 → G6, G7 [AND][`FALLBACK(G6,G7)`]**
  **Justification:** Door opening may be achieved with either human cooperation or another robot. Fallback is appropriate because if one access strategy fails, the robot should attempt another.

* **G8 → G9, G10, G11 [AND][parallel `G9#G10#G11`]**
  **Justification:** Patient ability, companion presence, and nurse availability are independent information sources and can be queried in parallel.

* **G12 → G13, G14 [AND][`FALLBACK(G13,G14)`]**
  **Justification:** Direct table placement completes delivery when the manipulation skill is available. If that is not possible, tray handoff is the alternative.

* **G14 → G15, G16, G17, G18 [AND][nested fallback]**
  **Justification:** Tray fetching requires cooperation with one of several possible actors. The fallback sequence handles uncertainty in whether the inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot can perform the retrieval.

* **G19 → G20, G21 [AND][parallel `G20#G21`]**
  **Justification:** Tracking retrieval metadata and detecting/alerting wrong-meal retrieval are both required and can occur during the delivery handoff.

* **G22 → G23, G24, G27, G31 [AND][sequential `G23;G24;G27;G31`]**
  **Justification:** Dish pickup requires a pickup context, room access, physical retrieval, and completion recording.

* **G24 → G25, G26 [AND][`FALLBACK(G25,G26)`]**
  **Justification:** Door access for dish retrieval can involve a human or another robot, so fallback handles unavailable cooperation.

* **G27 → G28, G29, G30 [AND][`FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G28,G29),G30)`]**
  **Justification:** Dirty dishes can be retrieved unassisted, by two robots, or by robots with a human. Fallback encodes escalation from simpler to more cooperative modes.
