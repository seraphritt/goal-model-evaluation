Below is a source-grounded goal model. I interpret the mission as one **multi-robot inpatient food-service mission** with two required service capabilities: **meal delivery** and **dirty-dish retrieval**. Delivery is triggered by kitchen requests, while dish pickup can be triggered by someone in the room; both are stated in the mission description. 

I use **AND** when all children are required, **OR** when one alternative is sufficient, and **FALLBACK** when an alternative is tried only after another fails, following the provided definitions.  

## 1. Structured rationale

The mission contains four main design drivers:

| Mission element                                                                                            | Modeling consequence                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “Deliver food from the kitchen to an inpatient room”                                                       | A delivery-achievement branch is required.                            |
| “Order-by-order basis in response to kitchen delivery requests”                                            | A query/intake goal is needed before navigation and handoff.          |
| “Delivered into a room table” OR “fetched from robot tray”                                                 | Meal handoff is decomposed into alternatives.                         |
| Tray fetching may require inpatient, companion, nurse, or another robot, and this information is uncertain | A query goal is needed to assess who can retrieve the meal.           |
| Robot may carry multiple meals and must indicate, track, and alert wrong retrieval                         | Identification, monitoring, logging, and alerting tasks are required. |
| “Dirty dishes should be retrieved from the room”                                                           | A dish-retrieval branch is required.                                  |
| Dish retrieval can be unassisted, two-robot, or robot-human cooperative                                    | Retrieval mode is modeled as a fallback/alternative structure.        |
| Door opening can require another robot or human                                                            | Door access is modeled as a cooperation subgoal.                      |

# 5. Logical relationships with justifications

**G1 -> G2, G14 [AND][parallel: `G2#G14`]**
**Justification:** The system must support both food delivery and dirty-dish retrieval. These are different service flows triggered by different events, so a multi-robot system could handle them in parallel.

**G2 -> G3, G4, G8, G5, G9 [AND][sequential + parallel: `G3;(G4#G8);G5;G9`]**
**Justification:** The kitchen request must be known first. Transport and handoff-support assessment can proceed in parallel. Door access must be resolved before final handoff.

**G3 -> AT1, AT2 [AND][sequential: `AT1;AT2`]**
**Justification:** The robot first receives the request, then identifies the meal, inpatient, and room mapping needed for correct delivery.

**G4 -> AT3, AT4 [AND][sequential: `AT3;AT4`]**
**Justification:** The robot must carry the meal(s) and then navigate to the inpatient room.

**G5 -> G6, G7 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G6,G7)`]**
**Justification:** Door opening may involve a human or another robot. Fallback is appropriate because if human cooperation is not available, robot cooperation can be attempted.

**G8 -> AT7, AT8 [AND][sequential: `AT7;AT8`]**
**Justification:** The system first queries the inpatient record and then assesses uncertain availability of a suitable retriever.

**G9 -> G10, G11 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G10,G11)`]**
**Justification:** Table placement is direct but requires special manipulation skill. If that cannot be achieved, tray retrieval with cooperation is the alternative.

**G11 -> G12, G13 [AND][sequential: `G12;G13`]**
**Justification:** The robot must first guide correct meal selection, then verify and log the retrieval event.

**G13 -> AT11, AT12, AT13 [AND][mixed: `AT11;(AT12#AT13)`]**
**Justification:** Monitoring must occur first. Once retrieval is observed, the system can alert on wrong retrieval and record the event information.

**G14 -> G15, G16, G17, G20 [AND][sequential: `G15;G16;G17;G20`]**
**Justification:** Dish retrieval begins with a pickup request, followed by navigation, door access, and dish collection.

**G15 -> AT14, AT15 [AND][sequential: `AT14;AT15`]**
**Justification:** The room call provides the pickup request, and the caller may also provide door-opening information.

**G17 -> G18, G19 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(G18,G19)`]**
**Justification:** If a person can open the door, human assistance is used; otherwise another robot can cooperate.

**G20 -> G21, G22, G23 [AND][fallback: `FALLBACK(FALLBACK(G21,G22),G23)`]**
**Justification:** Dirty-dish retrieval can be unassisted, performed by two robots, or performed by robots with a human. Fallback expresses escalating cooperation requirements.

**G21 -> AT19 [AND]**
**Justification:** The unassisted retrieval goal is operationalized by one robot picking up dirty dishes in the inpatient room.

**G22 -> AT20 [AND]**
**Justification:** The two-robot retrieval goal is operationalized by two robots cooperating in the inpatient room.

**G23 -> AT21 [AND]**
**Justification:** The robot-human retrieval goal is operationalized by one or more robots coordinating with a human in the inpatient room.
