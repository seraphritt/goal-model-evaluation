# Multi-Robot Goal Model: Hospital Food Delivery and Dish Retrieval

## Source-grounded design rationale

The mission requires a hospital multi-robot system that delivers food from the **kitchen** to an **inpatient room**, order by order, in response to kitchen delivery requests. Delivery may be done by placing food on the room table using a manipulation-capable robot, or by having the food fetched from the robot tray with cooperation from a patient, companion, nurse, or another robot. The mission also requires dirty dishes to be retrieved from the room, with possible unassisted, robot-robot, or robot-human cooperation. 

The model follows the provided goal-model definition: goals are hierarchically decomposed into sub-goals and tasks, using AND/OR relations.  Goal types are classified as Perform, Query, or Achieve according to the supplied definitions.  Tasks are treated as concrete assignable robot operations with location and number of robots.

## 6. Notes on uncertainty and fallback choices

The description explicitly says that information about whether the inpatient, companion, or nurse can retrieve food is subject to uncertainty. Therefore, tray retrieval is not modeled as a simple OR choice only; it is modeled as a fallback structure that can adapt when one actor is unavailable or unable.

The fallback order used here is a design choice:

1. For food handoff, table placement is attempted before tray retrieval because it avoids uncertainty about human availability, but it requires a manipulation-capable robot.
2. For tray retrieval, the model tries inpatient, then companion, then nurse, then another robot.
3. For dirty dishes, the model tries unassisted retrieval first, then two-robot cooperation, then robot-human cooperation.

These fallback orders can be changed without changing the mission semantics, as long as the same alternatives remain represented.
