# Mission Analysis: Hospital Meal Delivery & Dish Retrieval Robot System

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**1. Identifying the top-level responsibilities.** The description contains two clearly parallel processes signaled by "As with delivery, the dish retrieve can occur…" — (a) delivering food and (b) retrieving dirty dishes. Since both are independently triggered (kitchen requests vs. room calls) and nothing in the text makes one depend on the other's completion, I model them as an **AND** decomposition under a root goal, executed in **parallel (#)**.

**2. Decomposing the delivery flow.** The text gives an implicit sequence: a request must exist → food must be transported → the room must be accessed → the food must actually reach the inpatient. This gives a **sequential (;)** AND-chain: *identify request → transport → access room → deliver*.

**3. OR vs. FALLBACK.** The description states two delivery mechanisms ("table" *or* "tray") and three retrieval-assistance modes ("unassisted, two robots, or robot+human") and two door-opening modes ("another robot or a human") without ever stating an attempt order (e.g., "try X first, and if it fails do Y"). Per the FALLBACK rule, since no explicit precedence is given, **all of these are OR-decompositions**, not fallbacks, annotated with `#` since no ordering constraint is stated.

**4. Handling uncertainty.** The text explicitly flags that eligibility to retrieve from the tray (inpatient/companion/nurse) is "subject to uncertainty" and derived from inpatient records + companion presence. This is a clear **Query Goal** (information acquisition), distinct from the subsequent **Achieve Goal** of actually coordinating the hand-off (which can fail if nobody eligible is available/able).

**5. Perform vs. Achieve distinction.** Concrete, singular physical actions (placing food on a table, presenting a tray, logging a timestamp, sounding an alert, picking up dishes) are modeled as **Perform Goals** — they succeed simply by being executed. Higher-level objectives that describe a *state to be brought about*, possibly through multiple alternative or uncertain means (accessing a room, completing a delivery, collecting dishes), are modeled as **Achieve Goals** with an explicit target condition.

**6. Door access reused in both branches.** The sentence "Opening the room door can involve cooperation with another robot or a human" is stated generally (not exclusively inside the dish-pickup paragraph), and physically entering the room is a prerequisite for both placing food/presenting a tray *and* collecting dishes. I therefore instantiate an "Access Room" goal in **both** branches, each independently justified by this shared textual statement.

**7. Multi-meal handling.** "Indicate which meal," "track when/where retrieved," and "alert if wrong meal" are all explicitly tied to the *tray-retrieval-by-a-person* path (the word "retrieved" is used, never used for table delivery), so these three goals are nested only under the tray-retrieval branch, in AND with the retrieval-coordination goal itself.

**8. Locations and robot counts.** Only two physical locations are named in the text: **Kitchen** and **Inpatient Room** (the door is treated as part of the room). Robot counts are inferred strictly from cooperation cues: "another robot" / "two robots" → 2; single-agent actions → 1.

## Logical Relationships

- **G1 -> G2, G20 [AND] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Meal delivery and dish retrieval are two independent, complementary responsibilities of the system, each triggered by a different event (kitchen request vs. room call), so both are required (AND) and are not mutually dependent in time (parallel).

- **G2 -> G3, G4, G5, G8 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: A single delivery cannot succeed unless the request is known, then the food is moved, then the room is accessed, then the hand-off occurs — each step is necessary and logically ordered.

- **G5 -> G6, G7 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The text lists two alternative ways to open the door ("another robot or a human") without stating a preferred order, so either suffices (OR), with no prescribed sequencing (parallel/no-order).

- **G8 -> G9, G10 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: "Delivered into a room table… or fetched from the robot's tray" are explicitly alternative mechanisms; fulfilling either is sufficient, and no attempt order is stated.

- **G10 -> G11, G17, G12, (G18, G19) [AND] (sequential, with G18#G19 in parallel)**
  - **Justification**: Tray retrieval requires knowing who can retrieve (G11), telling them which meal (G17), actually coordinating the retrieval (G12), and then — once retrieval has occurred — tracking it and checking for errors, which can reasonably occur together (parallel) since neither depends on the other's outcome.

- **G12 -> G13, G14, G15, G16 [OR] (parallel)**
  - **Justification**: The text explicitly lists four alternative cooperating parties ("the inpatient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot"); any one succeeding fulfills the goal, with no stated precedence among them.

- **G20 -> G21, G22, G25 [AND] (sequential)**
  - **Justification**: Dish retrieval requires first knowing a pickup is needed, then gaining room access, then physically collecting the dishes — a necessary, ordered chain.

- **G22 -> G23, G24 [OR] (parallel)**
