## Mission Design Rationale (summary)

* The mission is primarily an **Achieve mission** because the robots must reach a desired state: all required patients have been checked within the required time constraints.
* Several parts are **Perform goals** because the robot must execute actions (visit rooms, approach patients, announce procedures, sterilize, recharge).
* Some sub-goals are **Query goals** because the robot must obtain information from the environment (patient identity, availability, battery level, risk condition).
* Requirements involving alternatives (e.g., identify patient by asking **or** scanning barcode) are modeled as **OR decompositions**.
* Requirements involving mandatory combined actions (e.g., visit room **and** check battery **and** collect vital signs) are modeled as **AND decompositions**.
* Runtime operators reflect execution order:

  * `;` = mandatory sequence.
  * `#` = activities that may happen in parallel.
  * `FALLBACK(a,b)` = execute `a`, and if it fails execute `b`.

**Justification:**
The mission requires performing monitoring, managing robot resources, respecting high-risk deadlines, and escalating impossible missions.

---

### G2 -> G3, G7 [AND][Sequential]

**Justification:**
The robot must visit and check patients before marking completion and continuing.

---

### G3 -> G4, G5, G6 [AND][Sequential]

**Justification:**
Before collecting vital signs, the robot must verify conditions, identify the correct patient, and perform checking.

**Justification:**
Availability and battery verification are independent checks that must both succeed before entry.
