**Step‑by‑step reasoning**

1. **Read the mission description carefully.**  
   * Two core activities are highlighted: **food delivery** (order‑by‑order) and **dish retrieval** after the meal.  
   * Delivery can be done in two modes –  
     * *Direct table delivery* (robot hands food to the table using a special manipulation skill).  
     * *Tray delivery* (food is left on a tray for a human – patient, companion, or nurse – to fetch).  
   * Tray delivery is contingent on whether a human is able and present. This is a *query* (information is retrieved from the patient record and the presence of a companion).  
   * If the human is not able to fetch, the system must **fallback** to the direct delivery mode.  
   * Dish retrieval can be **unassisted**, involve **two robots**, or involve **robots + human**.  
   * Door opening may also need cooperation with a human or another robot.  
   * All actions are performed by one or more robots, sometimes in cooperation.

2. **Identify the high‑level goal** that encompasses the whole mission.  
   * “Hospital Service Operations” – to deliver food and clean dishes in a hospital.

3. **Decompose the high‑level goal into sub‑goals.**  
   * Food delivery (needs fallback between the two delivery modes).  
   * Dish retrieval (parallel to food delivery).  

4. **Decide on the type of decomposition and execution structure.**  
   * The two main sub‑goals are **AND** – both must be satisfied.  
   * Within food delivery, the robot can choose between *tray* and *table* delivery – an **OR** relation.  
   * Since the robot may first try the tray mode and, if it fails (patient cannot fetch), fall back to table delivery, we model this with a **fallback** operator.  
   * Within each delivery mode, all the low‑level tasks must be executed – an **AND**.  
   * Dish retrieval tasks also form an **AND** chain.  
   * Door opening may require cooperation, so the *number of robots* for that task is expressed as a range [1,2].

5. **Classify each goal.**  
   * **Perform** goals – they describe actions the robot must execute (food delivery, dish retrieval).  
   * **Query** goal – obtaining the Boolean “can patient fetch?” from the record.  
   * **Achieve** goals were not needed because every goal explicitly specifies the desired world state.

6. **Create leaf tasks for each perform goal.**  
   * Tasks are named with the prefix **AT** and numbered in a depth‑first traversal order.  
   * Each task carries *Location* (kitchen, corridor, room door, patient table, etc.) and *Number of Robots* (1, [1,2]).

7. **Justify every choice** – runtime operators, decomposition type, goal type, task attributes – by referencing the mission description.
