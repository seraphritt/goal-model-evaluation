**1.  Mission‑level reasoning**

| # | Observation | Why it matters for the goal model |
|---|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 | Food is delivered *either* onto the table or left on a tray for the patient or an aide to fetch. | This yields a **choice** between two delivery modes → an OR decomposition. |
| 2 | The choice depends on whether the patient can fetch the food – this information is **uncertain** and must be queried. | Introduces a **Query** goal that feeds the delivery‑mode decision. |
| 3 | Door opening may need the patient, a companion, a nurse, or another robot – cooperation is required. | Two alternatives (robot‑only or human‑assisted) → OR decomposition with a fallback. |
| 4 | The robot can carry multiple meals and must identify which meal the patient must take and track its retrieval. | These are **performance** tasks under the tray‑delivery branch. |
| 5 | Dirty dishes must be retrieved; this can be done unassisted, with two robots, or with a human. | Again an OR decomposition between unassisted and assisted options. |
| 6 | All of the above activities belong to a higher‑level service that the robot is supposed to provide to a patient room. | A root **Achieve** goal that is satisfied when food delivery *and* dish retrieval are both completed. |
| 7 | Some steps (opening the door, retrieving dishes) can be performed **in parallel** with other steps (e.g., delivering food). | The root level therefore has a **parallel (#)** execution of its children. |

The depth‑first numbering below follows the tree traversal that respects the parent‑child relationships described above.

## 6.  Final remarks

The model captures all *intentional* objectives (goals) and their operationalization (tasks).  

* **OR/AND choices** are driven by the mission’s uncertainty (patient ability to fetch, human availability, robot capacity).  
* **Fallbacks** are used where a first attempt may fail (robot opening door, robot delivering directly).  
* **Parallel execution** is only used at the highest level (service split), because lower‑level activities are logically sequential or mutually exclusive.  
* **Query goals** provide the necessary information for decision‑making (patient fetchability).  
* **Perform goals** encapsulate concrete actions that the robot can execute autonomously or with assistance.

This structured goal‑task model is ready for implementation in a multi‑robot planning framework that supports hierarchical goal decomposition, fallback strategies, and cooperation with humans.
