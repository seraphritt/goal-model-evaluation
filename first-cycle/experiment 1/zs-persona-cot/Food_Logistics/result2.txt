### Justification of Goal Types  

| **Goal ID** | **Chosen Goal Type** | **Rationale** |
|-------------|----------------------|---------------|
| **G1** | Achieve | The mission’s ultimate objective is a *world‑state change*: the meals must be in the correct rooms and patient records updated. |
| **G2** | Query | It only requires the robot to *obtain information* (next kitchen order) without performing any physical action. |
| **G3** | Perform | This goal requires concrete actions (moving, picking up) whose success depends solely on whether those actions are executed. |
| **G4** | Perform | Delivery entails executing a physical action (placing or enabling retrieval). |
| **G4.1** | Perform | Direct manipulation of food on a table is an action that is either executed or not. |
| **G4.2** | Perform | Coordinating patient retrieval is a series of actions (querying, informing, waiting, tracking) that must be carried out. |
| **G5** | Perform | Dish retrieval involves a sequence of concrete actions (moving, picking up, transporting). |

---

**Notes for Implementation**

* The **runtime annotations** (e.g., `;`, `FALLBACK`) indicate how the robot’s planner should sequence or choose between sub‑tasks.  
* **Task locations** are defined at the hospital level (e.g., kitchen, patient room).  
* The **Number of Robots** column can be expanded to ranges `[1,2]` if cooperative scenarios with a companion or another robot are explicitly required.  
* The **Assist Patient Retrieval** task (`AT16`) can be activated conditionally if the patient is unable to retrieve food independently; this can be modeled as an additional fallback or OR child of `G4.2` in a refined design.  

This goal‑model gives a clear, executable structure for a multi‑robot system tasked with delivering food, handling patient‑specific retrieval, and cleaning up, while respecting uncertainty about patient capabilities and cooperation availability.
